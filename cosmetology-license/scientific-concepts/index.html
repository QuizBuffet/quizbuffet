Sample Questions

A new cosmetology student is studying how illness spreads in the salon environment. Which of the following is the most common way pathogens are transmitted from one client to another?
  • A. Through the air conditioning system alone
  • B. By verbal communication between clients
  • C. Through clean towels stored in a closed cabinet
  • D. Through direct contact with contaminated tools or surfaces

Pathogens are most commonly transmitted in the salon through direct contact with contaminated tools, implements, or surfaces, which is why EPA-registered disinfection of all reusable implements between clients is required.

Before beginning a client's service, a cosmetologist washes their hands thoroughly with soap and water. This practice is an example of which level of infection control?
  • A. Sterilization
  • B. Sanitation
  • C. Disinfection
  • D. Decontamination

Sanitation reduces the number of pathogens on a surface or the hands to a level considered safe; handwashing with soap and water is the foundational sanitation practice in the salon.

During a busy shift, a stylist observes that a client has open lesions on the scalp that appear inflamed and weeping. Understanding pathogen types, which category of microorganism most commonly causes contagious bacterial scalp infections?
  • A. Saprophytes
  • B. Bacteria
  • C. Allergens
  • D. Nonpathogenic organisms

Bacteria are pathogenic microorganisms responsible for contagious scalp infections such as folliculitis and impetigo, which can be spread through contaminated implements or direct contact.

After completing a client's haircut, a cosmetologist picks up the used comb to prepare it for the next client. Which step must occur before disinfection can be effective on this implement?
  • A. Immerse the comb immediately in a disinfectant solution
  • B. Apply a sterilizing agent directly to the comb
  • C. Place the comb in an ultraviolet sanitizer cabinet
  • D. Clean the comb to remove all visible debris and product residue

Cleaning to remove all visible debris is the mandatory first step before disinfection; organic material inhibits disinfectant efficacy and must be eliminated prior to chemical treatment.

A salon owner is selecting a new disinfectant for reusable implements. To meet professional salon standards, an EPA-registered disinfectant must demonstrate which combination of properties?
  • A. Antifungal properties only, as fungi are the primary salon pathogen
  • B. Bactericidal, virucidal, and fungicidal properties
  • C. Bactericidal properties only, registered with the FDA
  • D. Sporicidal properties, registered with the CDC

Salon disinfectants must be EPA-registered and demonstrate bactericidal (kills bacteria), virucidal (kills viruses), and fungicidal (kills fungi) properties to provide the broad-spectrum protection required by most state cosmetology boards.

A client presents with an intensely itchy scalp and upon inspection the cosmetologist notices tiny oval eggs attached to hair shafts near the scalp. This highly contagious condition caused by a parasitic infestation is classified as:
  • A. Pediculosis capitis
  • B. tinea pedis
  • C. Psoriasis
  • D. Seborrheic dermatitis

Pediculosis capitis is an infestation of head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis), a parasitic condition characterized by nits (eggs) attached to hair shafts; it is highly contagious and a contraindication for salon service.

A state board inspector reviews infection control practices at a busy salon. According to infection control principles, which item requires only sanitation rather than disinfection between clients?
  • A. A styling chair that has not come into contact with broken skin or bodily fluids
  • B. A razor blade used during a haircut
  • C. Metal cuticle nippers used during a manicure
  • D. A clipper blade used on a client with scalp abrasions

A styling chair that has not contacted broken skin or bodily fluids is an environmental surface that requires cleaning (sanitation) to remove debris between clients; it does not require the higher disinfection level reserved for skin-contact implements.

A cosmetology instructor asks students to distinguish between contagious and non-contagious diseases for salon safety purposes. Which statement most accurately captures this distinction?
  • A. A contagious disease is always caused by fungi, while a non-contagious disease is always caused by bacteria
  • B. A contagious disease cannot be spread through indirect contact with contaminated implements
  • C. A contagious disease can be transmitted directly or indirectly from one person to another, while a non-contagious disease cannot be transmitted between individuals
  • D. A non-contagious disease always requires an insect vector for transmission

A contagious (communicable) disease spreads directly through person-to-person contact or indirectly through contaminated surfaces and implements; a non-contagious condition such as alopecia areata or eczema cannot be transmitted between individuals.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Nail Structure: Nail plate (visible), matrix (where growth originates), lunula (whitish half-moon), nail bed, eponychium (cuticle skin), hyponychium (under free edge).
  • Hair Structure: Three layers: cuticle (outer protective scales), cortex (where color and curl live), medulla (inner core, often absent in fine hair).
  • Skin Structure: Epidermis (outermost, 5 sublayers), dermis (collagen, elastin, nerves, vessels), subcutis (fatty tissue). Stratum corneum is the outer dead cell layer.
  • Nail Disorders: Onychomycosis (fungal infection), paronychia (bacterial infection around nail), onycholysis (separation from bed), pterygium (overgrown cuticle). Refer infections to a physician.
  • Nail Enhancements: Acrylic (liquid monomer + polymer powder), hard gel (UV/LED-cured methacrylate), soft gel/gel polish (cured with UV/LED), dip powder. Maintain proper apex, structure, and prep.
  • Hair Characteristics: Texture (diameter: fine/medium/coarse), density (hairs/sq inch), porosity (cuticle openness), elasticity (stretch), growth pattern.
  • Hair Growth Cycle: Anagen (growth, 2-6 years), catagen (transition, 1-2 weeks), telogen (resting and shedding, 3-4 months).
  • Hair Color Theory: Levels 1-10 (darkness/lightness), tones (warm/cool/neutral), primary colors (red/yellow/blue). Underlying pigment determines lift.
  • Permanent Waves: Alkaline (cold, ammonium thioglycolate, pH 8.6-9.5, strong curl) vs acid (heat-activated, glyceryl monothioglycolate, pH 4.5-7, gentle curl on sensitive hair).
  • Chemical Relaxers: Sodium hydroxide (lye, strong, no preliminary shampoo, pH 12+) and thio relaxers (no-lye, ammonium thioglycolate). Both break disulfide bonds.
