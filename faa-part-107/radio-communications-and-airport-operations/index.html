Sample Questions

A remote pilot is conducting an sUAS inspection flight near a non-towered airport and wants to monitor for manned aircraft in the traffic pattern. The airport has no control tower but does have a published Common Traffic Advisory Frequency (CTAF). What is the primary purpose of the CTAF at a non-towered airport?

Answer: The CTAF allows pilots to self-announce their positions and intentions so that other aircraft in the area can maintain situational awareness without ATC services

The CTAF provides a common frequency where pilots at non-towered airports self-announce their position, altitude, and intentions (such as downwind, base, or final) so that other aircraft in the area can build situational awareness and sequence themselves safely without a controller.

A remote pilot is conducting an sUAS operation at an airport that has experienced a radio communication failure. The ATC tower begins using a light gun to signal ground and air traffic. A flashing red light gun signal directed at an aircraft on the ground means what?

Answer: Return to starting point on the airport; the aircraft on the ground must return to the ramp or last cleared position

A flashing red light gun signal directed at an aircraft on the ground means 'return to starting point on airport'; the aircraft must taxi back to its starting position and hold.

A remote pilot reviewing airport operations encounters an illustration of runway hold short markings. These markings consist of two solid yellow lines and two dashed yellow lines painted across a taxiway. Which direction relative to these markings requires ATC clearance before proceeding?

Answer: An aircraft facing the solid lines must hold and obtain ATC clearance before crossing; aircraft facing the dashed lines are on the runway and may proceed normally

At runway hold short markings, the solid double yellow lines face the taxiway side; aircraft approaching the runway see the solid lines and must stop and hold until cleared by ATC; aircraft on the runway side see the dashed lines, indicating they may proceed from the runway onto the taxiway.
