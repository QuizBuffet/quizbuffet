3.0 Machine Assembly Tattoo Practice Quiz

12 exam-style questions covering 14% of the Tattoo exam. Instant feedback on every answer, progress tracking, no signup required.

This domain is part of the Tattoo Artist License practice test. Each question is tagged by exam objective and difficulty so you can drill exactly the areas you need.

Sample Questions

Before beginning machine assembly, the candidate must present the packaged tube and needle to the examiner to
  • A. allow the examiner to assemble the machine on behalf of the candidate
  • B. demonstrate that the tube and needle are sterile by showing intact, uncompromised original packaging before opening
  • C. receive the examiner's permission to begin the examination
  • D. have the examiner date-stamp the packaging for regulatory records

Presenting the packaged tube and needle to the examiner before opening demonstrates that these critical skin-penetrating implements arrived at the procedure area in their original, intact sterile packaging, confirming they have not been compromised, opened, or contaminated before use; the examiner can verify the packaging is sealed, undamaged, and within its expiration date; this step is explicitly required by the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination before machine assembly begins.

During the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination machine assessment, after being instructed to power the machine on and off, the candidate must
  • A. touch the powered needle briefly to the model's skin to demonstrate it is functioning correctly
  • B. power the machine on and off without touching the needle to skin at any point
  • C. increase machine voltage to maximum to demonstrate full power range
  • D. disassemble the machine completely after the power demonstration

The machine power demonstration assesses whether the candidate has correctly assembled and connected the machine so it functions; the examiner instructs the candidate to power the machine on and off; the CIB explicitly states 'Do NOT touch the needle to skin' during this demonstration, the task is to confirm machine function, not to begin tattooing.

When assembling the tattoo machine, the needle should be
  • A. opened from its sterile packaging immediately before assembly and handled only with gloved hands, with the needle tip never touching any non-sterile surface
  • B. handled with ungloved hands for better tactile control during assembly
  • C. removed from its packaging well before assembly to allow it to reach room temperature
  • D. soaked in disinfectant for 2 minutes before insertion into the tube

Tattoo needles must be opened from their sterile packaging only when immediately ready for assembly; gloves must be worn during all handling; the needle tip must never contact any non-sterile surface (including the work table, glove exterior, or any other object) that would transfer contamination to the portion that will penetrate the client's skin; maintaining the needle's sterility up to the point of use is a critical safety requirement.

The power supply for a tattoo machine controls
  • A. the speed of pigment dispensed from the ink cap to the needle
  • B. the ambient temperature of the procedure room
  • C. the voltage delivered to the machine, which regulates the motor speed and the needle's reciprocation speed
  • D. the UV sterilization function of the work area lighting

The tattoo machine power supply converts standard AC electricity to regulated DC voltage output; the voltage setting on the power supply determines how much electrical energy reaches the machine's motor (in rotary machines) or electromagnets (in coil machines), which directly controls how fast the motor rotates or the coils cycle, and therefore how many times per second the needle reciprocates in and out of the skin; this voltage adjustment allows the artist to control needle speed for different techniques (lining vs. shading).

After machine assembly is completed and the examiner has evaluated the setup, any additional packaging waste or supply wrappers must be
  • A. organized on the work surface for reference during the procedure
  • B. discarded in the appropriate labeled container immediately to maintain a clean, organized work area
  • C. kept on the supply tray until the end of the full examination
  • D. returned to the supply container along with any unused portions of supplies

All packaging waste generated during machine assembly, opened sterile wrappers, any protective coverings removed from supplies, must be immediately discarded in the appropriate labeled container ('Trash' for soft waste, 'Sharps' for any sharps packaging that may have needle residue) to maintain an organized, uncontaminated work area; prompt waste disposal is evaluated throughout the NIC examination.

Rubber bands are used on a tattoo machine during assembly to
  • A. provide insulation between the machine frame and the power supply cord
  • B. secure the needle bar to the armature bar and provide tension that controls needle return speed and dampens vibration
  • C. attach the clip cord to the machine frame for a secure electrical connection
  • D. seal the tube junction to prevent ink from leaking during the procedure

In traditional coil tattoo machines, rubber bands are looped around the armature bar and the machine frame (or tube) to create tension that helps return the needle bar to its resting position after each stroke and dampens the vibration of the machine during operation; this tension affects the machine's overall feel, the needle's return speed, and the consistency of the tattooing stroke; the rubber band is a functional mechanical component of the traditional coil machine's operation.

A foot switch used during the tattooing examination is designated as a 'contaminated' item because
  • A. foot switches are always made of non-disinfectable porous materials
  • B. the floor where the foot switch rests is considered contaminated, and the foot switch contacts both the floor and the procedure environment through its power connection
  • C. the artist's foot contacts the switch while their body may carry blood-borne contamination from the procedure, and the switch contacts the floor, making it a contaminated item that cannot be placed with clean supplies
  • D. foot switches produce electromagnetic interference that contaminates the electrical signal

Foot switches can be made from various materials and can typically be wiped with EPA-registered disinfectant; the contamination designation is based on the infection control pathway, not on material porosity.

The clip cord connecting the tattoo machine to the power supply must be covered with a barrier during the procedure because
  • A. the clip cord barrier improves the electrical efficiency of the connection
  • B. it prevents the cord from tangling during the procedure
  • C. clip cords are reusable items that cannot be disinfected in a wet disinfectant jar, so the barrier protects them from blood and body fluid contamination during the procedure and is removed and replaced between clients
  • D. the plastic barrier prevents electrical shock if the cord contacts the client's skin

Barrier materials on cords are not electrical components and do not affect electrical efficiency or signal quality; their function is infection control.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Foot Switch: Pedal-operated power control for the tattoo machine; classified as a contaminated item during the NIC practical exam and must be stored separately from clean supplies, then disinfected for transport between clients.
  • Machine Assembly: Process of connecting the tattoo machine frame, drive bar, needle, tube, clip cord (or wireless power), and rubber bands into a ready-to-operate unit; tube and needle must be presented in sealed sterile packaging before assembly.
  • Power Supply: Device that regulates voltage delivered to the tattoo machine; paired with a clip cord (connects machine to power supply) and optionally a foot switch (pedal control considered contaminated during the exam).
  • Work Area Setup: First domain of the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination: candidate prepares themselves, disinfects all work surfaces, sets up supplies and equipment, designates the universal supply area, and disposes of contaminated supplies, all within 20 minutes.
  • Infection Control: Practices that prevent the transmission of pathogens, including hand hygiene, surface disinfection, barrier use, and single-use supplies, required throughout the tattooing process.
  • Tattoo Machine: Electromechanical device that drives a needle bar up and down to deposit pigment into the dermis; types include coil machines (electromagnetic) and rotary machines (motor-driven).
  • Tube: Sterile, single-use grip component that houses the needle bar and channels pigment delivery; must be presented in sealed sterile packaging to the examiner before machine assembly.
  • Autoclave: Device that uses saturated steam under pressure (typically 121 °C at 15 psi) to sterilize instruments and packaged supplies such as tubes and needles.
  • Barriers: Plastic wrap or other single-use coverings applied to machine frames, power supply buttons, clip cords, and other surfaces that cannot be sterilized, preventing cross-contamination during the procedure.
  • Single-Use Supplies: Items designed for one client only: including needles, tubes, ink caps, gloves, and razors, that must be discarded after each client to prevent cross-contamination.
