The Information Systems and Controls (ISC) section is one of three Discipline sections of the Uniform CPA Examination administered by the AICPA. Candidates pass three Core sections (AUD, FAR, REG) plus one Discipline of their choice — ISC is most often selected by candidates planning a career in IT audit, cybersecurity, or System and Organization Controls (SOC) attestation. The 4-hour exam consists of 82 multiple-choice questions and 6 task-based simulations, weighted 60% MCQ / 40% TBS in scoring (the only section without 50/50 split). Content covers three areas: Information Systems and Data Management (35–45%) — IT infrastructure components, cloud computing models (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS), enterprise and accounting information systems, system availability, business continuity and disaster recovery, IT change management, data management, relational databases, SQL queries, data warehouses and lakes, and business process modeling; Security, Confidentiality and Privacy (35–45%) — HIPAA Security and Privacy Rules, GDPR Articles 4–34, PCI DSS, NIST CSF and Privacy Framework, NIST SP 800-53, CIS Controls v8.1, COBIT 2019, threat types and cyber-attack stages, identification/authentication/authorization controls, encryption, Data Loss Prevention, security testing, and incident response; and Considerations for SOC Engagements (15–25%) — purpose and intended users of SOC 1®, SOC 2®, SOC 3®, and SOC for Cybersecurity reports, Trust Services Criteria, system description criteria, complementary user entity controls (CUECs) and complementary subservice organization controls (CSOCs), report opinions and modifications. References include the AICPA Trust Services Criteria, NIST frameworks, HIPAA 45 CFR Part 164, GDPR, PCI DSS, COSO ERM, and COBIT 2019.

Why ISC matters in 2026

CPA — Information Systems and Controls (ISC) is one of the most recognized credentials issued by AICPA, and 2026 hiring data continues to show ISC on job postings as either required or strongly preferred for the roles it targets. The certification validates that you can apply real working knowledge, not just recall facts, across 3 distinct exam domains, with the largest weight on Information Systems and Data Management. For candidates competing in the AICPA ecosystem, ISC is a clear signal to a hiring manager that you have invested in measurable, third-party-verified competence.

Who should take ISC

ISC is most useful for professionals working in or moving toward the AICPA ecosystem. In short: Discipline section of the Uniform CPA Examination — IT audit, security, and SOC engagements. If you are early in your career, ISC is one of the fastest credentials to add to a résumé that recruiters actively screen for. If you are mid-career, it formalizes the skills you already use day-to-day and unlocks roles that gate on it. If you are switching tracks, it gives you a structured curriculum that tells you exactly what to study, in what order, weighted by what the real exam tests.

Exam structure and difficulty

The CPA — Information Systems and Controls (ISC) exam is organized into 3 domains, each weighted by the official AICPA exam guide. The heaviest weighting is Information Systems and Data Management at 37% of the exam, so that is where you should spend the most preparation time. The lightest is Considerations for System and Organization Controls (SOC) Engagements at 26%, meaning you can dedicate roughly proportional review time without over-investing.

Common pitfalls

The three traps that kill ISC candidates: (1) over-memorizing acronyms instead of practicing the application of concepts in scenarios, when the exam is scenario-driven, not a vocab quiz. (2) Skipping the heaviest-weighted domain because it feels less interesting, which can fail the whole exam by neglecting Information Systems and Data Management. (3) Not timing practice sessions, when the exam has a real clock and pacing is its own skill. Build timing into your last two weeks of prep.
