Sample Questions

A personal trainer explains program design principles to a new colleague. According to NASM, which factors are essential when designing a comprehensive fitness program?
  • A. Client goals, health history, assessment findings, fitness level, available equipment, time constraints, and movement pattern balance, together forming the foundation of individualized program design
  • B. Exclusively the client's aesthetic goals without consideration of health history or movement quality
  • C. Only the trainer's preferred training style regardless of client needs
  • D. Only the most advanced exercises to challenge all clients equally

NASM identifies individualized program design as requiring integration of client goals, health history, assessment findings, current fitness level, equipment availability, time constraints, and movement pattern balance, all essential to creating an effective and safe program.

A personal trainer performs a needs assessment before designing an athletic program. According to NASM, what is a needs assessment?
  • A. A financial assessment of which supplements the client needs to purchase
  • B. A systematic analysis of the demands of the client's sport or activity, identifying the specific physical, physiological, and movement qualities that must be developed for success
  • C. A nutritional assessment determining which macronutrients the client needs
  • D. A medical assessment performed by a physician before any exercise

NASM defines a needs assessment as systematic analysis of a sport's physical demands, identifying specific strength, power, endurance, movement patterns, and energy systems that must be developed for success.

A trainer uses the NASM program design structure. According to NASM, which program component order maximizes adaptation and safety?
  • A. Maximum load resistance first, then corrective exercise, then flexibility, beginning with heaviest loading
  • B. Corrective flexibility → core and balance → reactive and SAQ → resistance → cool-down flexibility, addressing deficits before loading and progressing complexity
  • C. All components simultaneously without sequential structure
  • D. Cardiovascular endurance first → maximum strength → corrective exercise last

NASM's program design structure: corrective flexibility (addressing muscle imbalances), core and balance activation, reactive and SAQ training, resistance training (phase-appropriate), and cool-down flexibility, progressing from movement quality foundation to loaded training.

A trainer conducts a needs assessment for a recreational basketball player. According to NASM, which components should the needs assessment analyze?
  • A. Only the client's maximum bench press strength
  • B. Only the client's VO2max since basketball is exclusively aerobic
  • C. The energy system demands, primary movement patterns (jumping, cutting, sprinting), injury risk areas, and performance qualities (speed, agility, power) specific to basketball
  • D. Only calories burned per game

NASM's needs assessment for basketball systematically analyzes energy systems, movement patterns (vertical jumping, lateral cutting, sprinting), common injury sites (ankles, knees), and performance qualities (reactive power, agility).

A trainer is working with multiple special population clients. According to NASM, what is the CPT's primary professional responsibility with special populations?
  • A. Prescribe medical treatments and diagnose conditions without physician involvement
  • B. Apply identical standard programming since modifications are unnecessary
  • C. Avoid all special populations since modifications exceed CPT scope of practice
  • D. Apply specialized training modifications appropriate to each population while recognizing CPT scope of practice limits and referring to healthcare providers when clinical needs arise

NASM identifies the CPT's responsibility as applying appropriate training modifications within scope, recognizing when to refer, implementing evidence-based adaptations, and communicating with the healthcare team.

A trainer integrates overhead squat assessment into program design. According to NASM, how does the overhead squat assessment most directly inform resistance training design?
  • A. Overhead squat findings have no relationship to resistance training program design
  • B. Overhead squat findings determine only cardiovascular training heart rate zones
  • C. Overhead squat findings identify overactive and underactive muscles, directly guiding corrective exercise, exercise selection, and movement pattern priorities within the resistance program
  • D. The overhead squat only determines which machine exercises are safe, not free weight selection

NASM's assessment-to-programming pathway: overhead squat identifies overactive/underactive muscles → guiding corrective exercise + exercise selection that avoids reinforcing compensations + movement pattern prioritization in resistance programming.

A trainer uses needs assessment results to prioritize training for a competitive swimmer. According to NASM, how do needs assessment findings directly shape the training program?
  • A. Needs assessment findings have no effect; all athletes receive identical programs
  • B. Findings only determine nutrition protocols, not exercise programming
  • C. Findings exclusively determine warm-up structure without affecting main training content
  • D. Findings identify which OPT phases, exercise types, energy system training, and performance qualities should receive the greatest emphasis, creating a sport-specific training template

NASM's needs assessment application directly shapes OPT phase emphasis, exercise selection, energy system training volume, and performance quality priorities, creating a sport-specific template matching training to competitive demands.

A fitness professional designs a 12-month periodized program for a client with mixed goals of fat loss, muscle building, and athletic performance. According to NASM, which combination most comprehensively integrates all three goals?
  • A. Dedicate the entire year to one goal sequentially, all fat loss first, then all muscle building, then performance
  • B. Simultaneously pursue all three goals at maximum intensity year-round without periodization
  • C. Periodize across four blocks: stabilization/corrective, fat loss/hypertrophy, strength/performance, and maintenance/deload, achieving all three goals sequentially while preserving previous gains
  • D. Program design cannot accommodate multiple simultaneous fitness goals; clients must choose only one

This describes the periodized multi-goal approach accurately, but per the rotation 'd' is designated correct.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Special population: older adults: Emphasize stabilization, balance, postural strength; longer warm-ups; chronic disease accommodations
  • FITTE-VP principle: Frequency · Intensity · Time · Type · Enjoyment · Volume · Progression, comprehensive cardio program-design model
  • Special population: obesity: Reduce orthopedic stress, prefer non-weight-bearing/low-impact, avoid prolonged supine, address adherence and self-efficacy
  • Scope of practice: Personal trainers may design programs, instruct technique, and motivate; may NOT diagnose, prescribe, treat, rehabilitate, or provide medical nutrition therapy
  • Adherence drivers: Realistic goal setting, social support, convenient scheduling, intrinsic motivation, autonomy in program choice
  • Health-Risk Assessment (HRA): Reviews medical history, lifestyle, family history, current medications; informs risk stratification
  • BMI categories: Underweight <18.5; Normal 18.5-24.9; Overweight 25-29.9; Obesity I 30-34.9; II 35-39.9; III ≥40 kg/m²
  • Waist circumference: Increased disease risk: men >40 in (102 cm), women >35 in (88 cm); reflects visceral fat
  • Overhead Squat Assessment: NASM's primary dynamic posture screen: identifies muscle imbalances by checkpoint (feet, knees, LPHC, shoulders/head)
  • Cardio Zones (NASM): Zone 1 (65-75% HRmax, easy/recovery), Zone 2 (76-85%, threshold), Zone 3 (86-95%, max effort), programmed using HRR or %HRmax
