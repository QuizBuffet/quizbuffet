1.0 Ethics, Professional Responsibilities and General Principles — AUD Practice Quiz

Sample Questions

IRC §6694(b) imposes a penalty on a tax return preparer for willful or reckless conduct. What conduct triggers this penalty, and what is the penalty amount?

Answer: Willful attempt to understate tax liability; reckless or intentional disregard of rules or regulations; penalty = the greater of $5,000 or 75% of the income derived from the return

§6694(b) applies when a preparer: (1) willfully attempts in any manner to understate the tax liability on a return; or (2) recklessly or intentionally disregards rules or regulations in preparing a return. The penalty is the greater of $5,000 or 75% of the income derived (or to be derived) by the preparer from the return. This is more severe than §6694(a) and reflects deliberate or grossly negligent misconduct — a fundamentally different level of culpability from the unreasonable position standard.

Under IRC §6695, which of the following is a penalty imposed on a tax return preparer?

Answer: Failure to include the preparer's Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN) on the return — $50 per failure, up to a maximum of $27,000 per year

IRC §6695(b) imposes a $50 penalty (up to $27,000 per calendar year) on a preparer who fails to include a PTIN on a return or claim for refund. Other §6695 penalties include: §6695(a) failure to furnish a copy of the return to the taxpayer ($50/failure, up to $27,000); §6695(c) failure to retain a copy or list of returns prepared ($50/failure); §6695(d) failure to file correct information returns ($50/failure); §6695(e) negotiating a taxpayer's refund check ($500/check); and §6695(f) aiding and abetting understatement of another's tax liability.

A CPA prepares a tax return and takes an undisclosed position that lacks substantial authority. The CPA earned $2,000 in fees for preparing the return. The understatement is $10,000. The IRS audits the return and determines the position was unreasonable. What is the §6694(a) penalty?

Answer: $1,000 — the greater of $1,000 or 50% of the income derived from the return; 50% of $2,000 = $1,000, which equals the $1,000 minimum

§6694(a) penalty = the greater of: (1) $1,000; or (2) 50% of the income derived (or to be derived) by the preparer from the return or claim for refund with respect to which the penalty is imposed. Income derived = $2,000 preparation fee. 50% × $2,000 = $1,000. Since $1,000 = $1,000, the penalty is $1,000. If the preparation fee had been $500, 50% × $500 = $250 < $1,000 minimum, so the penalty would be $1,000. If the fee had been $3,000, 50% × $3,000 = $1,500 > $1,000, so the penalty would be $1,500.
