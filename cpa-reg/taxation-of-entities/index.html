Sample Questions

On Schedule M-1 of Form 1120, a corporation reconciles book net income to taxable income. Which item is a PERMANENT difference that increases taxable income above book income?

Answer: Federal income taxes accrued and deducted for book purposes — federal income taxes are NOT deductible for federal income tax purposes, creating a permanent difference

Federal income tax expense is accrued and deducted for GAAP book purposes (as an income statement expense) but is NEVER deductible for federal income tax purposes. This is a classic PERMANENT difference: Book deducts federal tax → Book income is lower → Taxable income must be adjusted UPWARD on Schedule M-1 (add back federal income tax expense). Other permanent differences: 50% of meals and entertainment (not currently deductible for tax post-TCJA); life insurance premiums on key employees where company is beneficiary; penalties and fines; tax-exempt interest income (reduces taxable income but stays in book income — opposite direction).

A domestic C corporation owns 25% of the stock of another domestic corporation and receives $100,000 of dividends. What is the dividends received deduction (DRD) available under §243?

Answer: $65,000 (65% DRD) — the 65% rate applies when ownership is at least 20% but less than 80%

§243 Dividends Received Deduction (DRD) ownership-based rates: Ownership < 20%: 50% DRD (most common rate for minority interests). Ownership ≥ 20% but < 80%: 65% DRD. Ownership ≥ 80% (affiliated group): 100% DRD. At 25% ownership (≥ 20% but < 80%): DRD = 65% × $100,000 = $65,000. The DRD prevents triple taxation of corporate earnings distributed as dividends — the paying corporation already paid corporate tax on the earnings; without the DRD, the recipient corporation would also pay tax; and ultimately the individual shareholders would pay dividend tax. The DRD reduces (but doesn't fully eliminate) this second level of corporate tax. Note: the DRD is also limited to the corporation's taxable income (after other deductions) to prevent it from creating an NOL, except when the corporation has an overall loss.

Which event would MOST likely terminate an existing S corporation election?

Answer: The corporation issues a second class of stock (e.g., preferred stock with preferential distribution rights) to attract an investor

S corporations may only have ONE CLASS OF STOCK (§1361(b)(1)(D)). Issuing preferred stock with preferential distribution rights or liquidation preferences creates a second class of stock — this immediately and automatically terminates the S election, converting the corporation to a C corporation. The distinction: different voting rights on shares of the same economic class (voting vs. non-voting common) are PERMITTED; different economic rights (preferred liquidation proceeds, different dividend rates) are NOT permitted.
