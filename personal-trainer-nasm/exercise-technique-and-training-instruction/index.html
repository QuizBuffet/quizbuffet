5.0 Exercise Technique and Training Instruction — NASM-CPT Practice Quiz

128 exam-style questions covering 14% of the NASM-CPT exam. Instant feedback on every answer, progress tracking, no signup required.

This domain is part of the Certified Personal Trainer (NASM-CPT) practice test. Each question is tagged by exam objective and difficulty so you can drill exactly the areas you need.

Sample Questions

A personal trainer introduces a new client to the NASM training philosophy. According to NASM, what does the OPT model stand for and what is its primary purpose?

Answer: Optimum Performance Training — a systematic, evidence-based framework for progressing clients through phases of training to develop all biomotor abilities safely and effectively

NASM defines the OPT (Optimum Performance Training) model as a systematic, evidence-based framework that progressively develops all biomotor abilities — stabilization, strength, and power — through structured training phases.

A personal trainer describes Phase 1 of the OPT model to a beginning client. According to NASM, what are the primary training goals of Phase 1 stabilization endurance?

Answer: Developing neuromuscular efficiency, joint stability, and postural endurance through proprioceptive-rich exercise at relatively low loads

NASM identifies Phase 1 stabilization endurance as developing neuromuscular efficiency, joint stability, and postural endurance through proprioceptively challenging exercises at relatively low loads (12–20 repetitions).

A personal trainer explains Phase 2 of the OPT model to an intermediate client. According to NASM, what is Phase 2 strength endurance and what does it primarily develop?

Answer: Phase 2 strength endurance develops both strength and endurance simultaneously through superset pairing of strength and stabilization exercises

NASM defines Phase 2 strength endurance as simultaneously developing strength and muscular endurance through superset pairing — combining a strength exercise with a stabilization exercise for the same body part to develop both qualities simultaneously.
