A Barber License is required in every U.S. state to perform haircutting, shaving, beard design, chemical hair services, and basic skin and scalp care for compensation. Most states use the NIC (National-Interstate Council of State Boards of Cosmetology) National Barber Theory Examination, a 60-item, 90-minute written exam, paired with a hands-on practical, both typically administered by PSI. The theory blueprint covers four domains: scientific concepts (infection control, anatomy and physiology, skin and hair disorders, product chemistry), implements and equipment (razors, shears, clippers, sanitation), hair care services (consultation, draping, shampoo, cutting, styling, chemical services), and facial hair and skin care services (shaving, facial hair design, facials, electrotherapy).

Barber questions, answered

What is on the barber state board written exam?

Most states use the NIC National Barber Theory exam or a state version of it. It covers scientific concepts (infection control, anatomy, chemistry), hair care services, facial hair and skin care services, and implements and equipment. A hands-on practical exam is also required in most states.

How many questions are on the barber theory exam?

The NIC barber theory exam has 60 items, of which 50 are scored, with 90 minutes to complete it. State versions vary slightly.

What is the passing score for the barber exam?

Your state board sets it. Most states require 70 to 75 percent on the written exam, and the practical is scored separately.

How many training hours do I need for a barber license?

It varies by state, most commonly 1,000 to 1,500 hours of barber school or an approved apprenticeship. Check your state board for its exact requirement and any reciprocity rules.

Can I take the barber exam online?

The written exam is usually taken at a PSI or Prometric testing center, and some states offer remote proctoring. The practical must be done in person on a live model or mannequin.

What happens if I fail the barber exam?

You can retake it after paying the exam fee again. Most states let you retake only the section you failed with no long waiting period, but some limit attempts before requiring more training.
