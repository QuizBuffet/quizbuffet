Sample Questions

A driver is leaving a stop sign in a fully loaded tractor-trailer on a flat road. According to the CDL manual, what is the correct way to apply power for a smooth, controlled start?
  • A. Press the accelerator firmly to load the drivetrain quickly
  • B. Pop the clutch to engage maximum torque immediately
  • C. Apply power smoothly so the vehicle moves forward without jerking
  • D. Slip the clutch heavily until the engine reaches governed rpm

The CDL manual instructs drivers to accelerate smoothly so the vehicle moves forward without jerking, protecting cargo, drivetrain, and traction.

The CDL manual gives a specific minimum time interval that drivers should look ahead while driving. About how far ahead, in seconds, should a CDL driver scan?
  • A. At least 12 to 15 seconds ahead
  • B. At least 3 to 5 seconds ahead
  • C. At least 30 to 40 seconds ahead
  • D. Only as far as the headlights illuminate, regardless of conditions

The manual states a driver should look 12 to 15 seconds ahead, which is roughly a quarter mile at highway speed and one block in city driving.

The CDL manual emphasizes a specific hand position on the steering wheel during normal driving and emergencies. Which grip does it recommend at all times for proper vehicle control?
  • A. Both hands firmly on the wheel, on opposite sides, at all times
  • B. One hand at the bottom of the wheel for relaxed cruising
  • C. Both hands palms-down on top of the wheel
  • D. One hand on the wheel, one on the gear shift, ready to downshift

The manual instructs drivers to keep both hands firmly on the wheel on opposite sides so they can react to a tire failure or emergency steering input without the wheel being twisted out of their hands.

A driver approaches a red light at moderate speed in a heavy combination vehicle. What is the manual's preferred braking technique to bring the vehicle to a smooth, safe stop?
  • A. Hold the brake pedal at maximum pressure until the vehicle stops
  • B. Apply steady, controlled pressure on the brake pedal so the vehicle stops smoothly
  • C. Pump the brakes rapidly all the way to the stop
  • D. Coast in neutral and rely entirely on the parking brake

Steady, controlled brake-pedal pressure delivers a smooth stop, keeps cargo secure, and keeps wheels rolling.

Under 40 mph and on dry pavement, the CDL manual gives a per-length rule of thumb for safe following distance behind another vehicle. What is that rule?
  • A. One second of following distance for every 5 feet of vehicle length
  • B. One second of following distance for every 10 feet of vehicle length
  • C. Two seconds of following distance regardless of vehicle length
  • D. Three seconds of following distance for any speed under 50 mph

The CDL manual specifies at least one second of following distance for every 10 feet of vehicle length at speeds below 40 mph.

A driver is operating a 60-foot tractor-trailer at 55 mph on dry pavement. Using the CDL manual's per-length following-distance rule with the high-speed adjustment, what minimum following distance applies?
  • A. 4 seconds total
  • B. 5 seconds total
  • C. 7 seconds total
  • D. 10 seconds total

At 60 feet of length, the under-40 rule yields 6 seconds, plus 1 additional second for travel over 40 mph, equaling 7 seconds total.

In addition to seeing the road, headlights help others see the CMV. Beyond night driving, in which condition does the CDL manual recommend turning on headlights to improve being seen?
  • A. Only between dusk and dawn, never during daylight hours
  • B. In rain, fog, snow, and any other low-visibility condition during the day
  • C. On city streets only, where pedestrians are present
  • D. Only when the windshield wipers fail to clear the glass

The manual instructs drivers to turn on headlights in any low-visibility condition such as rain, fog, or snow so the vehicle is more visible to others.

A driver must back a tractor-trailer into a tight loading bay. According to the CDL manual, which combination of techniques offers the safest backing operation?
  • A. Back at moderate highway-style speed to keep momentum through the dock
  • B. Steer with one hand and look only through the right mirror
  • C. Back blind and rely on hazard flashers to warn bystanders
  • D. Back slowly, use both mirrors, and use a helper to spot blind areas whenever possible

The manual prescribes backing slowly, using both mirrors, and using a helper when one is available because this combination minimizes blind-spot risk.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Black ice: Thin, transparent ice on the roadway; suspect on bridges, shaded areas, and when temps drop near freezing, reduce speed and avoid sudden steering or braking
  • Skid (front-wheel/steering): Caused by too much speed in a turn, too much braking, worn front tires, or load too far back; recovery: let the vehicle slow down
  • Warning devices: Three reflective triangles within 10 minutes of stopping: 10 ft behind, 100 ft behind, 100 ft ahead (or 100/200 on undivided highway)
  • Serious traffic violations: Excessive speeding (15+ mph over), reckless driving, improper lane change, following too close, fatal traffic offense, no CDL/improper class, texting while driving, 60-day suspension second offense within 3 years, 120-day third
  • Following distance (CMV): At least 1 second per 10 feet of vehicle length at speeds under 40 mph; add 1 second above 40 mph
  • Total stopping distance: Perception distance + reaction distance + brake lag distance + braking distance, at 55 mph an empty truck needs ~300 feet (longer than a football field)
  • Speed and stopping distance: When speed doubles, stopping distance increases roughly fourfold
  • Empty truck stopping distance: An empty truck takes longer to stop than a fully loaded one because there is less weight to push the tires onto the road for traction
  • Inside-cab check: Mirrors, windshield, gauges and warning lights, parking brake, steering play (≤10 degrees), horn, seatbelt, defroster, emergency equipment
  • Emergency equipment: Fire extinguisher, three reflective triangles, spare electrical fuses (unless circuit breakers used)
