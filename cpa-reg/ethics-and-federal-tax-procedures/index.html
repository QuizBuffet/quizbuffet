1.0 Ethics, Professional Responsibilities and Federal Tax Procedures REG Practice Quiz

Sample Questions

Under Treasury Circular 230, which individuals are generally authorized to represent taxpayers before all levels of the Internal Revenue Service, including appeals and collections?

Answer: Attorneys, certified public accountants (CPAs), and enrolled agents — all three hold unlimited practice rights before the IRS

Treasury Circular 230 (31 CFR Part 10) governs practice before the Internal Revenue Service. Three categories of professionals have UNLIMITED practice rights — they may represent clients before any office of the IRS, including audits, Appeals, Collections, and the Taxpayer Advocate Service: (1) Attorneys: admitted to practice law in any US state; (2) Certified Public Accountants (CPAs): licensed by a state board of accountancy; (3) Enrolled Agents (EAs): federally authorized tax practitioners who have either passed a comprehensive three-part IRS examination or have qualifying IRS experience. Additional categories with limited rights: enrolled actuaries, enrolled retirement plan agents, and Annual Filing Season Program completers.

In the hierarchy of federal tax authority, which source ranks HIGHEST and is therefore binding on both the IRS and taxpayers?

Answer: The Internal Revenue Code (IRC), as enacted by Congress under its constitutional taxing authority — the IRC is the primary statutory source of federal tax law

The complete hierarchy of federal tax authority (highest to lowest): (1) US Constitution — supreme law, grants Congress taxing authority; (2) Internal Revenue Code (IRC) — enacted by Congress pursuant to Constitutional authority; primary statutory source; (3) Treasury Regulations — Treasury/IRS interpretation of IRC; generally binding; (4) Revenue Rulings — IRS official positions on specific fact patterns; (5) Revenue Procedures — IRS procedural guidance; (6) Private Letter Rulings — binding only on the requesting taxpayer; (7) IRS Publications — informational only; not binding. The IRC ranks at the top of the administrative sources because it is an Act of Congress — only a court ruling or Congressional amendment can override it.

Under IRC §7525, what is the scope of the confidentiality privilege for federally authorized tax practitioners (such as enrolled agents and CPAs)?

Answer: The §7525 privilege covers confidential communications between a federally authorized tax practitioner and a client with respect to tax advice, but it does NOT extend to criminal tax matters, does NOT apply in state court proceedings, and may be waived by the client

IRC §7525 (enacted 1998) extended a limited form of privilege to federally authorized tax practitioners (enrolled agents, CPAs, and others authorized to practice before the IRS). Key characteristics: (1) Scope: applies to confidential communications between a federally authorized tax practitioner and a client concerning tax advice; (2) Limitations: does NOT apply to any criminal matter; does NOT apply in state court proceedings (only in federal proceedings before the IRS and federal courts); CANNOT be used to conceal documents that would otherwise be discoverable; (3) Waiver: the privilege belongs to the client and can be waived by the client; (4) Comparison to attorney-client: the attorney-client privilege is broader — it extends to criminal matters, all courts, and has deeper historical and legal roots; (5) Work-product doctrine: both attorneys and other practitioners may also claim work-product protection for materials prepared in anticipation of litigation.
