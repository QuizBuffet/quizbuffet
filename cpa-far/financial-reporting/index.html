Sample Questions

The fundamental accounting equation underlying the balance sheet states that

Answer: Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity

The balance sheet equation (Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity) reflects that every asset owned by the entity is financed either through obligations to creditors (liabilities) or through the owners' investment and retained earnings (stockholders' equity). This equation must remain in balance at all times and is the foundation of double-entry bookkeeping.

Under US GAAP, an asset is classified as current on the balance sheet when

Answer: It is expected to be converted to cash, sold, or consumed within one year or within the entity's operating cycle, whichever is longer

ASC 210-10-45 defines current assets as cash and other assets expected to be converted to cash, sold, or consumed within one year or within the normal operating cycle of the business, whichever is longer. The operating cycle is the time between cash outlays for inventory and the collection of cash from resulting sales.

At December 31, a company's long-term debt agreement contains a covenant requiring the company to maintain a debt-to-equity ratio below 2.0. At year-end the ratio is 2.4, and the lender has not granted a waiver. How should this debt be classified on the December 31 balance sheet?

Answer: As a current liability — the covenant violation gives the lender the right to call the debt immediately, making it due on demand and therefore current

ASC 470-10-45 requires that when a debt covenant is violated and the lender has the right to demand immediate payment — and has not waived that right as of the balance sheet date — the debt is reclassified as current. The right of the creditor to accelerate the debt makes it effectively due on demand, which requires current presentation regardless of the original stated maturity.
