Forklift Operator Certification is mandated by 29 CFR 1910.178 for any worker operating a powered industrial truck on the job. Training covers truck types and capacities, the physics of stability (fulcrum, center of gravity, stability triangle, load center), pre-operation inspection, safe load handling, operating around pedestrians, lifting personnel on work platforms, and the employer’s duty to evaluate and certify each operator at least every three years.

OSHA 1910.178 questions, answered

Is the OSHA forklift test free?

QuizBuffet’s forklift practice test is free, 300+ questions with instant feedback, no signup. The official employer-administered evaluation itself is not run by OSHA directly and is typically arranged (and paid for) through your employer or a training provider.

Can you get forklift certified online?

The written knowledge portion can be completed online, but 29 CFR 1910.178(l) requires a hands-on performance evaluation of your actual operating skills, which must be done in person on the equipment you will use.

How many questions are on the forklift test?

It varies by employer and training provider since OSHA does not publish one standard test; most written knowledge tests run 25 to 40 questions.

What does 29 CFR 1910.178 require?

29 CFR 1910.178 is OSHA’s Powered Industrial Trucks standard. It requires formal training on truck types and operation, a hands-on evaluation of each operator, refresher training after certain incidents or unsafe operation, and re-evaluation of every operator at least once every three years.

How often do you need forklift recertification?

At least every 3 years under 1910.178, though a near-miss, accident, unsafe operation, or a switch to a different truck type can trigger earlier refresher training.

What is a passing score for the forklift test?

OSHA does not set one fixed passing score since the written test and evaluation criteria are set by the employer or training provider; most programs require correctly demonstrating every required skill on the hands-on evaluation, not just a percentage on the written portion.
