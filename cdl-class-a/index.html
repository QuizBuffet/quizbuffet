CDL Class A licensing is governed by 49 CFR Part 383 and administered by state DMVs under the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA). The knowledge component covers driver qualifications and disqualifications, hours of service, safe driving and hazard recognition, cargo securement, the air brake system and 7-step inspection, combination vehicles and coupling/uncoupling, the pre-trip vehicle inspection, and basic vehicle control maneuvers. A Class A CDL is required to operate any combination vehicle with a gross combination weight rating (GCWR) over 26,001 pounds when the towed unit exceeds 10,000 pounds.

The CDL-A Guide

Everything you need to know before sitting for CDL Class A

Why CDL-A matters in 2026

CDL Class A is one of the most recognized credentials issued by FMCSA / State DMV, and 2026 hiring data continues to show CDL-A on job postings as either required or strongly preferred for the roles it targets. The certification validates that you can apply real working knowledge, not just recall facts, across 7 distinct exam domains, with the largest weight on Driving Safely and On-Road Operation. For candidates competing in the FMCSA / State DMV ecosystem, CDL-A is a clear signal to a hiring manager that you have invested in measurable, third-party-verified competence.

Who should take CDL-A

CDL-A is most useful for professionals working in or moving toward the FMCSA / State DMV ecosystem. In short: Federal license required to drive tractor-trailers and large combination vehicles. If you are early in your career, CDL-A is one of the fastest credentials to add to a résumé that recruiters actively screen for. If you are mid-career, it formalizes the skills you already use day-to-day and unlocks roles that gate on it. If you are switching tracks, it gives you a structured curriculum that tells you exactly what to study, in what order, weighted by what the real exam tests.

Exam structure and difficulty

The CDL Class A exam is organized into 7 domains, each weighted by the official FMCSA / State DMV exam guide. The heaviest weighting is Driving Safely and On-Road Operation at 25% of the exam, so that is where you should spend the most preparation time. The lightest is Driver Qualifications and CDL Fundamentals at 6%, meaning you can dedicate roughly proportional review time without over-investing.

Format details: State-administered written tests + skills test · Federal CDL standard · Endorsements: H, N, P, S, T, X · 49 CFR Parts 383, 391, 392, 393, 395, 396. Following the domain weights is the single biggest leverage point candidates miss, because many over-study lower-weighted material that feels comfortable.

How to prepare

A practical four-step plan for CDL-A:

  1. Read the official FMCSA / State DMV exam guide for CDL-A and write down every sub-objective. This becomes your study checklist.
  2. Use the domain practice quizzes on this page in weight order (heaviest first). Aim for 80% on each domain before moving on.

Common pitfalls

The three traps that kill CDL-A candidates: (1) over-memorizing acronyms instead of practicing the application of concepts in scenarios, when the exam is scenario-driven, not a vocab quiz. (2) Skipping the heaviest-weighted domain because it feels less interesting, which can fail the whole exam by neglecting Driving Safely and On-Road Operation. (3) Not timing practice sessions, when the exam has a real clock and pacing is its own skill. Build timing into your last two weeks of prep.

CDL-A questions, answered

How many questions are on the CDL general knowledge test?

Most states use a 50-question general knowledge test as the core written exam every CDL applicant must pass.

How many questions are on the CDL combination vehicles test?

Most states use a 20-question combination vehicles test, required for a Class A license since it covers coupling, uncoupling, and handling articulated vehicles.

How many questions are on the CDL air brakes test?

Most states use a 25-question air brakes test. If you skip it or fail it, you can still get a CDL but with an air brake restriction that prevents you from driving vehicles equipped with air brakes.

What is the passing score for the CDL written test?

Most states require 80% correct on each section (general knowledge, air brakes, combination vehicles) to pass.

Is the CDL pretest the same as the permit test?

Yes, in most usage "CDL pretest" and "CDL permit test" refer to the same thing: the written knowledge test you pass to get your commercial learner's permit (CLP) before scheduling the behind-the-wheel skills test.

Can I take the CDL test online?

The written knowledge test is administered at a state DMV or approved testing location, not taken independently online, though some states now offer computer-based testing on-site. QuizBuffet's free practice test is online and lets you study before you go in.
