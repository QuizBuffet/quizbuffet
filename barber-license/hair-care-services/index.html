Sample Questions

Hair porosity refers to the hair's ability to

Answer: absorb and retain moisture

Porosity is the hair's capacity to absorb and hold moisture; it is determined by the condition of the cuticle layer — the overlapping scale-like cells that form the outer protective layer of the hair shaft; low porosity hair has tightly packed, flat cuticle scales that resist moisture absorption and chemical penetration; normal porosity hair absorbs and retains moisture adequately; high porosity hair has lifted or damaged cuticle scales that absorb moisture quickly but also release it rapidly, making it prone to dryness and chemical overprocessing.

Tinea capitis on a child's scalp is a contraindication for barbershop services because

Answer: it is a highly contagious fungal infection that can spread to other clients through contaminated implements and surfaces

Tinea capitis is a dermatophyte fungal infection highly contagious through direct and indirect contact; the dermatophyte spores (particularly from Trichophyton and Microsporum species) are shed from infected hair and scalp and can survive on contaminated combs, clippers, caps, and pillow surfaces for extended periods; performing any barbershop service on an infected scalp risks contaminating all implements and surfaces used during that service, potentially transmitting the infection to subsequent clients.

When selecting a shampoo for a client with a dry scalp, the barber should choose

Answer: a moisturizing shampoo with gentle surfactants and conditioning agents to cleanse without stripping natural oils

A dry scalp has insufficient sebum production and requires a moisturizing shampoo formulated with mild, low-irritation surfactants (such as cocamidopropyl betaine or sodium laureth sulfate at lower concentrations) combined with conditioning and humectant ingredients (glycerin, panthenol, natural oils) that cleanse effectively while leaving enough natural oil in place and adding moisture to prevent further drying.
