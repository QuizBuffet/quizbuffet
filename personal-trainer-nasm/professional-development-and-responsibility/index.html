Sample Questions

NASM recommends that all fitness professionals maintain a focus on evidence-based practice. Which statement best defines evidence-based practice according to NASM?
  • A. The conscientious use of current best evidence in making decisions about client care
  • B. The application of personal training experience to guide all client programming decisions
  • C. The exclusive use of peer-reviewed journals to develop client fitness programs
  • D. The process of testing new fitness methods on clients before they are formally published

NASM defines evidence-based practice as the conscientious use of current best evidence in making decisions about patient or client care.

NASM developed a proprietary exercise training approach designed to be safe and effective for any client working toward any fitness goal. What is this proprietary approach called?
  • A. The Progressive Resistance Training System
  • B. The OPT model
  • C. The Functional Movement Screen
  • D. The Integrated Performance Protocol

NASM's proprietary approach to exercise training is called the OPT model, developed with evidence-based practice as its core guiding philosophy.

A client asks why NASM's training methodology is considered safe and effective for any fitness goal. Which core philosophy underlies NASM's systems and the OPT model?
  • A. Periodization principles developed through Eastern European sport science research
  • B. Evidence-based practice as the core guiding philosophy
  • C. Empirical observation of elite athletes over multiple competitive decades
  • D. Traditional progressive overload theory applied broadly to general populations

NASM's OPT model was developed with evidence-based practice as a core guiding philosophy, which is what makes its systems safe and effective for any client.

A new personal trainer is studying the overall structure of the OPT model. Which statement correctly describes how the OPT model is organized according to NASM?
  • A. It is divided into two levels and four phases of training
  • B. It prioritizes cardiovascular training above resistance training in all five phases
  • C. It is divided into three levels, stabilization, strength, and power, subdivided into five phases
  • D. It consists of a single linear progression from beginner to advanced without discrete phases

NASM's OPT model is divided into three levels (stabilization, strength, and power) that are subdivided into five phases of training.

When reviewing a client's health history, a personal trainer distinguishes between acute and chronic conditions. Which specific characteristic defines an acute disease according to NASM?
  • A. It persists for more than six months without complete resolution
  • B. It requires ongoing pharmaceutical management to prevent recurrence
  • C. It develops gradually over years due to cumulative lifestyle factors
  • D. It occurs suddenly and can typically be treated and healed in a short period of time

NASM defines acute disease as any suddenly occurring medical condition that can be treated and healed in a short period of time.

When designing a program, a fitness professional encounters a training claim that is ambiguous in the current literature. According to NASM's evidence-based practice standard, what should guide the professional's decision?
  • A. Rely on the most widely used method among fitness professionals in local facilities
  • B. Apply the method that produced the best subjective results for other clients in similar situations
  • C. Use the current best available evidence to make the most informed decision possible for the client
  • D. Default to the method recommended by the relevant equipment or supplement manufacturer

NASM's evidence-based practice principle requires using current best evidence to guide decisions, even when that evidence is limited or ambiguous.

A client with no prior resistance training experience begins working with an NASM-CPT. According to the OPT model, what is the primary focus of Phase 1 Stabilization Endurance Training?
  • A. Maximal prime mover strength by lifting heavy loads with low repetitions
  • B. Increasing rate of force production through explosive plyometric-based exercises
  • C. Enhancing stabilization endurance while simultaneously increasing prime mover strength
  • D. Teaching optimal movement patterns, core and joint stability, and familiarizing clients with various exercise modes

Phase 1 Stabilization Endurance Training is designed to teach optimal movement patterns, core and joint stability, and help clients become familiar with various modes of exercise.

Trainer A designs all programs based solely on anecdotal reports from veteran coaches, while Trainer B regularly reviews current peer-reviewed research to inform programming. Which trainer's approach aligns with NASM's recommended standard of practice, and why?
  • A. Trainer A, because veteran coaches reflect decades of real-world application that surpasses controlled study findings
  • B. Both equally, because NASM treats anecdotal and research evidence as equally valid sources of information
  • C. Neither trainer, because NASM recommends relying solely on NASM-proprietary publications and methods
  • D. Trainer B, because NASM specifically recommends an evidence-based practice approach using current best evidence to guide client care

NASM explicitly recommends that fitness professionals maintain a focus on evidence-based practice, the conscientious use of current best evidence, which aligns with Trainer B's approach.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Cardiorespiratory system: Heart, lungs, and blood vessels deliver O₂ and remove CO₂; trained pulse pressure widens, stroke volume rises, resting HR falls
  • FITTE-VP principle: Frequency · Intensity · Time · Type · Enjoyment · Volume · Progression, comprehensive cardio program-design model
  • Special population: older adults: Emphasize stabilization, balance, postural strength; longer warm-ups; chronic disease accommodations
  • Refer-out signs: Disordered eating, depression/anxiety, post-rehab medical, chronic disease without clearance, persistent pain, suspected injury
  • Code of conduct: Maintain confidentiality, avoid dual relationships, no discriminatory practices, accurate marketing, recognize personal limitations
  • Skeletal system roles: Support, protection, movement (lever system), mineral storage (Ca, P), blood cell formation (hematopoiesis)
  • Joint classification: Synarthrodial (immovable), amphiarthrodial (slightly movable), diarthrodial (synovial, freely movable, most movement-relevant)
  • Muscle fiber types: Type I (slow oxidative, fatigue-resistant, low force), Type IIa (fast oxidative-glycolytic, intermediate), Type IIx (fast glycolytic, high force, fatigues fast)
  • Health Belief Model: Behavior change driven by perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers, cues to action, and self-efficacy
  • Waist circumference: Increased disease risk: men >40 in (102 cm), women >35 in (88 cm); reflects visceral fat
