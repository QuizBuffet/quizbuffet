Sample Questions

What is the main purpose of PPE on a construction site?
  • A. To reduce exposure when hazards cannot be eliminated or controlled another way
  • B. Treat PPE as a reason to ignore the hazard
  • C. Use any available PPE even if it does not match the task
  • D. Rely on PPE that is damaged or missing parts

PPE helps protect workers from remaining hazards when other controls do not fully remove exposure. This is the correct answer.

Why should workers still follow safe work practices while wearing PPE?
  • A. PPE reduces exposure but does not make unsafe work safe
  • B. On the project, treat PPE as a reason to ignore the hazard
  • C. On the project, use any available PPE even if it does not match the task
  • D. On the project, rely on PPE that is damaged or missing parts

PPE is not permission to ignore hazards or unsafe conditions. This is the correct answer.

Why should PPE not be the only safety control considered?
  • A. On site, use any available PPE even if it does not match the task
  • B. Other controls may remove or reduce hazards before PPE is needed
  • C. On site, rely on PPE that is damaged or missing parts
  • D. Skip safe work practices because PPE is being worn

PPE is important, but elimination, substitution, engineering controls, and work practices may reduce exposure more effectively. This is the correct answer.

Which statement about PPE purpose and limits is correct?
  • A. At the worksite, use any available PPE even if it does not match the task
  • B. Wearing eye protection when grinding creates flying particles
  • C. At the worksite, rely on PPE that is damaged or missing parts
  • D. On the project, skip safe work practices because PPE is being worn

Eye protection is appropriate when flying particles could injure the eyes. This is the correct answer.

On site, a construction crew is reviewing PPE before starting work. Which task commonly requires eye or face protection?
  • A. During the shift, use cloth gloves for every chemical exposure
  • B. Cutting, grinding, chipping, or work with flying particles
  • C. On site, remove hard hats in active overhead work areas
  • D. On site, wear damaged footwear in areas with sharp debris

Flying particles can strike the eyes or face during these tasks. This is the correct answer.

A construction crew is reviewing PPE before starting work. Which statement about PPE is correct?
  • A. During the shift, rely on PPE that is damaged or missing parts
  • B. On site, skip safe work practices because PPE is being worn
  • C. PPE must match the hazard and be used correctly to provide protection
  • D. On site, treat PPE as a reason to ignore the hazard

PPE works only when it is appropriate for the hazard and used as intended. This is the correct answer.

What should a worker do if PPE interferes with safe work or visibility?
  • A. In the field, rely on PPE that is damaged or missing parts
  • B. At the worksite, skip safe work practices because PPE is being worn
  • C. Stop and ask for proper equipment or adjustment before continuing
  • D. At the worksite, treat PPE as a reason to ignore the hazard

Poorly fitting or obstructive PPE can create hazards and should be corrected. This is the correct answer.

What is a limitation of PPE?
  • A. During the shift, skip safe work practices because PPE is being worn
  • B. During the shift, treat PPE as a reason to ignore the hazard
  • C. During the shift, use any available PPE even if it does not match the task
  • D. It may fail to protect if damaged, poorly fitted, or used incorrectly

PPE can lose effectiveness when it is not maintained, fitted, or worn properly. This is the correct answer.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Hard hat (Class E/G/C): Head protection; Class E rated for high-voltage, Class G for general, Class C provides no electrical protection
  • High-visibility apparel: ANSI/ISEA 107 garments worn around traffic and equipment to make workers visible
  • Assured equipment grounding conductor program (AEGCP): Documented inspection program permitted as an alternative to GFCI on construction sites
  • Eye and face protection: Safety glasses, goggles, or face shields meeting ANSI Z87.1 against impact, chemical splash, or radiation
  • Hearing protection: Earplugs or earmuffs required when noise exposure equals or exceeds 85 dBA TWA
  • Hand protection: Gloves selected for the specific hazard: cut, chemical, electrical, abrasion, or thermal
  • Foot protection: Safety-toe footwear meeting ASTM F2413 against impact, compression, and puncture
  • Employer responsibilities: Provide a safe workplace, free PPE where required, training in a language workers understand, and report severe injuries to OSHA
  • OSHA complaint: A worker may file a confidential complaint requesting an OSHA inspection of a hazardous workplace
  • Whistleblower protection: Section 11(c) prohibits retaliation against workers who exercise OSHA-protected rights
