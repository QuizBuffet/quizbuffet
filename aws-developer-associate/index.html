The DVA-C02 Guide

Everything you need to know before sitting for AWS Certified Developer – Associate

Why DVA-C02 matters in 2026

AWS Certified Developer – Associate is one of the most recognized credentials issued by AWS, and 2026 hiring data continues to show DVA-C02 on job postings as either required or strongly preferred for the roles it targets. The certification validates that you can apply real working knowledge — not just recall facts — across 4 distinct exam domains, with the largest weight on Development with AWS Services. For candidates competing in the AWS ecosystem, DVA-C02 is a clear signal to a hiring manager that you have invested in measurable, third-party-verified competence.

Who should take DVA-C02

DVA-C02 is most useful for professionals working in or moving toward the AWS ecosystem. In short: Develop, deploy, and debug cloud-based applications on AWS. If you are early in your career, DVA-C02 is one of the fastest credentials to add to a résumé that recruiters actively screen for. If you are mid-career, it formalizes the skills you already use day-to-day and unlocks roles that gate on it. If you are switching tracks, it gives you a structured curriculum — exactly what to study, in what order, weighted by what the real exam tests.

Exam structure and difficulty

The AWS Certified Developer – Associate exam is organized into 4 domains, each weighted by the official AWS exam guide. The heaviest weighting is Development with AWS Services at 32% of the exam — that is where you should spend the most preparation time. The lightest is Troubleshooting and Optimization at 18%, meaning you can dedicate roughly proportional review time without over-investing.

Format details: 65 questions (50 scored + 15 unscored) · 130 minutes · Multiple-choice and multiple-response · Pass score 720/1000. Following the domain weights is the single biggest leverage point candidates miss — many over-study lower-weighted material because it feels comfortable.

Common pitfalls

The three traps that kill DVA-C02 candidates: (1) over-memorizing acronyms instead of practicing the application of concepts in scenarios — the exam is scenario-driven, not a vocab quiz. (2) Skipping the heaviest-weighted domain because it feels less interesting — you can fail the whole exam by neglecting Development with AWS Services. (3) Not timing practice sessions — the exam has a real clock, and pacing is its own skill. Build timing into your last two weeks of prep.
