The Auditing and Attestation (AUD) section is one of three Core sections of the Uniform CPA Examination administered by the AICPA. Every CPA candidate must pass AUD regardless of their chosen Discipline. The 4-hour exam consists of 78 multiple-choice questions and 7 task-based simulations, weighted 50/50 in scoring. Content covers four areas: Ethics, Professional Responsibilities and General Principles (15-25%), AICPA Code of Conduct, SEC/PCAOB/GAO/DOL independence, professional skepticism, engagement preconditions, documentation; Assessing Risk and Developing a Planned Response (25-35%), engagement strategy, the COSO Internal Control. Integrated Framework, IT general controls, SOC 1® reports, materiality and tolerable misstatement, fraud risk, related parties, single audits; Performing Further Procedures and Obtaining Evidence (30-40%), sampling, tests of controls and details, analytical procedures, external confirmations, audit data analytics, accounting estimates, going concern, subsequent events; and Forming Conclusions and Reporting (10-20%): audit and attestation reports, opinion types, emphasis-of-matter paragraphs, compilation and review engagements, compliance reporting, special-purpose frameworks, and additional GAO reporting requirements. References include AICPA Statements on Auditing Standards, PCAOB Auditing Standards, GAO Government Auditing Standards, Single Audit Act, OMB 2 CFR 200, and the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct.

Why AUD matters in 2026

CPA Auditing and Attestation (AUD) is one of the most recognized credentials issued by AICPA, and 2026 hiring data continues to show AUD on job postings as either required or strongly preferred for the roles it targets. The certification validates that you can apply real working knowledge, not just recall facts, across 4 distinct exam domains, with the largest weight on Assessing Risk and Developing a Planned Response. For candidates competing in the AICPA ecosystem, AUD is a clear signal to a hiring manager that you have invested in measurable, third-party-verified competence.

Who should take AUD

AUD is most useful for professionals working in or moving toward the AICPA ecosystem. In short: Core section of the Uniform CPA Examination, every candidate must pass AUD. If you are early in your career, AUD is one of the fastest credentials to add to a résumé that recruiters actively screen for. If you are mid-career, it formalizes the skills you already use day-to-day and unlocks roles that gate on it. If you are switching tracks, it gives you a structured curriculum that tells you exactly what to study, in what order, weighted by what the real exam tests.

Exam structure and difficulty

The CPA Auditing and Attestation (AUD) exam is organized into 4 domains, each weighted by the official AICPA exam guide. The heaviest weighting is Assessing Risk and Developing a Planned Response at 30% of the exam, so that is where you should spend the most preparation time. The lightest is Ethics, Professional Responsibilities and General Principles at 20%, meaning you can dedicate roughly proportional review time without over-investing.

Common pitfalls

The three traps that kill AUD candidates: (1) over-memorizing acronyms instead of practicing the application of concepts in scenarios, when the exam is scenario-driven, not a vocab quiz. (2) Skipping the heaviest-weighted domain because it feels less interesting, which can fail the whole exam by neglecting Assessing Risk and Developing a Planned Response. (3) Not timing practice sessions, when the exam has a real clock and pacing is its own skill. Build timing into your last two weeks of prep.

AUD questions, answered

What is the passing score for AUD?

Like every CPA Exam section, you need 75 on AICPA's 0-99 scale.

How much does the AUD exam cost?

CPA Exam section fees are set by your state board, typically in the $250 to $350 range per section, plus a separate application/registration fee. Check your specific state board for the exact current fee.

Do I have to pass AUD before the other sections?

No. AICPA does not require a specific order among the 3 Core sections (AUD, FAR, REG) and 1 Discipline section you choose. Most candidates start with whichever section aligns closest to their recent coursework or work experience.

How long do I have to pass all 4 CPA sections?

You have a rolling 30-month window from the date you pass your first section to pass the remaining 3, or your earliest passing score expires and you have to retake that section.

Is AUD the hardest CPA section?

Pass rates put it in the middle of the pack, harder than BEC-era sections used to be but generally seen as more approachable than FAR. The professional skepticism and judgment-based questions trip up candidates who only memorize procedures instead of understanding when to apply them.
