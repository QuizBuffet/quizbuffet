Sample Questions

When setting up supplies for a new client after breakdown, the candidate should
  • A. begin with freshly disinfected or newly barriered surfaces and new sterile packaged implements from the supply kit, never reusing single-session items from the previous client's procedure
  • B. reuse the same barriers from the previous client if they appear visually clean
  • C. use the same ink caps from the previous session if unused pigment remains
  • D. skip the surface disinfection step since the surfaces were just disinfected during breakdown

Each new client requires a completely fresh setup: all surfaces must have been disinfected during the breakdown of the previous procedure and new barriers applied before the new client's supplies are arranged; new sterile packaged implements (needles, tubes) from the supply kit are used for each client; no materials from the previous client's procedure, used or unused, are carried forward to the next client's session.

During Section 6, the examiner instructs the candidate to demonstrate disinfection and storage for transportation of the foot switch. The foot switch must first be
  • A. placed in the sharps container since it was near the procedure area
  • B. returned to the manufacturer in its original packaging
  • C. submerged in disinfectant solution for 20 minutes before transport
  • D. wiped thoroughly with EPA-registered disinfectant wipes on all surfaces before being placed on the floor under the table as directed by the examiner

The NIC CIB specifies in Section 6 that the examiner will instruct the candidate to 'demonstrate disinfection and storage for transportation of foot switch only' and to 'place the foot switch on the floor under the table'; the foot switch must be wiped with EPA-registered disinfectant wipes on all surfaces (particularly the areas contacted during use), allowed to air dry or the contact time to elapse, and then placed on the floor under the table as directed, separated from clean supplies during transport because it is designated as a contaminated item throughout the examination.

The NIC CIB instructs candidates not to assemble the machine or disperse pigment during Section 6 work area setup. This is because
  • A. machine assembly and pigment dispensing are evaluated as separate tasks in their own sections. Section 6 assesses the candidate's ability to prepare the universal work area and equipment setup, not the complete procedure-specific assembly
  • B. machines and pigment are borrowed from the examination facility for Section 6 only
  • C. the examination ends at the conclusion of Section 6 regardless of whether assembly is complete
  • D. assembling the machine in Section 6 would duplicate what was already done in Section 3 without adding examination value

The NIC Tattooing Practical Examination is structured so that each section evaluates specific competencies independently; Section 3 evaluates machine assembly; Section 4 evaluates pigment dispensing and tattooing technique; Section 6 specifically evaluates the candidate's ability to set up the work area and universal supply area for a new client, confirming they can establish a clean, properly organized work environment independently of the procedure-specific assembly steps; requiring assembly and pigment dispensing in Section 6 would conflate these distinct competencies and reduce the examination's ability to assess each independently.

Why must the foot switch be stored separately from clean supplies during transport?
  • A. The foot switch emits electrical signals that could interfere with other electronic equipment in the supply kit
  • B. The foot switch is bulky and takes up too much space in the standard supply kit
  • C. As a designated contaminated item from the procedure (having contacted the floor throughout the session), it must be kept separate from clean supplies to prevent cross-contamination during transport, even after disinfection
  • D. The foot switch belongs to the examination facility and must be returned separately at the end of the session

The NIC CIB explicitly designates the foot switch as a 'contaminated' item throughout the examination because it contacts the floor and is operated by the foot in the procedure environment; even after disinfection, the foot switch is categorized separately from clean supplies to maintain clear contamination status boundaries during transport; this categorical separation prevents any potential residual contamination from contaminating clean supply kit contents during storage and transit.

The sequence of hand hygiene and gloving for the new client setup should be
  • A. don gloves first, then perform hand hygiene through the gloves using hand sanitizer
  • B. perform hand hygiene first (handwashing or sanitizer), then don fresh medical-grade gloves, maintaining the inside of the gloves and the hands as clean as possible
  • C. skip hand hygiene if hands appear visually clean from the previous glove use
  • D. perform hand hygiene only after gloving to extend the clean period between hand contacts

The correct sequence is always: (1) perform hand hygiene on bare hands; (2) allow hands to dry completely; (3) don fresh medical-grade gloves; this sequence ensures the hands are clean before being enclosed in the gloves, preventing contamination from being sealed inside the glove environment; the inside of gloves remains as clean as the hands that were put into them, hence the requirement to clean hands first.

When reapplying barriers for the new client setup, the key difference from the initial setup is
  • A. barriers for the second client may be applied over the first client's used barriers to save time
  • B. fewer barriers are needed for the second client since some surfaces were not contaminated
  • C. there is no difference, the same barrier application principles and coverage standards apply regardless of which client is being prepared for
  • D. barriers for the second client can be a different color to differentiate the setups

Barrier color is an aesthetic variation with no infection control significance; the same barrier application standards, covering all surfaces that will be handled during the procedure, apply uniformly regardless of color or which client number this represents in the day.

At the conclusion of the entire examination, all kit supplies, equipment, and disposable materials are
  • A. left at the examination station for the facility to properly dispose of
  • B. donated to the examination facility for use in future examinations
  • C. taken by the candidate, the proctor reads: 'Make sure that all kit, supplies, equipment, and disposable materials are taken with you'
  • D. submitted to the examiner for review of supply adequacy before being returned to the candidate

The NIC CIB specifies that at the conclusion of the examination, the proctor reads: 'Make sure that all kit, supplies, equipment, and disposable materials are taken with you', this explicit instruction places full responsibility on the candidate for removing all their materials from the examination space; this includes clean supplies in the kit, equipment, waste containers, and all disposable materials used during the examination.

The candidate supply kit must remain closed during the examination except when removing specific materials. The infection control purpose of this requirement is
  • A. to prevent other candidates from accessing the candidate's supplies during the examination
  • B. to maintain the clean, uncontaminated status of supplies remaining in the kit, preventing airborne contamination from the procedure environment (blood aerosols, ink mist) from settling into open supply containers and contaminating unused supplies
  • C. to comply with the NIC examination rule that all kit materials must be documented before opening
  • D. to prevent supplies from becoming too warm from room temperature exposure while open

An active tattoo procedure generates aerosols of blood, ink, and skin debris from the needle's action; these particles become airborne and can settle on any open surface in the work area; supplies remaining in the kit (sterile packaged needles, tubes, clean barriers, unused ink caps) must be protected from this airborne contamination by keeping the kit closed; an open supply kit in the procedure environment could have clean supplies contaminated by settling procedure aerosols, making those supplies unsafe for the next client.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Work Area Setup: First domain of the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination: candidate prepares themselves, disinfects all work surfaces, sets up supplies and equipment, designates the universal supply area, and disposes of contaminated supplies, all within 20 minutes.
  • Cross-Contamination: Transfer of pathogens from a contaminated source to a clean surface or client; prevented by barriers, single-use items, and strict zone separation between clean and contaminated areas.
  • Foot Switch: Pedal-operated power control for the tattoo machine; classified as a contaminated item during the NIC practical exam and must be stored separately from clean supplies, then disinfected for transport between clients.
  • Bloodborne Pathogen Standard: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030: federal regulation requiring exposure control plans, PPE, hepatitis B vaccination offers, and post-exposure procedures for all workers with occupational blood exposure.
  • Single-Use Supplies: Items designed for one client only: including needles, tubes, ink caps, gloves, and razors, that must be discarded after each client to prevent cross-contamination.
  • Pigment: Tattoo colorant suspended in a carrier solution; regulated by state and local rules; must be stored and handled per SDS guidelines; dispersed into single-use ink caps: the bottle must never contact the client or used equipment.
  • Contraindications: Client conditions that prevent or delay tattooing, including active skin infection, blood-thinning medications, isotretinoin use, pregnancy, keloid history, and areas with impaired sensation or circulation.
  • Infection Control: Practices that prevent the transmission of pathogens, including hand hygiene, surface disinfection, barrier use, and single-use supplies, required throughout the tattooing process.
  • EPA-Registered Disinfectant: Surface disinfectant registered with the Environmental Protection Agency; must demonstrate bactericidal, fungicidal, and virucidal properties; aerosol sprays are prohibited in NIC testing environments.
  • Autoclave: Device that uses saturated steam under pressure (typically 121 °C at 15 psi) to sterilize instruments and packaged supplies such as tubes and needles.
