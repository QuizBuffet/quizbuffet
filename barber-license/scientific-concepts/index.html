Sample Questions

Anthrax and tetanus bacilli form which type of spores?
  • A. Endospores
  • B. Flagella
  • C. Spherical
  • D. Infectious

Anthrax (Bacillus anthracis) and tetanus (Clostridium tetani) are rod-shaped bacteria (bacilli) that produce endospores, highly resistant, dormant structures formed inside the bacterial cell; endospores can survive extreme heat, dryness, chemicals, and UV radiation for years, which is why they require sterilization (not mere disinfection) to destroy them.

Sanitation is defined as the process of
  • A. destroying all microbial life including spores
  • B. killing all pathogens on a surface
  • C. significantly reducing microorganisms on a surface to a safe level
  • D. removing all visible dirt with dry cloth

Sanitation is the lowest level of infection control; it reduces the number of microorganisms on a surface or implement to a level considered safe for public health, without claiming to kill all pathogens; examples include handwashing and wiping surfaces with soap and water; it is the required first step before disinfection.

The most common way pathogens spread in a barbershop is through
  • A. airborne particles only
  • B. contaminated food served in the shop
  • C. direct contact with infected persons or contaminated implements
  • D. touching doorknobs only

In a barbershop, pathogens spread most commonly through direct contact, touching an infected client's skin, scalp, or blood, and through indirect contact via contaminated implements (combs, clippers, razors) that carry microorganisms from one client to the next; this is why sanitation and disinfection of implements between clients is required.

Before placing implements into disinfectant solution, the barber must first
  • A. clean them thoroughly to remove all visible debris, hair, and organic material
  • B. soak them in alcohol for 30 minutes
  • C. place them in an autoclave
  • D. dry them completely in an oven

Cleaning (sanitation) must precede disinfection; organic material such as hair, skin cells, blood, and product residue physically shields microorganisms from the disinfectant solution; if implements are placed directly into disinfectant without pre-cleaning, the disinfectant cannot make effective contact with microbial cells, rendering the disinfection step unreliable.

An EPA-registered disinfectant used in a barbershop must be
  • A. made in the United States only
  • B. applied to uncleaned surfaces for best results
  • C. stored in a refrigerator at all times
  • D. used at the concentration and contact time specified on the label to achieve the claimed kill

The EPA registration of a disinfectant is based on specific testing at a particular concentration and dwell time (contact time); using the product at a lower concentration or wiping it off before the specified contact time means the full kill claims printed on the label are not achieved, the barber must follow label instructions precisely.

A contagious disease is one that
  • A. requires a vector such as a mosquito to spread
  • B. can be spread from person to person through direct or indirect contact
  • C. is caused only by fungi
  • D. cannot be treated with antibiotics

A contagious (communicable) disease is one that spreads from person to person either by direct contact (touching infected skin, blood, or secretions) or indirect contact (using contaminated implements, towels, or other objects); in barbering, recognizing contagious conditions is essential for preventing spread between clients.

Proper handwashing in a barbershop requires scrubbing with soap for at least
  • A. 5 seconds
  • B. 10 seconds
  • C. 30 seconds
  • D. 20 seconds

The CDC and public health guidelines recommend washing hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, roughly the time it takes to hum 'Happy Birthday' twice; this duration provides adequate friction and soap contact time to mechanically remove transient microorganisms from the skin surface.

Bacteria that divide by splitting into two equal cells are reproducing by a process called
  • A. sporulation
  • B. mitosis
  • C. conjugation
  • D. binary fission

Binary fission is the primary method of bacterial reproduction; a bacterium replicates its DNA and then divides into two genetically identical daughter cells; under ideal conditions bacteria can divide every 20 minutes, which explains how infections can escalate rapidly without adequate sanitation and disinfection.
