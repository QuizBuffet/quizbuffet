Sample Questions

A remote pilot is introduced to the FAA's Perceive-Process-Perform (3P) model of aeronautical decision-making during ground school. A complex sUAS mapping mission is being planned near a hospital with active helicopter traffic. Applying the 3P model, what does the 'Perceive' step require the pilot to do?
  • A. Select the best risk management option and execute the flight plan without further delay
  • B. Perform an immediate risk mitigation action such as cancelling the flight before gathering any situational data
  • C. Identify and list all hazards present in the environment, including airspace conflicts, weather, equipment status, and personnel factors
  • D. Notify the FAA of all potential hazards identified before each commercial sUAS operation

The 'Perceive' step of the 3P model requires the pilot to actively scan the environment and identify all relevant hazards including weather, airspace, equipment, human factors, and external pressures; this situational awareness forms the basis for subsequent processing and action.

A remote pilot says, 'I've been flying drones commercially for three years and I know what I'm doing, I don't need to follow every FAA rule.' This attitude most closely matches which hazardous attitude, and what is its antidote?
  • A. Anti-authority; the antidote is 'Follow the rules, they are usually right'
  • B. Invulnerability; the antidote is 'It could happen to me'
  • C. Impulsivity; the antidote is 'Not so fast, think first'
  • D. Macho; the antidote is 'Taking chances is foolish'

Anti-authority is the hazardous attitude of resenting or disregarding anyone who sets rules or limitations; the antidote is recognizing that 'rules are usually right' and exist because of hard-learned lessons; the pilot's dismissal of FAA regulations based on personal experience is a classic anti-authority statement.

A remote pilot uses the PAVE checklist as a preflight risk assessment tool. The pilot is under pressure from a client to fly in marginal weather to meet a project deadline. Which PAVE element specifically addresses this type of external pressure?
  • A. The 'P' element. Pilot, because external pressure directly affects the pilot's personal minimums and go/no-go judgment
  • B. The 'A' element. Aircraft, because client-imposed schedules affect maintenance intervals and aircraft readiness
  • C. The 'E' element. External Pressures, which is specifically designed to identify non-safety pressures such as schedules, economic concerns, and client expectations
  • D. The 'V' element, enVironment, because weather-driven client pressure is an environmental rather than a personal factor

The 'E' in PAVE stands for External Pressures, factors outside the cockpit (or control station) that push the pilot toward accepting risk, such as schedule demands, economic pressure, client expectations, and 'get-there-itis'; recognizing these pressures explicitly helps the pilot make an objective go/no-go decision.

During a preflight brief, a remote pilot dismisses concerns about a possible loss of GPS signal near high-voltage power lines, saying 'I've flown near power lines dozens of times and never had an issue.' This statement reflects which hazardous attitude?
  • A. Invulnerability, the pilot believes that because accidents haven't happened before, they won't happen in the future
  • B. Resignation, the pilot has given up trying to mitigate risks and accepts bad outcomes as inevitable
  • C. Anti-authority, the pilot is refusing to acknowledge the technical authority of those who identified the GPS interference risk
  • D. Impulsivity, the pilot is making a rushed decision to fly without properly thinking through the identified hazard

Invulnerability is the hazardous attitude that 'accidents happen to other people, not me'; past success without incident is a classic invulnerability trigger, the pilot is using a history of safe flights as evidence of immunity from future risk, which is statistically unsound.

A remote pilot uses the 5 Ps checklist (Plan, Plane, Pilot, Passengers, Programming) as an in-flight risk management tool. Thirty minutes into a complex automated survey mission, the pilot realizes the battery level will not support the full planned flight time. Which 'P' element is being evaluated in this real-time assessment?
  • A. Pilot, the pilot's personal minimums and decision-making capacity are being re-evaluated mid-flight
  • B. Passengers, the safety of any persons on the ground below is being reassessed based on flight time remaining
  • C. Programming, the autopilot's mission program is being assessed to determine whether it can be modified to reduce power consumption
  • D. Plan, the mission plan is being re-evaluated in light of new information about battery state, requiring a plan adjustment

The Plan element covers whether the intended mission can be completed as designed; discovering that battery capacity is insufficient to execute the full plan requires re-evaluating and modifying the plan, which is the Plan 'P' in the 5 Ps framework.

A remote pilot deliberately flies the sUAS through a narrow gap between two buildings at high speed to impress a film crew, saying 'Anyone can fly slow and safe, real skill means pushing limits.' Which hazardous attitude does this demonstrate and what is its antidote?
  • A. Macho, this pilot equates reckless high-speed gap flights with genuine skill; the antidote is recognizing that taking unnecessary chances is foolish, not impressive
  • B. Macho, the pilot is proving toughness by deliberately choosing a dangerous gap flight to impress the film crew; taking unnecessary risks to demonstrate ability is the hallmark of this attitude
  • C. Resignation, the pilot has accepted that spectacular maneuvers will eventually cause an accident and continues anyway
  • D. Anti-authority, the pilot is refusing to accept safe operating boundaries established by the manufacturer and FAA

Macho is the hazardous attitude of proving courage or superiority by taking needless risks; deliberately flying a narrow gap at high speed to impress a film crew exemplifies macho behavior; the antidote is the insight that taking unnecessary chances is foolish and that real professionalism is demonstrated by sound, safe decision-making.

A remote pilot has experienced an sUAS flyaway and the aircraft is heading toward a populated suburban neighborhood. The pilot has regained partial manual control but cannot stop forward flight. The aircraft will impact the ground in approximately 25 seconds. Which decision best reflects sound aeronautical decision-making under this emergency?
  • A. Direct the aircraft toward the least-populated or completely open area within reach, accepting a controlled crash over an open field rather than an uncontrolled impact near people
  • B. Command maximum altitude to gain time and wait for the flight controller to self-correct the fault before the aircraft runs out of battery
  • C. Immediately land in the nearest yard regardless of whether people are visible, because any ground contact minimizes the risk of aerial collision
  • D. Continue attempting full control inputs while calling 911 to alert authorities; do not deviate from the original flight path

When facing an imminent uncontrolled impact, the best ADM outcome is to direct the aircraft toward the area that minimizes risk to people; guiding the aircraft to a controlled crash in an open field, even at cost to the aircraft, is consistent with the principle of minimizing harm to third parties.

A remote pilot experiences a flyaway event where the sUAS departs the operating area at high speed despite maximum control inputs. After the event, the manufacturer confirms a compass calibration error caused the flight controller to misidentify north and command full speed in the wrong direction. Which preflight action would have been most likely to prevent this event?
  • A. Performing a full compass calibration at the launch site on the day of the flight, especially if the aircraft has been transported to a new location with different magnetic environment
  • B. Increasing the maximum control input sensitivity so the pilot's corrective inputs would have been fast enough to overcome the flight controller error
  • C. Using a backup mechanical compass mounted on the aircraft that provides independent heading reference independent of the electronic flight controller
  • D. Setting the maximum speed limit to 10 knots to prevent the sUAS from flying away faster than the pilot can walk to recover it

Compass calibration errors are a known cause of flyaway events; performing a fresh calibration at the operating site, particularly after transport (which can expose the aircraft to different magnetic environments), is the most effective preventive measure against compass-induced flyaways.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Aeronautical Decision Making (ADM): Systematic approach to consistently determine the best course of action in response to a given set of circumstances
  • Risk management 5-step: Identify hazard, assess risk, analyze controls, make control decisions, use controls and review
  • Battery management: Cold reduces capacity; pre-warm in cold weather; never fly to 0%: keep reserve for safe RTH/landing; LiPo fire risk if punctured / over-discharged
  • PAVE checklist: Pilot · Aircraft · enVironment · External pressures, preflight risk-identification model
  • DECIDE model: Detect · Estimate · Choose · Identify · Do · Evaluate, closed-loop decision-making
  • Hazardous attitudes: Antiauthority, Impulsivity, Invulnerability, Macho, Resignation: recognize and apply the antidote
  • Emergency procedures: Brief team before flight, maintain VLOS, plan abort points, identify safe landing zones, keep first-aid and fire suppression nearby for LiPo fires
  • Stress and fatigue: Both degrade decision-making; symptoms include slowed reactions, fixation, irritability: postpone or hand off the operation
  • One aircraft at a time §107.35: A remote PIC or person manipulating the controls may only operate one sUAS at a time
  • Preflight inspection §107.49: Required before each flight; assess local weather, airspace, op limits, condition of aircraft, control links, and battery
