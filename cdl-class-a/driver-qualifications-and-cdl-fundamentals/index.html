Sample Questions

A driver wants to operate a tractor pulling a 53-foot semitrailer with a gross combination weight rating of 80,000 pounds, where the trailer alone is rated at 30,000 pounds. Which CDL class is required to legally drive this combination?
  • A. Class B (single-vehicle license)
  • B. Class A (combination-vehicle license)
  • C. Class C (small-vehicle CDL)
  • D. A standard non-CDL operator's license is sufficient

Class A is required when the GCWR is 26,001 pounds or more and the towed unit's GVWR is more than 10,000 pounds, which fits this tractor-semitrailer.

A driver plans to haul liquid milk in a 6,000-gallon tank trailer permanently attached to the chassis, with no hazardous materials placards required. Which CDL endorsement is required?
  • A. N, the tank vehicle endorsement
  • B. H, the hazardous materials endorsement
  • C. P, the passenger transport endorsement
  • D. T, the doubles/triples endorsement

The N endorsement is required to haul liquid or gaseous materials in a tank with rated capacity over 119 gallons individually and 1,000 gallons aggregate.

Compare the licensing requirements for a single straight truck versus a tractor-trailer combination. Which CDL class covers a single vehicle with a GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more, or such a vehicle towing a unit not in excess of 10,000 pounds GVWR?
  • A. Class B, the heavy-single-vehicle license
  • B. Class A, which always requires a heavy combination
  • C. Class C, regardless of vehicle weight
  • D. Federal rules do not require a CDL in this case

Class B is defined as a single CMV at or above 26,001 pounds GVWR, including such a vehicle towing a unit no heavier than 10,000 pounds GVWR.

An applicant intends to haul placarded hazardous liquids in a 9,000-gallon tank that is permanently attached to the trailer chassis. According to federal endorsement codes, which single endorsement covers both the tank and hazardous-materials portions of this operation?
  • A. Only the H endorsement is needed
  • B. The X endorsement, which combines tank and hazardous materials
  • C. Only the N endorsement is needed
  • D. The S endorsement for school-related hauls

The X endorsement is the combined credential that authorizes both tank vehicle operation and hazardous materials hauling in one endorsement.

Federal law requires a Transportation Security Administration background check and fingerprinting before a state may issue one specific CDL endorsement. Which endorsement is subject to that TSA security threat assessment?
  • A. The N tank-vehicle endorsement
  • B. The P passenger-transport endorsement
  • C. The H hazardous-materials endorsement, which requires fingerprinting under federal law
  • D. The T doubles/triples endorsement

The H (hazardous materials) endorsement uniquely requires fingerprinting and a TSA security threat assessment under federal regulations.

An operator drives a 22,000-pound vehicle designed to transport 24 passengers including the driver, and it is not placarded for hazardous materials. According to federal CDL classification rules, which license is required?
  • A. Class A combination license
  • B. Class B straight-truck license
  • C. Class C, because the vehicle is rated for 16 or more passengers including the driver
  • D. A standard non-commercial license, since GVWR is below 26,001 pounds

Class C covers any vehicle under the Class A or B weight thresholds that is designed to transport 16 or more passengers including the driver.

Within a ten-year period, a CDL holder has been convicted of two separate violations of an out-of-service order. According to federal disqualification rules, what is the minimum disqualification period?
  • A. 60 days
  • B. 90 days
  • C. One year for the second out-of-service-order violation in a ten-year window
  • D. Three years

Two violations of an out-of-service order within a ten-year period carry a minimum one-year CDL disqualification.

Consider a tractor with a GVWR of 18,000 pounds towing a trailer with a GVWR of 12,000 pounds, where the rated combination weight is 30,500 pounds. Which CDL class applies to this operation under federal rules?
  • A. Class C, because passenger count drives the classification
  • B. Class B, since neither unit individually exceeds 26,001 pounds
  • C. No CDL is required because the tractor's GVWR is only 18,000 pounds
  • D. Class A, because the combined GCWR exceeds 26,001 pounds and the towed unit's GVWR exceeds 10,000 pounds

Both Class A triggers are met: GCWR of 30,500 pounds is at or above 26,001 pounds, and the towed trailer GVWR of 12,000 pounds exceeds 10,000 pounds.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • CDL Class A: Required for combination vehicles with GCWR over 26,001 lb when the towed unit exceeds 10,000 lb
  • CDL Class C: Required for a vehicle designed for 16+ passengers (incl. driver) or carrying placarded hazardous materials
  • Endorsement P (passenger): Required for vehicles designed for 16+ passengers including the driver
  • Endorsement T (doubles/triples): Required to operate longer combination vehicles with two or three trailers; Class A only
  • Endorsement X: Combined HAZMAT + tank vehicle endorsement
  • Endorsement N (tank): Required for tank vehicles transporting liquid or gas (single-tank capacity ≥ 119 gal or aggregate ≥ 1,000 gal)
  • Parallel parking: Maneuver the vehicle into a marked parking spot parallel to the curb on the driver or passenger side
  • CDL Class B: Required for a single vehicle with GVWR over 26,001 lb (or towing a unit 10,000 lb or less)
  • Restriction L (air brakes): Applied when a driver does not take the CDL skills test in a vehicle with full air brakes; cannot operate air-brake CMVs
  • BAC limit (CMV): 0.04% blood alcohol concentration is the per-se limit for CMV drivers; state limit for non-commercial is typically 0.08%
