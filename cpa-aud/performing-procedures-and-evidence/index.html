Sample Questions

In performing audit procedures, auditors commonly use data from which two primary sources?

Answer: Entity-produced data (accounting records, transaction files, master files) and external data (market prices, third-party records, industry benchmarks)

AU-C Section 500 recognizes that audit evidence comes from two broad sources: (1) entity-produced data — including accounting records (general ledger, subsidiary ledgers), transaction files (purchase orders, invoices, receiving reports), master files (customer files, vendor files), and entity-prepared reports (aged receivables listings, inventory compilations); and (2) external data — including market prices from external data providers, industry benchmarks, confirmation responses from third parties, and regulatory filings. The auditor uses both types, evaluating the reliability of each based on its source and how it was obtained.

Before using an entity-provided transaction data file to perform audit sampling, an auditor should verify the completeness and accuracy of the file. Which procedure is MOST appropriate for this purpose?

Answer: Reconcile the file's record counts and total dollar amounts to independently prepared control totals — such as summary reports from the entity's accounting system or the trial balance

Reconciling record counts (total number of transactions in the file) and total dollar amounts to independent control totals — such as totals from the entity's accounting system summary reports, the trial balance, or bank statements — provides evidence that the file contains all records (completeness) and that the amounts are accurate (accuracy). If the file reconciles to these independently prepared totals, the auditor has reasonable assurance that the data is complete and accurate for audit use.

Which of the following general principles BEST describes the relative reliability of audit evidence?

Answer: Evidence obtained directly by the auditor (e.g., through observation or physical inspection) and evidence from external independent sources are generally more reliable than entity-produced evidence obtained from management

AU-C Section 500 provides a reliability hierarchy: evidence obtained directly by the auditor (through inspection, observation, or computation) is generally more reliable than evidence provided by the entity; and evidence from independent external sources (bank confirmations, attorney letters, independent appraisals) is generally more reliable than internally generated entity evidence. These principles guide the auditor in designing procedures that provide the most persuasive evidence available.
