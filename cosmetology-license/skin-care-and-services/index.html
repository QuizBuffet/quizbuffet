Sample Questions

During a skin analysis before a facial service, a cosmetologist identifies a client's skin type. Which characteristic most clearly indicates normal skin type?

Answer: A balanced moisture and oil content, small pores, smooth texture, and even tone with no significant dry patches or oily zones

Normal skin is characterized by a balanced sebum-to-moisture ratio that produces a healthy, neither oily nor dry appearance; pores are small and not visibly enlarged, the texture is smooth and refined, and the tone is even — this is the skin type that requires the least corrective care and tolerates the widest range of products.

During a pre-facial consultation, a cosmetologist observes areas of darker pigmentation on a client's cheeks and forehead that are flat, non-raised, and symmetrically distributed. The client reports using no new products but has been spending more time outdoors. This presentation is consistent with which skin condition?

Answer: Hyperpigmentation — specifically solar lentigines or melasma — where melanocytes produce excess melanin in response to UV exposure, creating flat, darker-than-surrounding-skin patches

Hyperpigmentation describes areas where melanocytes produce excess melanin; solar lentigines (sun spots) are flat, uniformly darker macules caused by localized UV-induced melanocyte activity, and melasma is a hormonally influenced UV-triggered hyperpigmentation that appears in a symmetrical butterfly pattern on the cheeks, forehead, and upper lip — both fitting the described presentation of flat, symmetrical darker patches correlating with increased sun exposure.

A client arrives for a facial service and the cosmetologist observes several large, weeping, crusted lesions on the client's cheek. The cosmetologist should:

Answer: Decline the facial service, document the observation, and refer the client to a physician before providing any facial service — active weeping or crusted lesions indicate a possible infection or inflammatory condition that contraindicates professional skin services

Active, weeping, or crusted skin lesions are an absolute contraindication for cosmetology facial services; they may indicate an infectious condition (bacterial, viral, or fungal), an inflammatory flare, or another medical condition requiring diagnosis; the professional and legally appropriate response is to decline the service, document the finding, and refer the client to a physician for evaluation before any service is provided.
