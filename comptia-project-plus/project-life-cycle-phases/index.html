Sample Questions

What is the PRIMARY purpose of the business case in the discovery/concept phase of a project?

Answer: To provide the justification for undertaking the project — documenting the business need, expected benefits, estimated costs, risks, and the decision criteria that authorize the investment of organizational resources

BUSINESS CASE: the foundational document created in the discovery/concept phase that answers the question 'WHY should we do this project?' BUSINESS CASE COMPONENTS: PROBLEM/OPPORTUNITY STATEMENT: what problem is being solved or opportunity being captured?; BUSINESS OBJECTIVES: what specific, measurable outcomes are expected?; BENEFITS ANALYSIS: quantitative (revenue increase, cost savings, efficiency gains) and qualitative (customer experience, competitive positioning, regulatory compliance); COST ESTIMATES: total investment required (capital + operational costs); OPTIONS ANALYSIS: multiple approaches considered with trade-offs; RISKS: key risks and uncertainties; ROI / FINANCIAL ANALYSIS: return on investment, payback period, NPV, IRR; RECOMMENDATION: which option is recommended and why. PURPOSE: enables decision-makers (sponsors, steering committees, executives) to approve or reject the project authorization; ensures organizational resources are invested in projects that deliver sufficient value; provides the strategic rationale that will govern the project throughout its lifecycle.

A business case shows a project investment of $200,000 that will generate net benefits of $50,000 per year. The ROI formula is (Net Benefits / Investment) × 100%. What is the ROI, and what does this indicate?

Answer: ROI = 25% — for every $1 invested, the project returns $0.25 in net benefit annually; the payback period is 4 years ($200K ÷ $50K/year)

ROI CALCULATION: ROI = (Net Benefits / Investment) × 100% = ($50,000 / $200,000) × 100% = 25%. INTERPRETATION: the project returns 25 cents for every dollar invested per year; PAYBACK PERIOD: $200,000 ÷ $50,000/year = 4 years to fully recover the investment; after 4 years, the project generates pure net benefit. BUSINESS CASE DECISION: whether 25% ROI is acceptable depends on: the organization's required rate of return (hurdle rate); alternative investment opportunities; strategic value beyond the quantified financial return; risk profile. A 25% annual ROI is generally considered favorable for an IT project with manageable risk. ADDITIONAL FINANCIAL METRICS used alongside ROI in business cases: Net Present Value (NPV): accounts for time value of money (future benefits discounted to present value); Internal Rate of Return (IRR): the discount rate at which NPV = 0; Payback Period: how quickly the investment is recovered.

In the discovery/concept phase, a project manager creates a current state vs. future state analysis. What is the PRIMARY purpose of this analysis?

Answer: To document the existing situation (current state — what exists today, what problems exist) and the desired situation (future state — what the project will deliver, what problems will be solved) — defining the gap that the project must bridge and establishing the basis for requirements

CURRENT STATE vs. FUTURE STATE ANALYSIS: CURRENT STATE (as-is): documents what EXISTS today; processes, systems, technologies, organizational structures, data flows, pain points and limitations; performance metrics (current throughput, error rates, cycle times); stakeholder frustrations and workarounds; root causes of current problems; FUTURE STATE (to-be): documents what WILL EXIST after the project is delivered; target processes, systems, technologies, organizational changes; target performance metrics (desired throughput, error rates, cycle times); how the project's deliverables close the identified gaps; THE GAP: the difference between current and future state defines the PROJECT SCOPE at a conceptual level — what must change to get from AS-IS to TO-BE; BUSINESS CASE CONNECTION: the gap analysis quantifies the pain of the current state and the value of the future state — supporting the financial justification in the business case. EXAMPLE: Current state: customer support team manually processes 1,000 forms per day with 15% error rate and 3-day SLA. Future state: automated processing of 2,500 forms per day with <1% error rate and 4-hour SLA. Gap = automation system needed.
