Sample Questions

Under ASC 250, a change in accounting principle is generally accounted for using which approach?

Answer: Retrospective approach — all prior periods presented are restated to reflect the new principle, and the cumulative effect on periods not presented is recorded as an adjustment to the opening balance of retained earnings

ASC 250-10-45-5 requires retrospective application when an entity changes an accounting principle. All prior periods presented in the financial statements are revised to reflect the new principle. The cumulative effect on all periods prior to those presented is reported as an adjustment to the opening balance of retained earnings (or net assets, or other applicable equity account) of the earliest period presented. This restated presentation allows users to compare periods on a consistent basis.

A company changes depreciation method from declining balance to straight-line for all existing assets. This is classified as

Answer: A change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle — accounted for prospectively in current and future periods

ASC 250-10-45-17 classifies depreciation method changes (e.g., declining balance to straight-line) as changes in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle. Because depreciation methods inherently involve estimating how an asset's economic benefits are consumed, a method change reflects revised assumptions about the pattern of consumption. These are accounted for prospectively — applied to the current period and future periods — without restating prior periods. The change is disclosed in the notes including the justification that the new method is preferable.

A company discovers in Year 3 that it failed to record $200,000 of accrued wages expense at the end of Year 1 (wages were paid in Year 2). The error is material. Year 1 and Year 2 comparative financial statements are being presented with Year 3. How is this error corrected?

Answer: Restate Year 1 financial statements to include the $200,000 wages expense; adjust the beginning retained earnings balance for Year 2 (if Year 1 is not presented) or restate Year 1 directly; disclose the nature, effect, and reasons for the restatement

ASC 250-10-45-23 requires correction of material errors in previously issued financial statements by restating the affected periods. Since Year 1 is presented comparatively, the Year 1 financial statements are restated: wages expense increases $200,000; wages payable increases $200,000 (or decreases retained earnings at year-end Year 1). Year 2 is also affected — the wages payable would have been paid in Year 2, so Year 2 is corrected to remove any related misstatement. Required disclosures include the nature of the error, the effect on income and equity for each period, and that prior periods have been restated.
