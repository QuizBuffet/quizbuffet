4.0 Simulated Tattoo Procedure and Conclusion Tattoo Practice Quiz

20 exam-style questions covering 23% of the Tattoo exam. Instant feedback on every answer, progress tracking, no signup required.

This domain is part of the Tattoo Artist License practice test. Each question is tagged by exam objective and difficulty so you can drill exactly the areas you need.

Sample Questions

Pigment must be dispensed from the source bottle into a
  • A. single-use ink cap placed on the procedure tray, never directly into the tube or machine
  • B. reusable glass palette that is disinfected between clients
  • C. cup of distilled water to dilute the pigment before use
  • D. the tube reservoir by dripping pigment directly from the bottle

Pigment must be dispensed from the source bottle into individual single-use ink caps placed on the procedure tray; the ink cap holds the pigment for that session only; dispensing into a dedicated ink cap prevents any needle-contaminated material from entering the source bottle, maintains the sterility of the remaining pigment supply, and provides a clear, accessible pool of pigment for the needle to pick up during tattooing.

During the simulated tattoo application section, the power to the machine has been disconnected. This means the candidate demonstrates
  • A. only the positioning of supplies without moving the machine at all
  • B. the correct hand position, machine angle, skin stretching, and procedural movements as if tattooing, without actual needle penetration of the skin
  • C. verbally describing the tattooing technique to the examiner instead of performing any movements
  • D. only the needle dipping motion into the ink cap without approaching the skin

Only demonstrating needle dipping would omit the critical assessment of machine handling, skin stretch technique, machine angle, and movement across the skin, all of which are evaluated during the simulated application section.

When dispensing pigment from the source bottle to an ink cap, the bottle tip must
  • A. touch the ink cap surface to ensure all pigment is transferred without waste
  • B. be wiped with the same glove used during tattooing to remove any residue
  • C. never contact the ink cap or any other surface in the procedure area, the bottle tip must remain clean to prevent contamination of the source supply
  • D. be pre-dipped in the ink cap to warm the pigment before dispensing

The pigment source bottle must be held above the ink cap and dispensed by tilting or squeezing without allowing the bottle tip to contact any surface in the procedure area; maintaining this no-contact protocol preserves the integrity of the remaining pigment for future sessions; the bottle tip must remain uncontaminated throughout the dispensing process.

Stretching the skin taut during tattoo application is necessary because
  • A. it prevents the client from feeling the needle by compressing the sensory nerves
  • B. it increases the surface area of the skin to accommodate larger tattoo designs
  • C. unstretched skin folds and bunches under the needle, producing uneven, jagged lines, a taut surface allows the needle to penetrate consistently at the correct depth and produce clean, even lines
  • D. it stimulates collagen production that improves tattoo healing outcomes

Skin is an elastic organ that moves and bunches when a needle contacts it without tension; an unstretched skin surface undulates under the needle, causing the needle to penetrate at varying depths and angles, producing ragged, inconsistent lines; stretching the skin taut with the non-dominant hand (or a stretching grip) creates a flat, firm surface that allows the needle to penetrate consistently at the calibrated depth and track smoothly along the intended line, producing clean, precise work.

Irrigation bottles used during tattooing must be
  • A. filled with tap water at the studio sink immediately before each use
  • B. shared between multiple artists in a studio for efficiency
  • C. reused across multiple clients without any labeling requirements
  • D. labeled in English, contain appropriate liquid (distilled water or approved solution), and protected from contamination during the procedure with a barrier over the exterior

Irrigation bottles at the NIC examination must be labeled in English; they should contain distilled water or an approved irrigation solution; the bottle exterior that is handled during the procedure must be covered with a barrier (plastic wrap or sleeve) to prevent blood and ink contamination from gloved hands from contaminating the bottle surface throughout the session; the barrier is changed between clients along with all other barriers.

Petroleum jelly (Vaseline) or tattoo ointment applied to the skin during the tattooing procedure serves to
  • A. permanently seal the tattoo ink into the dermis once the needle deposits it
  • B. lubricate the skin surface to reduce needle friction, protect the stencil from premature removal, and keep the work area moist for better visibility of the tattoo during application
  • C. anesthetize the skin to eliminate pain during tattooing
  • D. act as an antiseptic barrier that sterilizes the skin between needle passes

Petroleum jelly or specialized tattoo ointment is applied sparingly to the skin over the stencil area before tattooing begins; it lubricates the skin surface so the needle glides smoothly rather than dragging and catching on the skin, reducing skin trauma and discomfort; it also helps preserve the stencil by providing a barrier between the stencil ink and the rubbing action of wiping during tattooing; and it keeps the skin surface moist, making the tattoo lines more visible against the glistening skin as the artist works.

The correct needle depth for tattooing implants ink into the
  • A. stratum corneum (outermost epidermal layer) for maximum color retention
  • B. subcutaneous fat layer for permanent anchoring of the pigment
  • C. dermis, the layer between the epidermis and subcutaneous tissue, where pigment is encapsulated by fibroblasts and macrophages for long-term retention
  • D. epidermis only, where cellular turnover makes the tattoo gradually fade to a natural finish

The dermis (specifically the papillary and upper reticular dermis) is the target layer for tattoo pigment; dermal cells (primarily fibroblasts) do not shed the way epidermal cells do; pigment particles deposited in the dermis are encapsulated by macrophages, some transported to lymph nodes, others remaining permanently encapsulated in the dermal tissue; this biological response produces the long-term retention of tattoo pigment.

Tongue depressors used to dispense petroleum jelly from a shared container must be
  • A. rinsed with water between dips to remove skin residue before returning to the container
  • B. shared between clients to reduce supply waste
  • C. soaked in the petroleum jelly container between uses during the same session
  • D. single-use, a fresh, unused tongue depressor is used each time product is dispensed; never double-dipped into the shared container

Any implement used to dispense from a shared container must be single-use; a fresh, uncontaminated tongue depressor is used to remove the required amount of petroleum jelly from the container, and then that depressor is discarded, it is never returned to the container; this prevents client skin cells, blood, or microorganisms from the current client from entering the shared supply that will be used for future clients.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Aftercare: Instructions given to the client at conclusion of the tattoo procedure covering wound care, moisturizer application, sun avoidance, and activity restrictions during the healing period.
  • Foot Switch: Pedal-operated power control for the tattoo machine; classified as a contaminated item during the NIC practical exam and must be stored separately from clean supplies, then disinfected for transport between clients.
  • Granuloma: Chronic immune response to tattoo pigment in which macrophages unable to clear pigment particles form nodular aggregates; appears as raised, firm tissue at the tattoo site, often months to years post-procedure.
  • Work Area Setup: First domain of the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination: candidate prepares themselves, disinfects all work surfaces, sets up supplies and equipment, designates the universal supply area, and disposes of contaminated supplies, all within 20 minutes.
  • Infection Control: Practices that prevent the transmission of pathogens, including hand hygiene, surface disinfection, barrier use, and single-use supplies, required throughout the tattooing process.
  • Epidermis: Outermost layer of the skin, composed of five sub-layers (strata); tattoo pigment is not intended to remain here as epidermal cells shed, causing fast fading.
  • Dermis: Middle layer of the skin divided into papillary dermis (upper, target depth for tattoo pigment) and reticular dermis (deeper, causes blue-gray color shift if pigment is placed there).
  • Papillary Dermis: Target implantation depth for tattoo pigment; composed of loose connective tissue and capillary loops that hold pigment particles stably without the lateral migration seen at deeper levels.
  • Stencil Application: Transfer of a prepared design (approximately 2×3 inches for NIC practical) to the pre-shaved, clean forearm using stencil solution; the outline guides needle placement during the procedure.
  • Tattoo Machine: Electromechanical device that drives a needle bar up and down to deposit pigment into the dermis; types include coil machines (electromagnetic) and rotary machines (motor-driven).
