Sample Questions

A remote pilot wants to develop an effective visual scanning technique for detecting manned aircraft during sUAS operations. Which scanning approach is most effective for detecting traffic in the visual environment?

Answer: Use a systematic block scanning technique — move the eyes in a series of short, deliberate shifts to successive 10-to-15 degree sectors, pausing briefly in each sector to allow the eye to focus

The most effective visual scanning technique for detecting traffic uses a systematic block scan with short, deliberate eye movements of 10–15 degrees and a brief pause (about 1 second) in each sector; this allows the eye to focus and process information in each area before moving to the next, maximizing the probability of detecting conflicting traffic.

A remote pilot is conducting an extended sUAS operation inside a partially enclosed parking structure where a gasoline-powered generator is running nearby. After 45 minutes, the pilot develops a headache, feels mildly confused, and finds it harder to concentrate on the flight. Which hazardous substance is most likely responsible for these symptoms?

Answer: Carbon monoxide (CO) from the gasoline generator, which displaces oxygen in the blood's hemoglobin and impairs brain function even at low concentrations

Carbon monoxide is an odorless, colorless gas produced by incomplete combustion in gasoline engines; CO binds to hemoglobin approximately 200 times more readily than oxygen, preventing oxygen delivery to the brain; early symptoms include headache, confusion, difficulty concentrating, and impaired judgment — all consistent with the described scenario; the pilot must immediately move to fresh air.

A remote pilot is conducting a 3-hour sUAS agricultural survey in 95°F (35°C) heat with direct sun exposure. The pilot has consumed only a single 12-ounce bottle of water during the entire operation. Near the end of the mission, the pilot makes several inaccurate altitude inputs and fails to notice an aircraft approaching from the south. What physiological condition most likely explains this performance degradation?

Answer: Dehydration — fluid loss from heat and sweating reduces blood volume and impairs brain function, reducing attention, reaction time, and decision-making quality

Dehydration reduces blood volume and impairs thermoregulation; even mild dehydration (1–2% of body weight) measurably degrades cognitive performance including attention, reaction time, short-term memory, and decision-making quality — all critical for safe sUAS operations in hot conditions; the pilot must maintain adequate hydration throughout extended outdoor operations.
