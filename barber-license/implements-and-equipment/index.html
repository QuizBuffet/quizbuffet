Sample Questions

The barber chair's headrest MUST be disinfected and covered before each client because
  • A. it directly contacts the client's neck and head, making it a surface that must be prepared to prevent cross-contamination
  • B. state boards require chairs to be repainted between every client
  • C. leather chairs are required to be steam-cleaned daily
  • D. the headrest motor requires lubrication before each use

The barber chair headrest contacts the client's neck, scalp, and potentially hair and skin during every seated service; as a surface that touches multiple clients throughout the day, it must be disinfected with an EPA-registered product between clients and then covered with a clean head-rest paper or fresh protective covering to present a sanitary surface for each new client and prevent cross-contamination.

The straight razor used in a barbershop must be
  • A. reused on multiple clients after wiping with a dry cloth
  • B. soaked in disinfectant for 5 minutes then reused immediately
  • C. stored in a UV cabinet only, with no additional disinfection needed
  • D. a single-use disposable blade or, if reusable, must be properly cleaned and disinfected between each client

Straight razors used in barbershop services contact the client's skin directly and may produce minor cuts and bleeding; for infection control, disposable single-use razor blades are the professional standard because they eliminate the risk of cross-contamination between clients entirely; if reusable straight razors (with replaceable blades) are used, the blade must be changed for each client, and the handle must be thoroughly cleaned and disinfected with an EPA-registered disinfectant between clients.

Between clients, work surfaces at the barbershop workstation must be
  • A. rearranged for a fresh appearance
  • B. left as-is until the end of the day when a full cleaning is performed
  • C. wiped down with an EPA-registered disinfectant after removing all hair clippings and product residue
  • D. sprayed with perfumed air freshener to eliminate odors

Between clients, the workstation surface must be cleared of hair clippings and debris (cleaning step) and then wiped with an EPA-registered disinfectant at the correct dilution for the full contact time specified on the label; this two-step clean-then-disinfect process is required by state board regulations to maintain a sanitary work environment between clients.

Shear tension that is too loose causes the shears to
  • A. cut hair faster and more efficiently
  • B. fold or push the hair rather than cutting cleanly through it
  • C. produce more heat from friction between the blades
  • D. automatically sterilize between uses from blade contact

When shear pivot tension is too loose, the blades flex apart during the cutting stroke instead of pressing evenly against each other along their full length; this causes the blades to push or fold the hair between them rather than shearing cleanly through it, producing ragged, uneven cuts and client discomfort; proper tension allows the blades to glide together with controlled resistance while maintaining blade contact throughout the stroke.

Towels used in barbershop services must be
  • A. reused throughout the day on multiple clients without washing
  • B. professionally laundered and sanitized before use on each client, stored in a clean, covered container between uses
  • C. replaced with new towels only when visibly soiled
  • D. air-dried on hooks between clients rather than laundered

Towels used in barbershop services (for hot towel services, neck wipes, face towels) must be freshly laundered (washed in hot water with detergent and dried at high heat) between uses on different clients; clean laundered towels must be stored in a clean, closed container (not piled loosely on an open shelf) to prevent recontamination before use; used towels must be placed in a separate, clearly designated soiled linen container.

Proper adjustment of the barber chair height before a haircut service serves to
  • A. impress the client with the chair's mechanical capabilities
  • B. allow the barber to work with relaxed shoulders and arms at the correct ergonomic height, reducing fatigue and musculoskeletal strain over the course of the workday
  • C. position the client closer to the ceiling lights for better visibility
  • D. prevent the client from moving during the service

Adjusting the barber chair so the client's head is at the barber's optimal working height allows the barber to maintain a neutral posture with relaxed shoulders, slightly bent elbows, and arms close to the body; this ergonomic positioning reduces the sustained muscle loading on the shoulders, neck, and upper back that causes cumulative musculoskeletal injuries (a significant occupational health concern for barbers who perform hundreds of services per month); working at the wrong height forces constant elevation of the arms or excessive bending that accelerates fatigue.

A barber's comb with fine teeth on one end and wide teeth on the other is designed to
  • A. use the fine-tooth section for detangling and the wide-tooth for finishing, allowing a single comb to perform multiple functions based on the area being worked
  • B. double as a ruler for measuring hair length
  • C. hold product on the fine-tooth end and distribute with the wide-tooth end
  • D. be used only on one hair type with the appropriate end selected by a randomized system

The barber's all-purpose comb (also called a styling comb or barbering comb) typically has fine teeth at one end and wider-spaced teeth at the other end; the fine-tooth section is used for close work, detailed finishing, parting, and working with finer hair, while the wide-tooth section is used for initial detangling, working through thicker or curlier hair, and distributing products; the two sections allow a single implement to perform multiple functions during a service without the barber needing to reach for a different tool.

All French style shears
  • A. are made in France
  • B. are cobalt metal
  • C. have tight pivots
  • D. have a finger brace

French style shears (French shears) are characterized by having a finger brace (also called a finger rest or tang), a small hook or extension on one of the finger rings that the little finger rests against during cutting; this brace provides stability and control during cutting strokes; it is the defining design feature that distinguishes French style from other shear styles regardless of where the shears are manufactured or what metal they are made from.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Implements: Hand tools the barber operates: razors (straight, changeable-blade), shears (French/German), thinning shears, combs, brushes. Cleaned and disinfected between clients.
  • Equipment: Larger fixtures: barber chair, hydraulic chair, workstation, shampoo bowl, sterilizer, towel warmer. Surfaces disinfected daily.
  • Draping: Chemical drape (water/chemical-resistant) for color and chemical services. Shampoo drape (water-resistant). Cutting cape (cloth/nylon). Neck strip or towel between cape and skin.
  • Muscles of Head and Neck: Frontalis, orbicularis oculi/oris, masseter, sternocleidomastoid, trapezius. Massage strokes move from insertion toward origin to avoid damage.
  • Electrical Tools: Clippers, trimmers, blow dryers, curling irons, flat irons. Blades detachable for disinfection; cords inspected for fraying; UL/ETL listed.
  • Haircutting Principles: Sectioning, elevation (0-180°), finger angle, guide (stationary vs traveling), tension. Shape determined by cutting line and elevation. Outlining defines the perimeter; neck shave finishes.
  • Facial Services: Cleansing, exfoliation, steam towel, massage, mask, moisturizer. Steam towels open follicles and soften hair before shaving; cool towels close pores after.
