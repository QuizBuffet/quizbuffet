Sample Questions

Under current federal tax law applicable to C corporations, what are the carryover rules for a net operating loss (NOL) generated in a tax year beginning after December 31, 2017?

Answer: NOLs may only be carried forward indefinitely, with the deduction in any carryover year limited to 80% of the corporation's taxable income (computed before the NOL deduction) for that year

Under TCJA §13302, for NOLs arising in taxable years beginning after December 31, 2017: (1) Carryback: generally eliminated for C corporations (exceptions remain for farming losses, which retain a 2-year carryback); (2) Carryforward: indefinite, NOLs do not expire; (3) Annual deduction limitation: 80% of the corporation's taxable income (computed without regard to the NOL deduction) for each carryover year. These changes replaced the prior-law rules that allowed 2-year carryback and 20-year carryforward with no percentage limitation on the annual deduction amount.

Under IRC §351, a taxpayer contributes property with a fair market value of $90,000 and an adjusted basis of $30,000 to a newly formed corporation in exchange for 100% of the stock. No other consideration is received. What is the tax treatment for the contributing taxpayer?

Answer: The taxpayer recognizes no gain or loss; the taxpayer's basis in the stock received equals $30,000 (substituted basis equal to the adjusted basis of the property contributed)

Under §351(a), no gain or loss is recognized when property is transferred to a corporation solely in exchange for stock if the transferors are in control (≥80%) immediately after the exchange. The taxpayer's basis in the stock received equals the adjusted basis of the property contributed ($30,000) under §358, a substituted (carryover) basis. The $60,000 of built-in gain is deferred, not eliminated; it will be recognized when the stock is later sold. The corporation's basis in the received property is also a carryover basis of $30,000 under §362.

Which ownership threshold must be met for a subsidiary corporation to be eligible to join a consolidated federal tax return with its parent?

Answer: The parent must own at least 80% of the subsidiary's total voting power and at least 80% of the total value of all outstanding stock

Under IRC §1504(a), an affiliated group eligible to file a consolidated federal income tax return consists of an includible corporation that owns directly stock possessing: (1) at least 80% of the total combined voting power of all classes of stock entitled to vote, AND (2) at least 80% of the total value of all outstanding stock of each includible corporation. Both thresholds (80% vote AND 80% value) must be met simultaneously. This 80% test applies through the ownership chain, a parent owning 80%+ of a subsidiary, which owns 80%+ of a second-tier subsidiary, brings all three corporations into the affiliated group.
