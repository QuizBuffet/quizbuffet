Sample Questions

A remote pilot wants to fly an sUAS over a group of non-participant bystanders at an outdoor event without obtaining a waiver. The pilot believes the aircraft may qualify as Category 1. Under 14 CFR Part 107, what maximum weight must the sUAS meet to qualify for a Category 1 operation over people?
  • A. The sUAS must weigh 0.55 pounds or less, including everything onboard at the time of operation
  • B. The sUAS must weigh less than 2 pounds total including all components and any attached payload
  • C. The sUAS must weigh 1 pound or less when measured without any detachable payload attached
  • D. The sUAS must weigh less than 55 pounds and have an FAA-accepted Declaration of Compliance

Under 14 CFR 107.115, a Category 1 operation is authorized when the sUAS weighs 0.55 pounds or less, including everything onboard at the time of the operation.

A remote pilot holds a Declaration of Compliance confirming the sUAS meets Category 3 requirements. The pilot is asked to fly over a crowd attending an outdoor music festival. Under 14 CFR Part 107, may a Category 3 sUAS conduct this operation over the open-air assembly?
  • A. Yes, if the remote pilot reduces airspeed to the minimum required for stable flight while passing over the festival crowd
  • B. No, Category 3 operations may not be conducted over an open-air assembly of human beings under any circumstance
  • C. Yes, provided each section of the crowd has a dedicated visual observer to monitor for hazards from below
  • D. Yes, as long as the sUAS altitude remains at least 400 feet above the highest person in the assembly

Under 14 CFR 107.125, a Category 3 operation may not be conducted over an open-air assembly of human beings; this prohibition applies regardless of speed, altitude, or the presence of visual observers.

A lightweight sUAS weighs 0.40 pounds and easily satisfies the Category 1 weight threshold. Before flying it over workers at a construction site, the remote pilot must confirm one additional design requirement. Under 14 CFR Part 107, what design characteristic must a Category 1 sUAS also satisfy?
  • A. The sUAS must be equipped with propeller guards that are FAA-certified to withstand full-throttle blade impact
  • B. The sUAS must not contain any exposed rotating parts that would lacerate human skin upon contact with a person
  • C. The sUAS must have an automatic landing function that activates if the command signal is lost during over-people flight
  • D. The sUAS must have anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles at the time of any over-people operation

Under 14 CFR 107.115, a Category 1 sUAS must not contain any exposed rotating parts that would lacerate human skin upon contact with a person; this is the second requirement alongside the 0.55-pound weight limit.

A remote pilot with a Category 3 Declaration of Compliance wants to fly over workers at an industrial facility. The facility is an enclosed yard with a locked perimeter fence; access is restricted to credentialed employees who have been briefed that sUAS operations occur on site. Under 14 CFR Part 107, does this scenario permit Category 3 flight over the workers?
  • A. Yes, because the operation takes place in a controlled or restricted-access site where all persons present are on notice that sUAS operations may occur
  • B. Yes, but only if each worker signs a written liability waiver acknowledging the risks before the operation begins
  • C. No, Category 3 operations over workers always require a certificate of waiver regardless of site access controls
  • D. No, Category 3 may only be conducted in Class G airspace below 200 ft AGL regardless of site configuration

Under 14 CFR 107.125, a Category 3 operation may be conducted if the operation is within a controlled or restricted-access site where all human beings on the ground are on notice that sUAS operations may occur; a credentialed, briefed, fenced facility meets this criterion.

Using a Category 3 sUAS with a valid Declaration of Compliance, a remote pilot wants to fly sustained over moving delivery trucks making rounds through a mixed residential and commercial neighborhood. The neighborhood is open to the public and not a controlled or restricted-access area. Under Part 107, is this Category 3 flight over the moving trucks permitted?
  • A. No, Category 3 sUAS may only conduct sustained flight over moving vehicles within a controlled or restricted-access site where all persons are on notice of sUAS operations
  • B. Yes, because moving vehicles provide a physical barrier between people and the sUAS, reducing injury risk below the Category 3 threshold
  • C. Yes, provided the sUAS speed does not exceed the maximum speed of the trucks being followed during the operation
  • D. No, but the pilot may obtain a certificate of waiver specifically for sustained flight over moving vehicles in residential areas

Under 14 CFR 107.145 and 107.125, Category 3 operations over moving vehicles require the flight to take place in a controlled or restricted-access site where all persons are on notice; an open public neighborhood does not qualify.

A remote pilot plans a Category 2 sUAS operation over non-participant workers conducting exterior building maintenance. The sUAS meets the injury severity and design requirements of Category 2. Under 14 CFR Part 107, what document must the remote PIC have in their possession or readily accessible during the operation?
  • A. A Part 107 certificate of waiver from the FAA specifically authorizing operations over non-participant human beings
  • B. A letter of authorization from the local FSDO confirming Category 2 eligibility for the specific aircraft being flown
  • C. A Declaration of Compliance indicating the sUAS satisfies the Category 2 injury severity and design standards
  • D. An FAA-issued type certificate or supplemental type certificate for the sUAS being used in the over-people operation

Under 14 CFR 107.120, a Category 2 operation requires the remote PIC to have a Declaration of Compliance, documenting that the sUAS meets the FAA-accepted injury severity limit and design requirements, in their possession or readily accessible.

An sUAS manufacturer wants to offer a drone that remote pilots can operate over people under Category 4, allowing greater operational flexibility than Categories 1 through 3. What is the primary regulatory requirement that sets Category 4 apart from the other categories?
  • A. Category 4 requires a Declaration of Compliance with a higher injury severity threshold than Category 3
  • B. The sUAS must have an airworthiness certificate issued under 14 CFR Part 21
  • C. Category 4 requires the remote pilot to hold a commercial remote pilot certificate with an over-people endorsement
  • D. The sUAS must demonstrate at least 500 hours of accident-free flight testing before Category 4 certification

Under 14 CFR 107.130, a Category 4 operation requires the sUAS to have an airworthiness certificate issued under 14 CFR Part 21, such as a standard airworthiness certificate or a special airworthiness certificate.

A drone manufacturer is designing an sUAS to qualify for Category 2 operations over people. The engineering team asks how the FAA evaluates whether the aircraft meets the Category 2 injury severity requirement. Under 14 CFR Part 107, the Category 2 injury severity standard is based on what?
  • A. The sUAS must produce a noise level below 65 decibels at 25 feet, which correlates with reduced impact hazard to persons
  • B. The sUAS must be rated by an independent safety laboratory as posing no more than a 1-in-1,000,000 risk of serious injury
  • C. The sUAS must weigh less than 4 pounds and travel no faster than 45 knots during any over-people segment
  • D. The sUAS must not exceed the FAA-accepted injury severity limit, which manufacturers demonstrate through a means of compliance accepted by the FAA and documented in a Declaration of Compliance

Under 14 CFR 107.120, a Category 2 sUAS must not exceed the FAA-accepted limit for injury severity; manufacturers demonstrate compliance through an FAA-accepted means of compliance and record this in a Declaration of Compliance submitted to the FAA.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Operation over moving vehicles: Allowed by category if the operation meets the corresponding people-operations category and the vehicles are within a closed/restricted-access site or covered
  • Operations over people §107.39 / Subpart D: Categorized 1-4 by injury risk; Category 1 (≤ 0.55 lb, no exposed rotating parts) allowed over people without waiver
  • Waivers §107.200: Many operating rules (e.g., daylight, VLOS, over people, max altitude, BVLOS, multiple aircraft) may be waived if applicant shows equivalent safety
  • Part 107 applicability: Applies to commercial / non-recreational sUAS operations under 55 lb (25 kg); recreational flyers fly under §44809 (TRUST), not Part 107
  • Daylight operation §107.29: Operations permitted day, civil twilight, and night; night ops require anti-collision lighting visible for ≥3 SM
  • Anti-collision lighting: Required for civil twilight and night operations under §107.29; must be visible for at least 3 statute miles
  • Carriage of property §107.36 / Subpart C: Allowed if total weight (aircraft + payload) < 55 lb, securely attached, no hazmat, intrastate only, and does not adversely affect handling
  • Class A airspace: 18,000 ft MSL up to and including FL600; sUAS operations not allowed under Part 107 (would require waiver)
  • Class E airspace: Controlled airspace not A/B/C/D; surface E shown by dashed magenta; ATC authorization required for sUAS at the surface
  • Class G airspace: Uncontrolled airspace; ATC authorization NOT required for Part 107 ops; still subject to op limits and right of way
