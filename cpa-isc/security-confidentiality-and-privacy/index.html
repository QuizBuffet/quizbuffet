Sample Questions

Under HIPAA, which entities are classified as 'covered entities' and are directly subject to the HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules?

Answer: Health plans, health care clearinghouses, and health care providers that transmit health information electronically — these three categories directly handle protected health information as part of their core functions

HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996) defines three categories of covered entities: (1) Health plans: companies that provide or pay for medical care — health insurance companies, HMOs, Medicare/Medicaid programs, employer-sponsored health plans; (2) Health care clearinghouses: entities that process non-standard health information into standard electronic formats — medical billing services; (3) Health care providers who transmit health information electronically: physicians, hospitals, pharmacies, nursing homes, and other providers that submit electronic claims or conduct other electronic health transactions. Business Associates (third parties who handle PHI on behalf of covered entities — such as cloud storage providers, billing companies, IT service providers) are also subject to HIPAA under the Business Associate Agreement requirement, though they are a separate category from covered entities.

The GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) establishes six principles relating to the processing of personal data. Which principle requires that personal data be collected only for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes?

Answer: Purpose limitation — personal data shall be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a way incompatible with those purposes

Purpose limitation (GDPR Article 5(1)(b)) is one of the six data processing principles: personal data may be collected only for specific, clearly defined purposes that are communicated to data subjects. Once collected, the data cannot be repurposed for uses incompatible with the original collection purpose without additional legal basis. Example: a company that collects email addresses for order confirmation cannot then use those addresses for marketing campaigns without a separate legal basis (such as consent). Exception: further processing for archiving in the public interest, scientific/historical research, or statistical purposes may be compatible under specific conditions.

PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) was developed by the major payment card brands to protect cardholder data. Which organizations are required to comply with PCI DSS?

Answer: Any entity that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data — including merchants, payment processors, banks, and service providers involved in payment card transactions

PCI DSS v4.0 applies to all entities that store, process, or transmit cardholder data, including: (1) Merchants: any business that accepts payment cards for goods or services; (2) Service providers: companies that process, store, or transmit cardholder data on behalf of others (payment gateways, managed service providers, web hosts); (3) Acquiring banks: banks that process card payments on behalf of merchants; (4) Issuing banks: banks that issue credit/debit cards; (5) Payment processors: companies that route transaction authorization and settlement. The compliance level (Level 1 through 4 for merchants) is determined by transaction volume — but all merchants are subject to PCI DSS regardless of size. Small merchants may use simpler Self-Assessment Questionnaires (SAQs) rather than full QSA audits.
