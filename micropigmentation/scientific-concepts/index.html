Sample Questions

Bacteria that require oxygen to survive are classified as
  • A. aerobic
  • B. anaerobic
  • C. pathogenic
  • D. saprophytic

Aerobic bacteria require oxygen to grow and reproduce; their presence in wound environments is relevant to micropigmentation because oxygen-rich surface tissues support aerobic species such as Staphylococcus aureus.

The virus of greatest bloodborne transmission concern during micropigmentation procedures is
  • A. rhinovirus
  • B. influenza A
  • C. hepatitis B
  • D. varicella-zoster

Hepatitis B virus (HBV) is the bloodborne pathogen of greatest concern in needle-based procedures; it survives on dry surfaces for up to seven days, is 50-100 times more infectious than HIV, and can be transmitted through microscopic amounts of blood on contaminated needles or surfaces.

The bacterium most commonly associated with post-procedure wound infections in micropigmentation is
  • A. Escherichia coli
  • B. Mycobacterium tuberculosis
  • C. Staphylococcus aureus
  • D. Clostridium tetani

Staphylococcus aureus is the most common gram-positive bacterium responsible for post-procedure skin and soft tissue infections; it colonizes the skin surface and can enter the dermis through needle punctures, making it the leading bacterial concern in micropigmentation wound care.

A micropigmentation client with a history of cold sores requests a lip procedure. The technician should
  • A. require physician clearance and antiviral pre-treatment before performing the lip procedure
  • B. proceed normally because lip pigment deactivates herpes simplex
  • C. treat only the outer vermillion border, avoiding the mucosa
  • D. decline all future services permanently

Herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) remains latent in the trigeminal ganglion and can be reactivated by the trauma of a lip micropigmentation procedure; requiring physician clearance and prophylactic antiviral medication (typically oral acyclovir or valacyclovir begun 48-72 hours before the procedure) is the standard professional protocol to prevent a potentially severe outbreak that can damage the healing result.

A technician observes a client's eyebrow area with circular, scaly, slightly raised patches with clear centers prior to a procedure. This presentation suggests
  • A. seborrheic dermatitis
  • B. rosacea
  • C. psoriasis
  • D. tinea corporis

Tinea corporis (ringworm of the body) is a dermatophyte fungal infection presenting as circular, scaly, erythematous plaques with a raised advancing border and central clearing as the fungus moves outward; its presence in the eyebrow area is a contraindication for micropigmentation and requires medical treatment before any procedure.

A client's healing eyebrow procedure develops a honey-colored crust with surrounding redness two weeks post-treatment. This presentation is most consistent with
  • A. normal eschar formation
  • B. bacterial impetigo
  • C. allergic contact dermatitis
  • D. herpes simplex reactivation

Honey-colored crusting with surrounding erythema is the hallmark presentation of impetigo, a superficial bacterial skin infection caused by Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus pyogenes; its appearance two weeks post-procedure indicates bacterial colonization of the healing wound requiring medical referral.

HIV is classified as a retrovirus because it
  • A. replicates only on intact skin surfaces
  • B. is destroyed within seconds by soap and water
  • C. produces toxins that destroy red blood cells
  • D. uses reverse transcriptase to convert its RNA into DNA within the host cell

HIV is classified as a retrovirus because it carries its genetic material as RNA and uses the enzyme reverse transcriptase to convert that RNA into DNA, which is then integrated into the host cell's genome, a replication strategy that distinguishes retroviruses from conventional viruses and has implications for the persistence of infection and antiviral drug development.

Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) is a concern in micropigmentation because it
  • A. resists first-line antibiotics and spreads through direct skin contact and contaminated surfaces
  • B. is destroyed by standard EPA-registered disinfectants
  • C. forms spores that survive autoclaving
  • D. is transmitted only through respiratory droplets

MRSA's clinical significance in micropigmentation is its resistance to beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillins and cephalosporins), making skin infections difficult to treat; it spreads via direct contact with infected skin or contaminated implements, making rigorous single-use implement protocols and surface disinfection critical in the micropigmentation studio.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Bloodborne Pathogen Standard: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030: exposure control plan, single-use supplies, sharps disposal, post-exposure protocol, hepatitis B vaccination offered to all at-risk employees.
  • Levels of Infection Control: Three levels: cleaning (soap + water), disinfection (EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectant), and sterilization (autoclave for reusable instruments).
  • Universal Precautions: Treat all blood and bodily fluids as if infectious. Always wear gloves; use barrier film on equipment; dispose of needles in a sharps container.
  • Skin Structure: Three layers: epidermis (outermost, 5 sublayers), dermis (where pigment is implanted, collagen, elastin, capillaries, nerves), subcutaneous (fat layer).
  • Allergic Reaction: Red, itchy, swollen tissue at the pigment site. Usually triggered by red iron-oxide pigments. Patch testing is recommended for high-risk clients.
  • Pigment Implantation Depth: Target the upper papillary dermis for crisp, long-lasting color. Too shallow (epidermis) → fades fast; too deep (reticular dermis) → blue/gray shift and migration.
  • Mucosal Tissue: Lip and eye margins; thinner and more vascular than facial skin, requires different needle depth, slower passes, and specific pre-procedure prep.
  • Color Theory: Skin undertone (warm/neutral/cool) determines pigment selection. Complementary colors neutralize unwanted hues (e.g., green neutralizes red lip tones).
  • Titanium Dioxide: Opaque white pigment used to lighten or correct pigments. Heavy use can cause unnatural opacity; healed result appears chalky if overused.
  • Organic vs. Inorganic Pigment: Organic (carbon-based): brighter, more vivid colors, may fade faster. Inorganic (mineral oxides): more stable, may shift color (warm/orange) over time.
