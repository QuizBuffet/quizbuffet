Sample Questions

Which type of agency authority arises when a third party reasonably believes an agent has authority because the principal's words or conduct created that impression, even though the agent has no actual authority?

Answer: Apparent authority — authority that exists because the principal's conduct led the third party to reasonably believe the agent was authorized

Apparent authority (also called ostensible authority) arises from the principal's manifestations to THIRD PARTIES that lead those parties to reasonably believe the agent has authority. Key elements: (1) The principal must make some representation (words, conduct, past dealings) that creates the appearance of authority; (2) The third party must reasonably rely on that representation; (3) The agent may not actually have authority. Example: a company consistently allows its sales representative to sign contracts — even if the representative's authority has been revoked, apparent authority may continue for third parties who knew of the prior pattern and received no notice of revocation. Apparent authority binds the principal to the third party even though the agent acted without actual authority.

Under common law contract principles, which of the following must be present for a valid contract to be formed?

Answer: An offer, acceptance, consideration, contractual capacity, and legality of subject matter — all five elements must be present

A valid common law contract requires ALL FIVE elements: (1) OFFER: a definite proposal by the offeror communicated to the offeree expressing intent to be bound upon acceptance; must be definite (identify parties, subject matter, price, quantity, time); (2) ACCEPTANCE: the offeree's unequivocal agreement to all terms of the offer (mirror image rule under common law); must be communicated to the offeror; (3) CONSIDERATION: something of legal value given in exchange (money, goods, services, forbearance from legal right); must be bargained-for; past consideration is not valid; (4) CONTRACTUAL CAPACITY: parties must have legal capacity to contract — minors (under 18) can generally disaffirm contracts; incompetents and intoxicated persons may have voidable contracts; (5) LEGALITY OF SUBJECT MATTER: the contract's purpose must be legal — contracts for illegal activities are void.

A contract provides that 'this contract is conditioned upon the buyer obtaining mortgage financing within 30 days.' The buyer fails to obtain financing within 30 days. Which type of condition is this, and what is the legal effect?

Answer: This is a condition precedent — the condition must occur (obtaining financing) before the buyer's duty to purchase arises; if the condition fails, neither party has a duty to perform and the contract is discharged

A condition precedent is an event that must occur before a contractual duty arises. Here, the buyer's duty to purchase the property does not arise unless and until the buyer obtains mortgage financing within 30 days. If the financing condition fails: (1) The buyer has no duty to purchase (the condition precedent did not occur); (2) The seller has no duty to sell (neither party's duty ripened); (3) The contract is discharged — neither party is in breach; (4) Any earnest money deposited may be returned to the buyer. Financing contingencies are common in real estate contracts — they protect buyers from being bound to purchase when financing cannot be obtained.
