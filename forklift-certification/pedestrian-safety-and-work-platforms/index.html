Why are forklifts around pedestrians a serious concern?
  • A. Pedestrians can be struck, crushed, or pinned by moving forklifts or loads
  • B. Pedestrians always have the same visibility as forklift operators
  • C. Forklifts cannot injure pedestrians at low speed
  • D. Pedestrian hazards disappear when the forklift is unloaded

The workbook notes that many forklift-related incidents involve pedestrians, so separation and awareness are critical. This is the correct answer.

On site, why are forklifts around pedestrians a serious concern?
  • A. On site, pedestrians can be struck, crushed, or pinned by moving forklifts or loads
  • B. On site, forklifts cannot injure pedestrians at low speed
  • C. On site, pedestrian hazards disappear when the forklift is unloaded
  • D. On site, pedestrians always have the same visibility as forklift operators

The workbook notes that many forklift-related incidents involve pedestrians, so separation and awareness are critical. This is the correct answer.

What is a best practice for protecting pedestrians from forklift traffic?
  • A. Rely only on pedestrians hearing the forklift
  • B. Establish clear, separate pathways for pedestrians and forklifts
  • C. Allow shortcuts through loading areas during busy periods
  • D. Let pedestrians choose any aisle when forklifts are operating

Separate pathways reduce the chance that pedestrians enter forklift travel routes. This is the correct answer.

  • B. On site, establish clear, separate pathways for pedestrians and forklifts
  • C. On site, let pedestrians choose any aisle when forklifts are operating

Separate pathways reduce the chance that pedestrians enter forklift travel routes. This is the correct answer.

What control can help isolate workstations from aisles traveled by forklifts?

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Pedestrian incidents: Most forklift-related incidents involve pedestrians; controls must address visibility, separation, and communication
  • Traveling with a load: Communicate with pedestrians, no riders, safe speed, follow distance of multiple forklift lengths, watch surface, slight back-tilt, sweeping turns, sound horn at corners and blind areas, lift/lower only when stopped
  • Blind spots: Areas the operator cannot see directly; minimize with mirrors, signage, and route planning
  • Audible warning (horn): Used at corners, intersections, blind areas, and any location where pedestrians may not see the truck
  • Workplace-related topics: Surface conditions, load composition, manipulation/stacking, pedestrian traffic, narrow aisles, hazardous (classified) locations, ramps, ventilation/CO buildup
  • Operator restraint use: Operators must use restraints when exposed to overturn hazards or areas where they could be thrown from the compartment; OSHA can cite for non-use
  • Hazard evaluation: Considers speed, docks, ramps, traffic, surface condition, tight areas, speed bumps, debris, tire condition, railroad tracks, potholes, slick surfaces, and combined CG
  • High-visibility apparel: ANSI/ISEA 107 vests required for workers walking outside designated walkways
  • Operator stays with truck: The forklift operator must remain at the controls the entire time workers are on the platform
  • Mobile elevating work platform (MEWP) preferred: When feasible, use equipment specifically designed to lift people (scissor lift, boom lift) instead of a forklift
