Sample Questions

What is the purpose of a forklift seat belt or operator restraint system?
  • A. To help keep the operator in the seat during a tip-over or when thrown-from-truck hazards exist
  • B. To increase the forklift's rated lifting capacity
  • C. To replace safe speed and surface controls
  • D. To allow passengers to ride on the forklift

Seat belts and restraints help prevent the operator from being thrown from the seat during tip-over or similar hazards. This is the correct answer.

On site, what is the purpose of a forklift seat belt or operator restraint system?
  • A. On site, to help keep the operator in the seat during a tip-over or when thrown-from-truck hazards exist
  • B. On site, to replace safe speed and surface controls
  • C. On site, to allow passengers to ride on the forklift
  • D. On site, to increase the forklift's rated lifting capacity

Seat belts and restraints help prevent the operator from being thrown from the seat during tip-over or similar hazards. This is the correct answer.

When should operators use installed seat restraints?
  • A. Solely after the forklift begins to tip
  • B. When the forklift has seat restraints and the operator is exposed to overturn or thrown-from-compartment hazards
  • C. Solely when the forklift has no load
  • D. Solely when traveling outdoors

The workbook explains that operators must use installed restraints when exposed to overturn or ejection hazards. This is the correct answer.

Which statement best addresses seat belts and operator restraints? When should operators use installed seat restraints?
  • A. On site, solely when the forklift has no load
  • B. On site, when the forklift has seat restraints and the operator is exposed to overturn or thrown-from-compartment hazards
  • C. On site, solely when traveling outdoors
  • D. On site, solely after the forklift begins to tip

The workbook explains that operators must use installed restraints when exposed to overturn or ejection hazards. This is the correct answer.

Why are ramps and inclines important in tip-over evaluation?
  • A. They always make forklifts more stable
  • B. They can shift stability and affect how the load and truck behave during travel
  • C. They remove the need for seat belts
  • D. They make traffic rules unnecessary

Ramps and inclines can affect the center of gravity and create overturn or load-control hazards. This is the correct answer.

During forklift operating-condition training, why are manufacturer operating manuals important for seat belt use?
  • A. They prove restraints are unnecessary
  • B. They let operators ignore employer procedures
  • C. They include instructions, warnings, and precautions operators must be trained to follow
  • D. They are needed only by mechanics

The workbook notes that manufacturers' operating manuals require seat belt use and operators must be trained on manual instructions and warnings. This is the correct answer.

Which workplace condition can contribute to an operator being thrown from the compartment?
  • A. A smooth, dry, level floor
  • B. A route cleared of debris
  • C. Speed bumps, potholes, rough surfaces, or sudden movement
  • D. A forklift operated at safe speed

Uneven surfaces and sudden movement can throw an operator from the compartment, especially without restraint use. This is the correct answer.

What should an employer do if older forklifts lack restraints and are used where overturn or ejection is possible?
  • A. Install any homemade belt that fits the seat
  • B. Ignore the hazard because the forklift is older
  • C. Tell operators to jump clear during a tip-over
  • D. Contact the manufacturer for an approved conversion kit and install it as recommended

The workbook directs employers to contact the manufacturer for an approved conversion kit when restraints are needed on existing forklifts. This is the correct answer.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Hazard evaluation: Considers speed, docks, ramps, traffic, surface condition, tight areas, speed bumps, debris, tire condition, railroad tracks, potholes, slick surfaces, and combined CG
  • Seat belt / operator restraint: Required when provided; prevents the operator from being thrown from the seat during a tip-over
  • Dynamic forces: Acceleration, braking, turning, and traveling on inclines or uneven surfaces all shift the combined CG and can cause tip-over
  • Operator restraint use: Operators must use restraints when exposed to overturn hazards or areas where they could be thrown from the compartment; OSHA can cite for non-use
  • Normal operating position: Operator must be in the normal seat/stand position when raising or lowering a worker on a platform
  • Workplace-related topics: Surface conditions, load composition, manipulation/stacking, pedestrian traffic, narrow aisles, hazardous (classified) locations, ramps, ventilation/CO buildup
  • Overhead guard: Cage-style guard that protects the operator from falling objects; openings must not exceed 6 inches in one dimension and must extend over the operator including during forward tilt
  • Prior-training credit: If a topic was previously covered and is appropriate for the current forklift and worksite, repeat training is not required, but the operator must still be evaluated as competent
  • Certification document: Must record operator name, trainer name, training and evaluation dates, topics covered, and how training was implemented
  • Refresher trigger: unsafe operation: Required when the operator is observed operating unsafely or receives an evaluation showing unsafe operation
