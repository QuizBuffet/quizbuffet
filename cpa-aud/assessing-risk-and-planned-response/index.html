Sample Questions

Establishing an overall audit strategy at the beginning of an engagement serves to

Answer: Set the scope, timing, and direction of the audit and guide the development of the more detailed engagement plan

AU-C Section 300 requires the auditor to establish an overall audit strategy that sets the scope, timing, and direction of the audit and guides the development of the audit plan. The strategy addresses the characteristics of the engagement (entity size, industry, reporting requirements), the reporting objectives (form and timing of reports), the significant factors that determine the engagement team's focus, the results of preliminary engagement activities, and the nature, timing, and extent of resources the firm expects to deploy.

Which of the following decisions is BEST characterized as part of the overall audit strategy rather than the more detailed engagement plan?

Answer: The determination that the audit will rely primarily on substantive analytical procedures rather than tests of controls in areas where the assessed control environment is weak

The decision to rely on substantive analytical procedures rather than tests of controls for areas with weak control environments is a strategic-level decision. The overall strategy determines the general approach, for example, whether the audit will be primarily controls-reliance based (tests of controls followed by reduced substantive procedures) or primarily substantive (direct testing of financial statement assertions with little or no reliance on controls). This direction-setting decision flows into the engagement plan, which specifies the actual procedures.

The audit engagement plan is BEST described as

Answer: A more detailed document than the overall strategy that specifies the nature, timing, and extent of audit procedures to be performed in each area, responsive to identified risks

AU-C Section 300 distinguishes the audit plan from the overall strategy: while the strategy sets the scope and direction, the engagement plan (also called the audit program) translates that strategy into specific, documented procedures. The plan specifies the nature of procedures (e.g., confirmation, inspection, recalculation), their timing (interim vs. year-end), and their extent (sample sizes, coverage percentages) for each significant account and assertion, all designed to respond to the specific risks of material misstatement identified during risk assessment.
