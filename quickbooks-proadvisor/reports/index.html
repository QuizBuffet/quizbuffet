Sample Questions

A bookkeeper needs to find a specific financial report in QuickBooks Online and wants to access the central location where all reports are organized. Starting from the left navigation bar, which tab opens the Report Center?
  • A. Accounting
  • B. Reports
  • C. Dashboard
  • D. Sales

The Reports tab in the left navigation bar opens the Report Center, which organizes all available standard, custom, and management reports.

A small business owner wants to quickly run a Profit and Loss report in QuickBooks Online to see income and expenses for the current month. They open the Report Center and scan the category groups. Under which Report Center group is the Profit and Loss report found?
  • A. Who Owes You
  • B. What You Owe
  • C. For My Accountant
  • D. Business Overview

The Student Guide lists the Profit and Loss report under the Business Overview group in the QBO Report Center.

A controller runs the Profit and Loss report and sees only category-level totals but no individual transactions. A colleague mentions they prefer to see every underlying transaction. The QBO Report Center offers a distinct report format that shows transaction-level data. What is the key difference between a Summary report and a Detail report in QBO?
  • A. Summary reports display data by calendar year only; Detail reports can show any custom date range
  • B. Summary reports are only available in Plus and Advanced; Detail reports are available in all subscriptions
  • C. Summary reports can be exported to Excel; Detail reports can only be printed or saved as PDFs
  • D. Summary reports show totals by category without individual transactions; Detail reports list each underlying transaction

The Student Guide defines Summary reports as showing totals grouped by category, while Detail reports list every individual transaction making up those totals.

A lender asks a small business owner to provide a Statement of Cash Flows as part of a loan application review. The owner logs into QBO and navigates to the Report Center to find this report. Under which Report Center group is the Statement of Cash Flows found?
  • A. For My Accountant section
  • B. Sales and Customers section
  • C. Business Overview section
  • D. What You Owe section

The Student Guide lists the Statement of Cash Flows under the Business Overview section group alongside the Profit and Loss and Balance Sheet.

A business owner opens the Profit and Loss report in QBO and wants to change the reporting period to the prior fiscal year and switch the display from accrual to cash basis. These changes are made using a toolbar that appears at the top of the open report. Which toolbar or button is used to customize an open QBO report?
  • A. The Edit button, which opens a report properties dialog
  • B. The Customize button located above the report data
  • C. The gear icon in the upper right corner of the main screen
  • D. The Filters panel accessible from Account and Settings

The Student Guide states that the Customize button located above the report data allows users to change date ranges, accounting methods, columns, and filters.

An accountant explains to a business owner how the Balance Sheet differs from the Profit and Loss in terms of the time period it covers. Unlike the P&L, which covers a date range, the Balance Sheet is structured differently. How does the date structure of a Balance Sheet differ from that of a Profit and Loss report?
  • A. The Balance Sheet covers a full calendar year and cannot be run for custom date ranges like the Profit and Loss
  • B. Both the Balance Sheet and the Profit and Loss cover the same date range and have the same date structure
  • C. The Balance Sheet covers a date range of transactions just like the Profit and Loss report
  • D. The Balance Sheet shows financial position as of a single point in time, while the Profit and Loss covers a date range of activity

The Balance Sheet shows the company's financial position, assets, liabilities, and equity, as of a specific point in time, while the Profit and Loss covers income and expenses over a date range.

During a QBO training session, a participant asks how many named report groups exist within the Standard tab of the Report Center. The trainer explains that QBO organizes its standard reports into multiple distinct groups. Which set correctly lists report groups found in the Standard tab of the QBO Report Center?
  • A. Business Overview, Who Owes You, What You Owe, Sales and Customers, Expenses and Vendors, Employees, and For My Accountant
  • B. Profit and Loss, Balance Sheet, Banking, Payroll, Inventory, and Taxes
  • C. Income Reports, Expense Reports, Payroll Reports, Tax Reports, and Audit Reports
  • D. Customer Reports, Vendor Reports, Employee Reports, and Management Reports

The Student Guide lists these groups in the Standard tab: Business Overview, Who Owes You, What You Owe, Sales and Customers, Expenses and Vendors, Employees, and For My Accountant (plus Sales Tax and Payroll).

A QBO trainer explains that QuickBooks Online reports can be categorized into four fundamental types based on how they present data. Understanding report types helps users select the right report for the information needed. Which set correctly identifies the four report types in QBO?
  • A. Summary, Detail, List, and Specialized
  • B. Income, Expense, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow
  • C. Standard, Custom, Management, and Scheduled
  • D. Summary, Detail, Comparison, and Trend

The Student Guide identifies four QBO report types: Summary (totals by category), Detail (individual transactions), List (list data), and Specialized (unique formats like the Business Snapshot).

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Customer reports: A/R Aging, Open Invoices, Customer Balance Detail, Sales by Customer Summary
  • Bank reconciliation: Settings → Reconcile; match QBO transactions to bank statement; ending balance must match; differences flagged in reconciliation report
  • P&L (Profit & Loss): Income − Expenses for a date range; default is YTD; cash vs accrual toggle; group by Class/Location/Tag
  • Vendor reports: A/P Aging, Unpaid Bills, Vendor Balance Detail, Purchases by Vendor
  • Performance Center (Advanced): Real-time dashboard widgets pulling KPIs from data; replaces traditional report navigation
  • User permissions: QBO user types: Standard (full or limited), Company admin, Reports-only, Time-tracking-only; Advanced adds custom roles
  • Sales receipts and tips: Track tips by adding "Tips" item to sales form; sales receipts can be exported to deposit-grouping report
  • A/R Aging report: Groups unpaid invoices into 0-30, 31-60, 61-90, >90 day buckets; key for collections workflow
  • A/P Aging report: Groups unpaid bills by aging buckets; key for cash-flow planning
  • Sales-tax reporting & filing: Taxes → Sales tax → "Returns due"; QBO generates a summary; some states integrate filing through partners
