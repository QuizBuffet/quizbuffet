Sample Questions

Anthrax and tetanus bacilli form which type of spores?

Answer: Endospores

Anthrax (Bacillus anthracis) and tetanus (Clostridium tetani) are rod-shaped bacteria (bacilli) that produce endospores — highly resistant, dormant structures formed inside the bacterial cell; endospores can survive extreme heat, dryness, chemicals, and UV radiation for years, which is why they require sterilization (not mere disinfection) to destroy them.

Sanitation is defined as the process of

Answer: significantly reducing microorganisms on a surface to a safe level

Sanitation is the lowest level of infection control; it reduces the number of microorganisms on a surface or implement to a level considered safe for public health, without claiming to kill all pathogens; examples include handwashing and wiping surfaces with soap and water; it is the required first step before disinfection.

Disinfection destroys most harmful microorganisms on non-living surfaces but does NOT destroy

Answer: bacterial endospores

Disinfection kills most pathogenic bacteria (in their active vegetative state), most viruses, and fungi on non-living surfaces; however, it does not reliably destroy bacterial endospores — the highly resistant dormant forms produced by Bacillus and Clostridium species; only sterilization achieves the level of microbial kill required to destroy endospores.
