Sample Questions

Forklift stability is based on what basic principle?
  • A. Two weights balanced on opposite sides of a fulcrum point
  • B. A forklift becomes more stable whenever the load is raised
  • C. The rear wheels always carry the entire load weight
  • D. The operator’s body weight is the main stabilizing force

Forklift stability is based on the same balancing principle as a teeter-totter, with the front wheels acting as the fulcrum point. This is the correct answer.

Which action increases forward tip-over risk?
  • A. Traveling down a ramp with the load forward when that orientation creates instability
  • B. Keeping the load slightly tilted back while traveling
  • C. Avoiding sudden braking
  • D. Following site procedures for ramps and inclines

Ramp direction and load position affect the combined center of gravity and forward stability. This is the correct answer.

What serves as the fulcrum point on a forklift?
  • A. The operator’s seat
  • B. The front wheels
  • C. The steering wheel
  • D. The top of the overhead guard

The workbook explains that the front wheels are the fulcrum point for forklift balance. This is the correct answer.

Which statement best addresses fulcrum point and forward stability? Forklift stability is based on what basic principle?
  • A. On site, the operator’s body weight is the main stabilizing force
  • B. On site, two weights balanced on opposite sides of a fulcrum point
  • C. On site, a forklift becomes more stable whenever the load is raised
  • D. On site, the rear wheels always carry the entire load weight

Forklift stability is based on the same balancing principle as a teeter-totter, with the front wheels acting as the fulcrum point. This is the correct answer.

On site, which action increases forward tip-over risk?
  • A. On site, following site procedures for ramps and inclines
  • B. On site, traveling down a ramp with the load forward when that orientation creates instability
  • C. On site, keeping the load slightly tilted back while traveling
  • D. On site, avoiding sudden braking

Ramp direction and load position affect the combined center of gravity and forward stability. This is the correct answer.

During forklift stability training, what can contribute to a forklift tipping forward?
  • A. Driving as far under a stable load as possible before lifting
  • B. Keeping the load low and within capacity
  • C. Exceeding rated capacity or carrying a load too far forward
  • D. Checking the data plate before lifting

A load that is too heavy or too far from the forklift can move the combined center of gravity forward and cause forward tipping. This is the correct answer.

On site, what serves as the fulcrum point on a forklift?
  • A. On site, the top of the overhead guard
  • B. On site, the operator’s seat
  • C. On site, the front wheels
  • D. On site, the steering wheel

The workbook explains that the front wheels are the fulcrum point for forklift balance. This is the correct answer.

Why should an operator drive as far under the load as possible before lifting?
  • A. To make the load center farther away from the forklift
  • B. To lift with the tips of the forks only
  • C. To avoid checking whether the load is secure
  • D. To help keep the load close and reduce forward leverage

Keeping the load close helps control leverage around the front-wheel fulcrum. This is the correct answer.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Combined center of gravity: New center of gravity of the forklift plus the load it is carrying, must remain inside the stability triangle
  • Capacity reduction: When a load extends beyond the rated load center, or when attachments are used, the forklift’s usable capacity decreases
  • Attachments increase capacity needs: Trucks fitted with attachments must be operated as partially loaded even when "empty," because the attachment shifts the CG forward
  • Data plate: Manufacturer plate showing model, serial, weight, attachment, and rated capacity at a specified load center and lift height, must be legible
  • Load center: Horizontal distance from the front vertical face of the forks to the line of action through the load’s center of gravity; most forklifts are rated at 24 inches
  • Rated capacity: Maximum load the forklift can safely handle at the rated load center and lift height shown on the data plate
  • Forklift-related topics: Truck-specific operating instructions, controls, engine/motor, steering, visibility, attachments, capacity, stability, inspection, refueling/charging, and operating limits
  • Front-end attachment marking: Non-OEM attachments must be marked with the attachment ID and approximate combined weight of forklift + attachment at maximum elevation with a centered load
  • Center of gravity (CG): Single point around which an object is balanced; on a forklift it shifts as the mast tilts and the upright moves
  • Picking up a load: Confirm rated capacity, position forks properly, balance the load, drive fully under, slightly tilt back, lift, then back, stop, and lower 2-6 inches from the floor
