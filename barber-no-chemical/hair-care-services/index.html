Sample Questions

Scalp analysis is performed before a service primarily to accomplish which of the following?

Answer: To identify scalp and hair conditions that may affect service selection and product choices

CORRECT: Scalp analysis — performed before every service — is the systematic visual and tactile examination of the scalp and hair to identify conditions that will affect how the barber approaches the service. A thorough scalp analysis evaluates: scalp health (dry, oily, normal, or inflamed), presence of scalp disorders or infections (dandruff, psoriasis, tinea capitis, folliculitis), hair characteristics (texture, density, porosity, elasticity, growth pattern), and any contraindications (open sores, active infections, lesions, or conditions that prohibit service). Based on this analysis, the barber selects appropriate services, modifies techniques, avoids contraindicated areas, and chooses products suited to the client's specific scalp and hair type. Performing a scalp analysis without identifying a contraindication can result in harm to the client, cross-contamination, or service outcomes that worsen the client's condition.

A contraindication in barbering is BEST defined as which of the following?

Answer: A condition or circumstance that prohibits a service or requires significant service modification

CORRECT: A contraindication is any condition, circumstance, or factor that makes a particular service inadvisable, unsafe, or prohibited because it could cause harm to the client, the barber, or other clients. Contraindications in barbering fall into two categories: (1) absolute contraindications — conditions that completely prohibit a service from being performed, such as active contagious infections (tinea capitis, impetigo, open herpes lesions), open wounds, bleeding lesions, or actively inflamed skin conditions in the service area; and (2) relative contraindications — conditions that require modification of a service rather than complete refusal, such as mild, stable psoriasis (requires gentle technique), sensitivity to certain products (requires product substitution), or limited neck mobility (requires positioning adjustments). Identifying contraindications is the barber's professional and ethical responsibility — performing a service despite a clear contraindication puts the client at risk and may constitute professional misconduct.

Which of the following items is MOST important to document in a client's intake record before the very first service?

Answer: Known allergies, scalp or skin conditions, and any medications that may affect the service

CORRECT: Before performing any service on a new client, the most important intake information to capture is health-related: (1) known allergies — particularly to products, chemicals, metals, latex, or fragrances that may be encountered during barbershop services; (2) scalp and skin conditions — active or chronic conditions such as psoriasis, eczema, seborrheic dermatitis, alopecia, or a history of fungal infections that affect service planning, product selection, and contraindication assessment; and (3) medications — certain medications have side effects that directly affect barbershop services (blood thinners increase bleeding risk during shaving, immunosuppressants increase infection risk, certain medications cause photosensitivity or scalp dryness, corticosteroids thin the skin). This information directly determines whether a service is safe to perform, which products to avoid, what modifications are necessary, and what signs of adverse reaction to monitor for. Without this information, the barber cannot make fully informed service decisions for the new client.
