The Taxation and Regulation (REG) section is one of three Core sections of the Uniform CPA Examination administered by the AICPA. Every CPA candidate must pass REG regardless of chosen Discipline. The 4-hour exam consists of 72 multiple-choice questions and 8 task-based simulations, weighted 50/50 in scoring. Content covers five areas: Ethics, Professional Responsibilities and Federal Tax Procedures (10-20%). Treasury Department Circular 230, tax return preparer rules and penalties, state board licensing, IRS audits and appeals, tax authoritative hierarchy, privileged communications; Business Law (15-25%): agency, contracts (UCC), debtor-creditor relationships, bankruptcy under BAPCPA 2005, employment taxes, qualified health plans, worker classifications, anti-bribery under FCPA 1977, business entity formation and termination; Federal Taxation of Property Transactions (5-15%): tax basis of purchased and converted assets, gifted and inherited property, wash sales, intangible assets, MACRS depreciation, Section 179, amortization; Federal Taxation of Individuals (22-32%): Form 1040 gross income inclusions and exclusions, pass-through income, AGI adjustments, itemized deductions, QBI deduction, loss limitations, filing status, tax credits, estimated tax safe harbor; and Federal Taxation of Entities (23-33%): Schedule M-1/M-3 book-tax differences, C corporation taxable income and state nexus, S corporation eligibility and ordinary business income, partnership separately stated items and partner basis, LLCs, tax-exempt organizations. References include the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 as amended, Treasury Regulations, Uniform Commercial Code, Revised Model Business Corporation Act, FCPA, and ACA.

Everything you need to know before sitting for CPA Taxation and Regulation (REG)

Why REG matters in 2026

CPA Taxation and Regulation (REG) is one of the most recognized credentials issued by AICPA, and 2026 hiring data continues to show REG on job postings as either required or strongly preferred for the roles it targets. The certification validates that you can apply real working knowledge, not just recall facts, across 5 distinct exam domains, with the largest weight on Business Law. For candidates competing in the AICPA ecosystem, REG is a clear signal to a hiring manager that you have invested in measurable, third-party-verified competence.

Who should take REG

REG is most useful for professionals working in or moving toward the AICPA ecosystem. In short: Core section of the Uniform CPA Examination, U.S. federal taxation, ethics, and business law. If you are early in your career, REG is one of the fastest credentials to add to a résumé that recruiters actively screen for. If you are mid-career, it formalizes the skills you already use day-to-day and unlocks roles that gate on it. If you are switching tracks, it gives you a structured curriculum that tells you exactly what to study, in what order, weighted by what the real exam tests.

Exam structure and difficulty

The CPA Taxation and Regulation (REG) exam is organized into 5 domains, each weighted by the official AICPA exam guide. The heaviest weighting is Business Law at 25% of the exam, so that is where you should spend the most preparation time. The lightest is Federal Taxation of Property Transactions at 14%, meaning you can dedicate roughly proportional review time without over-investing.

Common pitfalls

The three traps that kill REG candidates: (1) over-memorizing acronyms instead of practicing the application of concepts in scenarios, when the exam is scenario-driven, not a vocab quiz. (2) Skipping the heaviest-weighted domain because it feels less interesting, which can fail the whole exam by neglecting Business Law. (3) Not timing practice sessions, when the exam has a real clock and pacing is its own skill. Build timing into your last two weeks of prep.

REG questions, answered

What is the passing score for REG?

Like every CPA Exam section, you need 75 on AICPA's 0-99 scale.

How much does the REG exam cost?

CPA Exam section fees are set by your state board, typically $250 to $350 per section, plus a separate application/registration fee.

What does REG cover?

Federal taxation of individuals, entities, and property transactions, business law, and ethics and professional responsibilities in tax practice. You are not tested on specific inflation-indexed tax rate percentages, just the underlying rules and calculations.

Do I have to pass REG before the other sections?

No. AICPA does not require a specific order among the 3 Core sections (AUD, FAR, REG) and your chosen Discipline section.

How long do I have to pass all 4 CPA sections?

A rolling 30-month window from the date you pass your first section, or your earliest passing score expires and you retake that section.
