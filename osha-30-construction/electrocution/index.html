Sample Questions

Which condition is a common electrocution hazard on a construction site?
  • A. A damaged extension cord with exposed conductors
  • B. A cord routed away from traffic and protected from damage
  • C. A tool inspected before use and removed when defective
  • D. A dry, covered receptacle protected by a GFCI

Exposed conductors can energize workers, tools, or surfaces and create shock or electrocution hazards. This is the correct answer.

On site, which condition is a common electrocution hazard on a construction site?
  • A. On site, a damaged extension cord with exposed conductors
  • B. On site, a tool inspected before use and removed when defective
  • C. On site, a dry, covered receptacle protected by a GFCI
  • D. On site, a cord routed away from traffic and protected from damage

Exposed conductors can energize workers, tools, or surfaces and create shock or electrocution hazards. This is the correct answer.

A worker raises a metal ladder near energized overhead power lines. What is the main hazard?
  • A. A minor housekeeping concern
  • B. Electrocution from contact or close approach to energized lines
  • C. A respiratory hazard from ladder material
  • D. Noise exposure from nearby traffic

Metal ladders can conduct electricity if they contact or approach energized overhead lines. This is the correct answer.

During electrical hazard planning on a construction site, which task creates an electrical hazard that should be controlled before work starts?
  • A. Reviewing drawings in the project trailer
  • B. Using a battery-powered radio in a break area

Which worker is exposed to an electrocution hazard?
  • A. A worker using a properly guarded non-defective tool on a dry surface
  • B. A worker walking through a marked pedestrian path away from electrical work
  • C. A worker using a listed tool according to the manufacturer’s instructions
  • D. A worker operating equipment beneath overhead energized power lines without clearance controls

Equipment near overhead power lines can contact or approach energized conductors and energize the machine. This is the correct answer.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Hand protection: Gloves selected for the specific hazard: cut, chemical, electrical, abrasion, or thermal
  • Hazard prevention and control: Hierarchy: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, PPE, applied in order
  • Job hazard analysis (JHA): Step-by-step task review that identifies hazards and specifies controls before work begins
  • Pre-task plan: Daily/shift planning document covering scope, hazards, controls, PPE, and emergency response
  • Ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI): Device that detects current imbalance and shuts off power to prevent shock; required on construction sites for 120 V receptacles
  • Assured equipment grounding conductor program (AEGCP): Documented inspection program permitted as an alternative to GFCI on construction sites
  • Qualified person (electrical): Worker trained in construction or operation of electric equipment and the hazards involved
  • Energized work permit: Authorization required to work on energized electrical equipment when de-energizing is infeasible
  • Hard hat (Class E/G/C): Head protection; Class E rated for high-voltage, Class G for general, Class C provides no electrical protection
  • Engage and empower: Involve crew members in finding hazards and solutions; give them authority to act
