Sample Questions

When is an overhead guard needed on a forklift?
  • A. When the operator is exposed to hoisted or stacked objects that might fall
  • B. Whenever the operator wants to drive faster
  • C. Solely when the forklift is parked outdoors
  • D. When the forklift is used as a passenger vehicle

An overhead guard protects the operator from certain falling-object hazards when hoisted or stacked objects could fall. This is the correct answer.

On site, when is an overhead guard needed on a forklift?
  • A. On site, when the operator is exposed to hoisted or stacked objects that might fall
  • B. On site, solely when the forklift is parked outdoors
  • C. On site, when the forklift is used as a passenger vehicle
  • D. On site, whenever the operator wants to drive faster

An overhead guard protects the operator from certain falling-object hazards when hoisted or stacked objects could fall. This is the correct answer.

What should be true about overhead guard design?
  • A. It should be removed when loads are tall
  • B. It must protect the operator while not interfering with good visibility
  • C. It should be used as a storage rack for tools
  • D. It should block the operator’s view to prevent distraction

The workbook states guards must be constructed so they do not interfere with good visibility. This is the correct answer.

Which statement best addresses overhead guards? What should be true about overhead guard design?
  • A. On site, it should be used as a storage rack for tools
  • B. On site, it must protect the operator while not interfering with good visibility
  • C. On site, it should block the operator’s view to prevent distraction
  • D. On site, it should be removed when loads are tall

The workbook states guards must be constructed so they do not interfere with good visibility. This is the correct answer.

When are forklifts required to have a vertical-load backrest in the workbook?
  • A. When carrying workers on the forks
  • B. When handling small objects or unbanded units
  • C. Solely when operating on public highways
  • D. When the operator wants better rear visibility

Forklifts that handle small objects or unbanded units need a backrest to help prevent material from falling toward the operator. This is the correct answer.

During forklift component inspection, what is a key requirement for openings in the top of an overhead guard?
  • A. Openings should be cut larger when visibility is poor
  • B. Openings must be large enough for loose material to pass through
  • C. Openings must not exceed 6 inches in one of the two dimensions, width or length
  • D. Openings are unrestricted if the operator wears a hard hat

The workbook identifies a 6-inch limit in one dimension for overhead guard openings. This is the correct answer.

What characteristics should a load backrest have?
  • A. A lightweight design that can bend away from the load
  • B. Large openings that allow small items through
  • C. Sufficient height, width, and strength to prevent the load from falling toward the operator
  • D. A removable design used only when convenient

The workbook states the backrest must have enough height, width, and strength for this protective purpose. This is the correct answer.

Why must an overhead guard extend over the operator under normal conditions, including forward tilt?
  • A. So it can carry extra pallets above the operator
  • B. So operators can stand on it during maintenance
  • C. So it replaces the need for safe stacking
  • D. So the guard protects the operator during normal operating positions

The guard must be large enough to extend over the operator during normal operation, including forward tilt. This is the correct answer.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Platform requirements: Guardrails or equivalent fall protection, firmly secured to the carriage or forks, protective barrier between worker and mast where chains/shear points exist
  • Shear-point guard: Barrier between the operator and the mast chains or other shear points to prevent caught-in injuries
  • Pre-operation inspection: Daily walk-around and operational check before each shift; defects must be reported and the forklift removed from service if unsafe
  • Manufacturer approval: Modifications or additions affecting capacity or safe operation require prior written approval from the manufacturer
  • Front-end attachment marking: Non-OEM attachments must be marked with the attachment ID and approximate combined weight of forklift + attachment at maximum elevation with a centered load
  • Overhead guard: Cage-style guard that protects the operator from falling objects; openings must not exceed 6 inches in one dimension and must extend over the operator including during forward tilt
  • Load backrest: Vertical extension behind the forks that prevents loose or stacked items from falling toward the operator; openings must not exceed 6 inches in one dimension
  • Data plate: Manufacturer plate showing model, serial, weight, attachment, and rated capacity at a specified load center and lift height, must be legible
  • Fulcrum point: Pivot used to balance the forklift; the front (drive) wheels are the fulcrum on a sit-down counterbalanced forklift
  • Counterbalance: Mass of the forklift behind the drive axle that offsets the load weight in front of the forks
