Sample Questions

A consumer calls an MLO to ask about general rate ranges and requests product information. The consumer has not yet provided the items needed to constitute an application. Under Regulation Z, this contact is best classified as:
  • A. An application that triggers the 3-business-day Loan Estimate clock
  • B. A pre-application inquiry that does not yet trigger the Loan Estimate clock
  • C. A formal request for a Closing Disclosure
  • D. A request for a Verification of Deposit

Correct, until the consumer provides all six application items, the contact is a pre-application inquiry under 12 CFR 1026.2(a)(3).

MLOs handle nonpublic personal information from consumers. Mishandling this information can violate GLBA and state law. An MLO who collects a consumer's Social Security number, income documents, and bank statements must:
  • A. Safeguard the information consistent with the company's GLBA-required information security program
  • B. Post the information to a public website for transparency
  • C. Forward the information to any party requesting it
  • D. Discard the information immediately after the call to avoid compliance risk

Correct, GLBA's Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314) requires an information security program protecting nonpublic personal information.

During an initial inquiry, an MLO may ask the consumer general questions about products and rates without triggering an application. The MLO should avoid requesting any of the six items that would convert the contact into an application. Which question would NOT, by itself, contribute to making the contact an application?
  • A. What loan products are you most interested in hearing about?
  • B. What is your full legal name?
  • C. What is your Social Security number?
  • D. What is the property address?

Correct, asking about product preferences does not capture any of the six application items under 12 CFR 1026.2(a)(3).

Before the consumer indicates intent to proceed, Regulation Z restricts what fees a creditor can charge. The rule preserves the consumer's right to shop without pressure. Before the consumer's indication of intent to proceed, the creditor may charge only:
  • A. An origination fee equal to 1% of the loan amount
  • B. Discount points selected by the consumer
  • C. A bona fide and reasonable fee for obtaining a credit report
  • D. An appraisal deposit equal to the estimated appraisal cost

Correct, 12 CFR 1026.19(e)(2)(i)(B) allows only a bona fide and reasonable credit-report fee before intent to proceed.

After receiving an application, the MLO must provide certain disclosures to the borrower. The communication preserves the integrity of the disclosure timing rules. If the LE was placed in the mail, the consumer is presumed to have received it:
  • A. On the same day it was mailed
  • B. On the next business day after mailing
  • C. On the second business day after mailing
  • D. On the third business day after the LE was placed in the mail

Correct, 12 CFR 1026.19(e)(1)(iv) presumes receipt 3 business days after mailing.

On a residential mortgage application, the borrower is using a gift to fund part of the down payment. The lender must document the gift and verify acceptable donor status. A gift donor on a conventional residential loan must generally:
  • A. Be a creditor or financial institution making a loan
  • B. Be an unrelated individual to ensure independence
  • C. Provide collateral equal to the gift amount
  • D. Have a documented qualifying relationship to the borrower (such as a relative, fiance, or domestic partner) and provide a signed gift letter stating the funds need not be repaid

Correct. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac selling guides require an eligible donor relationship and a gift letter affirming the funds are not a loan.

Regulation B permits creditors to ask certain questions on residential mortgage applications that would otherwise touch protected bases. The exceptions support fair-lending oversight and underwriting. On a residential mortgage application, an MLO MAY ask:
  • A. The applicant's plans for childbearing
  • B. Whether the applicant practices a particular religion
  • C. The applicant's political party affiliation
  • D. Whether income from alimony, child support, or separate maintenance should be considered, with notice that disclosure is optional

Correct, 12 CFR 1002.5(c)(2) permits asking, with notice that disclosure is optional unless the applicant wants the income considered.

An MLO and consumer have been discussing options. Once the MLO has all six items defined as an application, the MLO cannot delay the LE by withholding action. The consumer 'completes the application' as soon as the MLO has received:
  • A. Verified asset and income documents and employment verifications
  • B. Name, monthly income, Social Security number, property address, estimated property value, and the loan amount sought
  • C. A signed and notarized loan agreement and title report
  • D. The full underwriting package, including appraisal and title commitment

Correct, 12 CFR 1026.2(a)(3) defines application as receipt of these six items.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • TRID: Loan Estimate timing: 3 business days from application; "application" triggered by 6 items: name, SSN, income, address, estimated value, loan amount
  • HPML (12 CFR 1026.35): Higher-Priced Mortgage Loan: APR ≥ APOR + 1.5% (first-lien conforming); requires escrow for ≥5 years and stricter appraisal
  • TRID: Closing Disclosure timing: Provided at least 3 business days before consummation; rate change ≥ 1/8% (fixed) or 1/4% (ARM) triggers a new 3-day waiting period
  • HMDA / Reg C (12 CFR 1003): Home Mortgage Disclosure Act: requires data collection on housing-related loans; LAR submitted annually by March 1
  • State-licensed MLO: Originates loans for a non-depository (independent mortgage company); requires NMLS license, education, testing, background and credit checks
  • Conventional / conforming loan: Non-government loan that meets Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac standards (loan limit, DTI, credit, documentation)
  • Primary mortgage market: Where loans are originated and funded: banks, credit unions, mortgage companies, brokers
  • Pre-approval: Formal lender review of credit, income, and asset documentation; typically conditional on property appraisal and clear title
  • APR (Annual Percentage Rate): Total cost of credit including interest plus most fees, expressed as a yearly rate; central TILA disclosure
  • HOEPA (12 CFR 1026.32): High-cost mortgage rule: triggers based on APR, points & fees, prepayment penalty thresholds; adds protections and prohibitions
