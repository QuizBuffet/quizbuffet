Sample Questions

Before beginning any tattoo procedure, the artist must don which personal protective equipment at minimum?
  • A. Medical-grade disposable gloves
  • B. A full face shield and surgical gown
  • C. Safety glasses and a lab coat
  • D. Sterile surgical gloves and a respirator

Medical-grade disposable gloves are the minimum required PPE before beginning any tattoo procedure; they protect both the artist and the client from bloodborne pathogen transmission through the skin punctures inherent in tattooing; gloves must be intact, properly fitted, and replaced if torn or contaminated during the service.

Disinfectant wipes used in the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination must be
  • A. any brand of antibacterial household wipes
  • B. homemade bleach-soaked paper towels in a labeled container
  • C. fragrance-free wipes regardless of EPA registration
  • D. EPA-registered wipes with manufacturer's label demonstrating bactericidal, fungicidal, and virucidal properties

The NIC Tattooing Practical Examination specifically requires EPA-registered disinfectant wipes bearing the original manufacturer's label that demonstrates bactericidal (kills bacteria), fungicidal (kills fungi), and virucidal (kills viruses) properties; these three claims together confirm the product meets the minimum antimicrobial efficacy standard for tattooing surface disinfection; aerosol sprays are prohibited in the testing environment.

Proper handwashing before gloving for a tattoo procedure requires
  • A. scrubbing all surfaces of the hands and wrists with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, then rinsing and drying with a clean towel before gloving
  • B. applying hand sanitizer only, which eliminates the need for handwashing
  • C. rinsing hands with water for 5 seconds
  • D. wiping hands with an EPA-registered disinfectant wipe as a substitute for handwashing

Effective handwashing requires wetting hands, applying soap, and actively scrubbing all surfaces (palms, backs, between fingers, fingertips, thumbs, and wrists) for a minimum of 20 seconds before rinsing and drying with a clean paper towel; this mechanical action removes transient microorganisms and organic material from the skin surface before gloves are donned.

After disinfecting a work surface, barrier protection such as plastic wrap or disposable covers must be applied because
  • A. barriers improve the appearance of the workstation for client comfort
  • B. barriers are required only on surfaces that the needle will directly contact
  • C. barriers eliminate the need to disinfect surfaces entirely at setup
  • D. barriers are applied over the disinfected surface to maintain cleanliness and prevent recontamination of surfaces that cannot be disinfected between clients or would be difficult to disinfect adequately

After surfaces are disinfected, barrier materials (plastic wrap, plastic bags, disposable covers, or barrier film) are applied over the clean disinfected surface to maintain its clean status throughout the procedure; barriers protect surfaces from blood, ink, and body fluid contamination during the tattoo procedure and allow post-procedure breakdown by removing and discarding the barrier rather than requiring full re-disinfection of every surface; barriers are particularly critical on items that cannot be easily disinfected between clients (spray bottles, machines, power supplies, clip cords).

The candidate supply container used during the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination functions as
  • A. a wet storage container for disinfected implements between uses
  • B. a sharps disposal container for used needles
  • C. dry storage that must remain closed except when removing materials for a specific task, items removed from the kit may not be returned
  • D. an open tray for displaying all supplies throughout the examination

The NIC Tattooing Practical Examination specifies that the candidate supply container serves as dry storage and must be kept closed except when removing materials for a particular task; once items are removed from the kit for a service, they may not be returned to the kit, this prevents recontamination of clean stored items from items that have been in the service environment.

If a tattoo artist has an open cut or abrasion on their hand, they should
  • A. proceed normally since gloves provide full protection regardless of hand condition
  • B. cover the wound with a waterproof bandage, then don gloves over it before beginning the procedure
  • C. avoid the service entirely since no protective measures can be taken
  • D. use two pairs of gloves only, without covering the wound first

An open wound on the artist's hand must first be covered with a waterproof bandage or occlusive dressing that seals the wound completely before donning gloves; this two-layer protection, waterproof covering plus glove, prevents the wound from serving as a route of pathogen entry or exit if the glove is compromised during the procedure.

Barriers applied to the tattoo machine body and power supply before a procedure must be
  • A. removed and replaced between clients, not reused, to prevent cross-contamination from one client's blood and body fluids to the next
  • B. clear plastic so the examiner can see the equipment underneath
  • C. changed between each color of ink dispensed during the same procedure
  • D. secured with non-sterile tape to ensure they do not slip during the procedure

Barriers are single-use infection control tools; after a procedure, the client's blood and body fluids have contaminated the outer surface of each barrier covering; these barriers must be carefully removed without touching the contaminated outer surface (typically by inverting them inward) and placed in the trash; new, clean barriers must be applied for each new client to ensure the underlying equipment and surfaces do not transfer the previous client's biological material to the next client.

The primary reason tattoo artists must not wear jewelry (rings, bracelets, watches) on their hands and wrists during a procedure is
  • A. jewelry can scratch the tattoo machine and damage its finish
  • B. jewelry distracts the client during the service
  • C. jewelry prevents gloves from being donned correctly
  • D. jewelry harbors microorganisms in crevices that handwashing cannot reach, creating a persistent contamination source even after gloving, and can create glove puncture points that compromise the barrier

Rings, bracelets, and watches create spaces where microorganisms can accumulate in biofilm on the metal, clasps, and settings that cannot be effectively reached by handwashing; standard handwashing reduces surface microorganisms on the skin but cannot penetrate the crevices of jewelry; additionally, sharp settings or edges on jewelry can puncture gloves during the procedure, compromising the glove barrier and creating a pathway for blood exposure; removing all hand and wrist jewelry before handwashing and gloving is a fundamental infection control requirement.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Work Area Setup: First domain of the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination: candidate prepares themselves, disinfects all work surfaces, sets up supplies and equipment, designates the universal supply area, and disposes of contaminated supplies, all within 20 minutes.
  • Foot Switch: Pedal-operated power control for the tattoo machine; classified as a contaminated item during the NIC practical exam and must be stored separately from clean supplies, then disinfected for transport between clients.
  • Infection Control: Practices that prevent the transmission of pathogens, including hand hygiene, surface disinfection, barrier use, and single-use supplies, required throughout the tattooing process.
  • Bloodborne Pathogen Standard: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030: federal regulation requiring exposure control plans, PPE, hepatitis B vaccination offers, and post-exposure procedures for all workers with occupational blood exposure.
  • Barriers: Plastic wrap or other single-use coverings applied to machine frames, power supply buttons, clip cords, and other surfaces that cannot be sterilized, preventing cross-contamination during the procedure.
  • Cross-Contamination: Transfer of pathogens from a contaminated source to a clean surface or client; prevented by barriers, single-use items, and strict zone separation between clean and contaminated areas.
  • Pigment: Tattoo colorant suspended in a carrier solution; regulated by state and local rules; must be stored and handled per SDS guidelines; dispersed into single-use ink caps: the bottle must never contact the client or used equipment.
  • Power Supply: Device that regulates voltage delivered to the tattoo machine; paired with a clip cord (connects machine to power supply) and optionally a foot switch (pedal control considered contaminated during the exam).
  • Aftercare: Instructions given to the client at conclusion of the tattoo procedure covering wound care, moisturizer application, sun avoidance, and activity restrictions during the healing period.
  • Contraindications: Client conditions that prevent or delay tattooing, including active skin infection, blood-thinning medications, isotretinoin use, pregnancy, keloid history, and areas with impaired sensation or circulation.
