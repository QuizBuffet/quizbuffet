Sample Questions

During the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination, the razor used for the simulated shave must be
  • A. uncapped and demonstrated on the model's actual forearm skin
  • B. a multi-use safety razor cleaned with alcohol between clients
  • C. capped throughout the simulated shaving demonstration
  • D. a battery-powered electric razor to avoid any blade risk

The NIC Tattooing Practical Examination instructions specify that the candidate will perform a simulated shave and that the razor must NOT be uncapped during this demonstration; the candidate demonstrates the motion and technique of shaving with the protective cap remaining on the razor throughout, assessing proper shaving technique knowledge without actual skin contact with the blade.

The stencil used in the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination must be approximately
  • A. 2x3 inches
  • B. 5x5 inches
  • C. 4x6 inches
  • D. 1x1 inch

The NIC Tattooing Practical Examination supplies list specifies a 'Prepared Stencil (Approx 2x3 Inches)'; this size is sufficient to demonstrate stencil application technique clearly while fitting appropriately within the forearm area designated for the simulated procedure.

The forearm area on the model for the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination must be
  • A. covered with a protective sleeve until the procedure begins
  • B. moisturized with lotion before the stencil application
  • C. marked with a reference grid to assist stencil placement
  • D. pre-shaved and free of tattoos before the examination

The NIC CIB specifies that the live model must have a pre-shaved forearm that is free of tattoos; pre-shaving ensures the examination stencil application area is free of hair that would interfere with stencil adhesion and visibility; the forearm must also be free of tattoos so the stencil can be clearly seen and evaluated against the skin.

Stencil solution is applied to the skin before placing a thermal stencil because
  • A. it creates a moist, adhesive surface that allows the stencil ink to transfer from the thermal paper to the skin clearly and durably
  • B. it sterilizes the skin surface before the stencil contacts it
  • C. it prevents the stencil from being permanently tattooed into the skin
  • D. it numbs the skin surface to reduce pain during stencil application

Thermal stencil paper contains an ink layer on one side that transfers to the skin when the paper is pressed against a properly prepared surface; stencil solution creates a slightly tacky, moist surface on the skin that chemically interacts with the stencil ink to facilitate clear transfer; too dry a surface produces incomplete transfer; too wet a surface causes the ink to run and blur; stencil solution provides the optimal moisture and adhesion balance for a clean, clear transfer.

Used ink caps from pigment dispensing that have contacted the needle or been in the procedure area must be
  • A. returned to the pigment bottle for reuse if pigment remains
  • B. saved in the supply kit for the next client session
  • C. soaked in disinfectant and reused for the same client's subsequent sessions
  • D. discarded in the 'Trash' container, never returned to the source pigment bottle, to prevent contamination of the entire pigment supply

Ink caps (small disposable cups used to hold dispensed pigment during the procedure) are single-use consumables; after the procedure, any remaining pigment in the caps is discarded along with the caps in the 'Trash' container; the pigment remaining in used caps is considered contaminated by the procedure environment and must never be returned to the source bottle; this is one of the most critical cross-contamination prevention rules in tattooing.

The purpose of cleaning the skin with a skin prep product before stencil application is to
  • A. permanently remove the client's natural skin oils to improve tattoo longevity
  • B. remove surface oils, debris, and any residue from the shave that could interfere with stencil adhesion and create a clean surface for the procedure
  • C. sterilize the skin before the needle contacts it during the actual tattoo
  • D. anesthetize the skin to reduce client discomfort during the stencil removal

Pre-stencil skin preparation removes the surface oils, perspiration, shave residue, and any debris that would prevent the stencil solution from adhering properly to the skin; clean, properly prepared skin allows the stencil to transfer clearly and adhere throughout the design consultation; additionally, removing surface contamination before the procedure reduces the microbial load at the needle penetration site.

After applying the stencil to the client's forearm, the artist should
  • A. immediately begin tattooing without waiting for the stencil to dry, to maintain pigment adhesion
  • B. allow the stencil to fully dry before beginning the tattoo procedure so the design is not smeared by contact with gloves or the machine
  • C. apply a second layer of stencil solution over the dried stencil to seal it
  • D. remove the stencil and redo it if any part of the design is not perfectly centered

After stencil application and removal of the backing paper, the transferred stencil image must be allowed to fully air dry before any tattooing begins; the drying time allows the transferred ink to bond to the skin surface and set; a fully dried stencil resists smearing from glove contact, perspiration, and the petroleum jelly often applied during tattooing, all of which would distort or erase a still-wet stencil.

A client armrest is required during both the stencil application and the simulated tattoo procedure primarily because
  • A. it is a state board requirement that applies to all states without exception
  • B. it elevates the forearm above table level to improve blood circulation during the procedure
  • C. it provides stable, consistent positioning of the client's arm that reduces movement, improving stencil placement accuracy and procedural safety during tattoo application
  • D. it prevents the client's arm from directly touching the work surface to avoid contamination of the table

A stable, properly supported arm position is critical during tattooing and stencil application: if the client's arm moves during the procedure, the stencil placement or tattoo needle path can shift, producing misaligned stencil transfer or irregular tattoo lines; the armrest supports the forearm in a stable, comfortable position that minimizes involuntary movement from muscle fatigue, discomfort, or reflex; this positional stability directly improves both the quality of the stencil placement and the safety of the needle work.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Stencil Application: Transfer of a prepared design (approximately 2×3 inches for NIC practical) to the pre-shaved, clean forearm using stencil solution; the outline guides needle placement during the procedure.
  • Foot Switch: Pedal-operated power control for the tattoo machine; classified as a contaminated item during the NIC practical exam and must be stored separately from clean supplies, then disinfected for transport between clients.
  • Work Area Setup: First domain of the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination: candidate prepares themselves, disinfects all work surfaces, sets up supplies and equipment, designates the universal supply area, and disposes of contaminated supplies, all within 20 minutes.
  • Single-Use Supplies: Items designed for one client only: including needles, tubes, ink caps, gloves, and razors, that must be discarded after each client to prevent cross-contamination.
  • Cross-Contamination: Transfer of pathogens from a contaminated source to a clean surface or client; prevented by barriers, single-use items, and strict zone separation between clean and contaminated areas.
  • Stencil Solution: Product applied to the skin to temporarily fix the stencil transfer; typically contains ingredients that help the dye adhere to clean, dry skin during the procedure setup.
  • Aftercare: Instructions given to the client at conclusion of the tattoo procedure covering wound care, moisturizer application, sun avoidance, and activity restrictions during the healing period.
  • Contraindications: Client conditions that prevent or delay tattooing, including active skin infection, blood-thinning medications, isotretinoin use, pregnancy, keloid history, and areas with impaired sensation or circulation.
  • Infection Control: Practices that prevent the transmission of pathogens, including hand hygiene, surface disinfection, barrier use, and single-use supplies, required throughout the tattooing process.
  • Sterilization: Process that destroys all microbial life including bacterial spores, achieved by autoclave (steam under pressure); required for any instrument that penetrates the skin.
