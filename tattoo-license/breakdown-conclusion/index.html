Sample Questions

When breaking down the procedural work area after a tattoo service, barriers must be
  • A. removed carefully to avoid touching the contaminated outer surface, then discarded in the 'Trash' container before the underlying surface is disinfected
  • B. left in place to protect the surface until the next client is seated
  • C. folded neatly and saved in the supply kit for reuse at the next session
  • D. sprayed with disinfectant through the barrier to disinfect both barrier and surface simultaneously

Barrier removal during breakdown requires careful technique to prevent contact with the contaminated outer surface: the barrier is grasped only at clean, unexposed edges or folded inward so the contaminated side faces inward, then discarded in the 'Trash' container; after barrier removal, the underlying surface (which was protected by the barrier) is wiped with EPA-registered disinfectant wipes to complete the decontamination; this two-step process, remove barriers then disinfect, is the correct breakdown sequence.

After breakdown is complete, the work area should be
  • A. left organized on the table for the examiner to verify the setup before clearing
  • B. organized with supplies returned to the candidate kit, waste containers sealed appropriately, and all surfaces confirmed disinfected and ready for the next use
  • C. covered with a clean sheet until the next client is seated
  • D. documented on the state board cleanup form before the next section begins

A complete service breakdown results in: the work area clear of used supplies and contaminated waste; all surface barriers removed and surfaces disinfected; waste containers (trash, sharps, to be disinfected) sealed or appropriately managed; reusable items placed in the 'To be Disinfected/Sterilized' container; and the candidate kit organized and closed; this state of the work area confirms that breakdown was thorough and the space is prepared for subsequent use.

After removing all barriers and discarding contaminated supplies, the work surfaces must be disinfected with EPA-registered wipes by
  • A. a single fast wipe across the entire surface to maximize efficiency during breakdown
  • B. wiping with a dry paper towel first to remove visible material, then applying the disinfectant wipe
  • C. thoroughly wiping all surface areas while ensuring the disinfectant remains visibly wet on the surface for the full manufacturer-specified contact time before wiping dry
  • D. applying disinfectant only to areas visibly stained with ink, leaving unstained areas untreated

Effective disinfection with EPA-registered wipes requires: thorough wiping to cover all surface areas completely; maintaining visible wetness on the surface for the full contact time specified on the manufacturer's label (typically 1-10 minutes depending on the product); this dwell time is required for the disinfectant's active ingredients to achieve the bactericidal, fungicidal, and virucidal kill claims; wiping the surface dry before the contact time has elapsed interrupts the disinfection process and compromises its effectiveness.

The armrest used during the tattoo procedure must be disinfected during breakdown because
  • A. armrests are listed as suggested equipment, making them subject to the same disinfection requirements as all other reusable equipment in the procedure area
  • B. armrests are sterile equipment that must be re-sterilized between clients
  • C. armrests only need to be cleaned with soap and water since they contact covered forearms, not open wounds
  • D. the NIC examination does not require armrest disinfection as it is not a procedure tool

The armrest is a required piece of equipment that directly contacts the client's forearm throughout the procedure, an area with an active open wound from the tattooing; it is subject to blood and body fluid contamination from the procedure; as a reusable item, it must be barrier-protected during the procedure and disinfected with EPA-registered wipes during breakdown, just like all other reusable equipment and surfaces in the procedure area.

When is the sharps container sealed during the breakdown process?
  • A. When it is full, the container must be sealed and replaced with a new one when full
  • B. At the end of each procedure session or when the container is 3/4 full, whichever comes first, to prevent overflow and needlestick risk during transport
  • C. Only when the studio owner gives permission to close the container
  • D. It is never sealed, it remains open throughout the day for ongoing access by all studio artists

OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard requires that sharps containers be closed and sealed when they are approximately 3/4 full to prevent sharps from protruding through the opening and to make the container safe for handling and transport; in a single-session examination context, the container is also sealed at the end of the procedure; a sealed sharps container must be handled and transported appropriately according to local regulations.

The tattoo machine body must be disinfected during breakdown by
  • A. submerging the entire assembled machine in a disinfectant jar for 10 minutes
  • B. wiping all external surfaces thoroughly with EPA-registered disinfectant wipes after removing all barriers, ensuring all areas that could have been touched during the procedure are addressed
  • C. autoclaving the machine between every client to achieve sterilization
  • D. rinsing the machine body under running water to remove ink and blood residue

The tattoo machine is a reusable item that cannot be sterilized but must be disinfected between clients; after barriers are removed from the machine body, all external surfaces, the frame, tube vice, binding posts, and any area that may have been contacted during the procedure, are wiped thoroughly with EPA-registered disinfectant wipes; the disinfectant must contact all surfaces and remain wet for the required contact time before the machine is handled further.

Reusable tubes and grips that have contacted blood during the tattoo procedure must undergo which level of reprocessing before use on the next client?
  • A. Soaking in 70% isopropyl alcohol for 30 minutes to achieve adequate decontamination
  • B. Disinfection with EPA-registered wipes, which is sufficient for all reusable tattoo implements
  • C. Thorough cleaning to remove all organic material, followed by sterilization, typically autoclaving, to destroy all microbial life including spores
  • D. Rinsing with distilled water and air-drying before storage in the UV cabinet

Tattoo tubes and grips that penetrate or directly contact the open wound during tattooing are classified as critical items by the Spaulding classification system; critical items require sterilization, not merely disinfection; the correct reprocessing pathway is: (1) immediately place in the 'To be Disinfected/Sterilized' container; (2) after the session, thoroughly clean the items to remove all organic material (blood, ink, debris) using a brush and appropriate detergent; (3) sterilize using a validated sterilization method, typically autoclaving at the correct temperature and pressure; (4) store in sterile packaging until next use.

During Section 5 breakdown, the NIC CIB instructs the candidate to do nothing with the foot switch until specifically instructed. This is because
  • A. the foot switch requires a special disinfectant not included in the standard supply kit
  • B. the foot switch automatically disinfects itself after 20 minutes of non-use
  • C. the power supply must be fully cooled before the foot switch can be safely disconnected
  • D. the foot switch disinfection and storage procedure is a separately assessed task evaluated in Section 6, premature handling would interfere with the examiner's ability to evaluate the correct sequence

The NIC Tattooing Practical Examination evaluates foot switch disinfection and storage as a specific assessed task within Section 6; the CIB specifically instructs candidates to 'Do nothing with the foot switch' during Section 5 breakdown to ensure this competency is separately observable and evaluable by the examiner during the dedicated Section 6 assessment; handling the foot switch prematurely during Section 5 would either skip or interfere with the Section 6 foot switch evaluation.

Key Terms in This Domain

  • Work Area Setup: First domain of the NIC Tattooing Practical Examination: candidate prepares themselves, disinfects all work surfaces, sets up supplies and equipment, designates the universal supply area, and disposes of contaminated supplies, all within 20 minutes.
  • Foot Switch: Pedal-operated power control for the tattoo machine; classified as a contaminated item during the NIC practical exam and must be stored separately from clean supplies, then disinfected for transport between clients.
  • Infection Control: Practices that prevent the transmission of pathogens, including hand hygiene, surface disinfection, barrier use, and single-use supplies, required throughout the tattooing process.
  • Bloodborne Pathogen Standard: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030: federal regulation requiring exposure control plans, PPE, hepatitis B vaccination offers, and post-exposure procedures for all workers with occupational blood exposure.
  • Cross-Contamination: Transfer of pathogens from a contaminated source to a clean surface or client; prevented by barriers, single-use items, and strict zone separation between clean and contaminated areas.
  • Tattoo Machine: Electromechanical device that drives a needle bar up and down to deposit pigment into the dermis; types include coil machines (electromagnetic) and rotary machines (motor-driven).
  • Power Supply: Device that regulates voltage delivered to the tattoo machine; paired with a clip cord (connects machine to power supply) and optionally a foot switch (pedal control considered contaminated during the exam).
  • Aftercare: Instructions given to the client at conclusion of the tattoo procedure covering wound care, moisturizer application, sun avoidance, and activity restrictions during the healing period.
  • Contraindications: Client conditions that prevent or delay tattooing, including active skin infection, blood-thinning medications, isotretinoin use, pregnancy, keloid history, and areas with impaired sensation or circulation.
  • EPA-Registered Disinfectant: Surface disinfectant registered with the Environmental Protection Agency; must demonstrate bactericidal, fungicidal, and virucidal properties; aerosol sprays are prohibited in NIC testing environments.
