Sample Questions

Under ASC 305, an investment qualifies as a cash equivalent when it is

Answer: A short-term, highly liquid investment readily convertible to a known cash amount with an original maturity of three months or less from the date of purchase — presenting insignificant risk of value changes

ASC 305-10-20 defines cash equivalents as short-term, highly liquid investments that are (1) readily convertible to known amounts of cash and (2) so near their maturity that they present insignificant risk of value changes. The operative criterion is an original maturity of three months or less from the date of purchase. Examples include 90-day Treasury bills, commercial paper with ≤90-day maturities, and qualifying money market funds.

On December 1, a company purchases a 60-day commercial paper instrument with face value of $500,000. How should this investment be classified on the December 31 balance sheet?

Answer: Cash equivalent — the 60-day original maturity at purchase is within the three-month threshold, qualifying it as a cash equivalent

The 60-day original maturity from the purchase date (December 1) falls within the 3-month (~90-day) threshold. The instrument is (1) short-term and highly liquid; (2) readily convertible to a known cash amount; and (3) purchased with original maturity ≤3 months — satisfying the ASC 305 cash equivalent definition. Classification is determined at purchase and does not change as the instrument approaches maturity.

A bond indenture requires maintaining $2 million in a segregated sinking fund for bond repayment in four years. How should this $2 million be classified on the balance sheet?

Answer: Non-current restricted asset labeled 'Restricted Cash' or 'Sinking Fund' — legally restricted from general use with restriction extending beyond one year

ASC 305-10-45 requires that legally restricted cash (such as bond indenture sinking funds) be excluded from Cash and Cash Equivalents and presented separately. Since the bonds mature in four years, the sinking fund is a non-current restricted asset labeled accordingly ('Sinking Fund' or 'Restricted Cash, non-current') to communicate to users that this cash is unavailable for general operations.
