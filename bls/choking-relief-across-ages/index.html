7.0 Choking Relief Across Ages — BLS Practice Quiz

30 exam-style questions covering 10% of the BLS exam. Instant feedback on every answer, progress tracking, no signup required.

This domain is part of the Basic Life Support (BLS) practice test. Each question is tagged by exam objective and difficulty so you can drill exactly the areas you need.

Sample Questions

An adult suddenly grabs the front of their neck with both hands and looks panicked after eating. AHA Part 11 names this gesture by what term?

Answer: The universal choking sign

AHA Part 11 names the universal choking sign — clutching the neck with both hands — as the indicator that someone is choking.

An adult is coughing forcefully and can speak in short bursts after eating. This presentation is consistent with mild airway obstruction. What is the appropriate action per AHA Part 11?

Answer: Encourage the person to keep coughing and monitor for worsening signs

Mild airway obstruction is best handled by encouraging forceful coughing while monitoring for signs of severe obstruction.

An adult clutches their throat, nods yes to 'Are you choking?', and cannot speak. According to AHA Part 11's Adult FBAO sequence, what is the correct relief sequence?

Answer: Give 5 forceful back blows between the shoulder blades, then 5 abdominal thrusts if needed

AHA Part 11 directs the rescuer to deliver 5 forceful back blows; if those do not relieve the choking, perform 5 abdominal thrusts.
