CPR / AED Certification is the lay-rescuer credential for responding to sudden cardiac arrest, choking, and other breathing emergencies. The knowledge component is standardized across the American Heart Association and American Red Cross and is built on the AHA Guidelines for CPR & ECC: scene safety, the Chain of Survival, recognition of cardiac arrest, high-quality compressions and ventilations for adults, children, and infants, AED operation, choking response, and special situations such as opioid overdose, drowning, and pregnancy. Required for many healthcare, education, fitness, lifeguard, and child-care roles, and broadly recommended for any responsible adult.

Everything you need to know before sitting for CPR / AED Certification

Why CPR/AED matters in 2026

CPR / AED Certification is one of the most recognized credentials issued by American Red Cross / AHA, and 2026 hiring data continues to show CPR/AED on job postings as either required or strongly preferred for the roles it targets. The certification validates that you can apply real working knowledge, not just recall facts, across 7 distinct exam domains, with the largest weight on Adult CPR. For candidates competing in the American Red Cross / AHA ecosystem, CPR/AED is a clear signal to a hiring manager that you have invested in measurable, third-party-verified competence.

Who should take CPR/AED

CPR/AED is most useful for professionals working in or moving toward the American Red Cross / AHA ecosystem. In short: Lifesaving cardiac and breathing emergency skills. If you are early in your career, CPR/AED is one of the fastest credentials to add to a résumé that recruiters actively screen for. If you are mid-career, it formalizes the skills you already use day-to-day and unlocks roles that gate on it. If you are switching tracks, it gives you a structured curriculum that tells you exactly what to study, in what order, weighted by what the real exam tests.

Exam structure and difficulty

The CPR / AED Certification exam is organized into 7 domains, each weighted by the official American Red Cross / AHA exam guide. The heaviest weighting is Adult CPR at 22% of the exam, so that is where you should spend the most preparation time. The lightest is Introduction and Chain of Survival at 8%, meaning you can dedicate roughly proportional review time without over-investing.

Format details: Hands-only and full-CPR pathways · AED skill check required · 2-year certification cycle (AHA / ARC). Following the domain weights is the single biggest leverage point candidates miss, because many over-study lower-weighted material that feels comfortable.

Common pitfalls

The three traps that kill CPR/AED candidates: (1) over-memorizing acronyms instead of practicing the application of concepts in scenarios, when the exam is scenario-driven, not a vocab quiz. (2) Skipping the heaviest-weighted domain because it feels less interesting, which can fail the whole exam by neglecting Adult CPR. (3) Not timing practice sessions, when the exam has a real clock and pacing is its own skill. Build timing into your last two weeks of prep.

CPR/AED questions, answered

What kind of questions are on the CPR test?

Most CPR/AED exams are multiple choice. They cover the Chain of Survival, how to check responsiveness and breathing, compression rate and depth for adults, children, and infants, how to use an AED, and how to help someone who is choking. QuizBuffet mirrors that exact mix across all seven domains.

How hard is it to get AED certified?

Not very. An AED is designed for untrained bystanders, so the device speaks each step out loud as you go. Most courses pair a short written test with a hands-on skill check, and the written part usually asks for around 80% or better to pass.

Are AED and CPR certification the same thing?

No. CPR is the manual skill of chest compressions and rescue breaths, while an AED is a portable device that reads the heart rhythm and delivers a shock when one is needed. Most certifications teach both together in a single CPR/AED course.

Is it safe to use an AED on a pregnant patient?

Yes. Using an AED on a pregnant woman is safe and recommended, and the shock will not harm the baby. Place the pads in the standard positions and follow the voice prompts the same way you would for any adult.

How many questions are on a CPR/AED exam?

It depends on the provider, but written exams commonly run between 25 and 35 multiple-choice questions. QuizBuffet gives you 300+ practice questions so the real test feels familiar before you sit it.

What score do you need to pass CPR certification?

Most courses set the passing score around 80% on the written exam, plus a successful hands-on skill check. Some are higher; the AHA BLS Provider exam, for example, requires 84%. Check your specific provider for the exact cutoff.

How long does CPR/AED certification last?

For both the American Heart Association and the American Red Cross, CPR/AED certification is valid for two years. After that you renew it with a shorter refresher course.

Can you fail a CPR test?

Yes, but it is uncommon and easy to recover from. If you miss the passing score or a skill step, instructors let you review and retake it. Working through practice questions first is the simplest way to avoid that.
