Sample Questions

A remote pilot is reviewing a METAR report before a commercial sUAS flight. The report contains the group '27015KT.' What information does this group convey?

Answer: Wind from 270 degrees (west) at 15 knots, with no gusts reported

In a METAR, the wind group is formatted as direction in degrees true followed by speed and units; '27015KT' means wind from 270 degrees (west) at 15 knots.

A remote pilot reviews a METAR that shows '3SM' in the visibility group. Under 14 CFR Part 107, what is the minimum flight visibility required to operate an sUAS, and does the observed 3 SM satisfy that requirement?

Answer: The minimum visibility is 3 statute miles; the observed 3 SM exactly meets the regulatory minimum for Part 107 sUAS operations

Under 14 CFR 107.51(c), the minimum flight visibility for sUAS operations is 3 statute miles; an observed visibility of 3 SM exactly meets this regulatory minimum.

A remote pilot arrives at the launch site and observes winds at 12 knots with gusts to 24 knots. No manufacturer-specific wind limits are available for the sUAS being used. Under Part 107 performance considerations, how should the pilot factor in gusts during this flight planning decision?

Answer: The pilot should use the gust factor (24 knots) as the effective maximum wind speed for planning purposes, since gusts represent the peak instantaneous load on the sUAS structure and control system

When wind gusts are present, the gust value represents the peak wind speed the aircraft will actually experience; remote pilots should evaluate the sUAS's ability to handle peak gust loads, not just the sustained wind, because gusts impose the most severe structural and control demands.
